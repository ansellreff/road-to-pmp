## Key points

- The **Scope** domain makes sure the project includes **all the work required, and only the work required**. In the 8th edition it also covers **quality of the deliverables**.
- **Product scope** is the features and functions of the result. **Project scope** is the work done to deliver it.
- A **requirement** is a condition or capability that must be present in the result to satisfy a business need.
- The **scope baseline** = **project scope statement + WBS + WBS dictionary**.
- The **work breakdown structure (WBS)** is a hierarchical decomposition of the total scope. Its lowest level is the **work package**. What is not in the WBS is not in the project (the **100 percent rule**).
- **Monitor and Control Scope** checks correctness and produces **verified deliverables**. **Validate Scope** obtains formal acceptance and produces **accepted deliverables**.
- In adaptive work, the **product backlog** takes the place of the WBS and the **definition of done** takes the place of acceptance criteria.

## What the Scope domain covers

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.2

Scope has a central place in project management, because the **value** of a project comes from delivering the outcome defined in its scope. The domain has two jobs:

1. **Include all required work, and no unnecessary work.** Extra work costs time and money without adding value.
2. **Make sure deliverables meet their requirements and acceptance criteria.** Quality here means meeting stakeholder expectations and fulfilling project and product requirements.

> **Edition note.** Older material has a separate Quality knowledge area with a Plan Quality Management and a Control Quality process. In the 8th edition, planning for deliverable quality sits inside Plan Scope Management and Define Scope, and checking deliverables sits inside Monitor and Control Scope.

## Product scope, project scope and requirements

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.2.1

| Term | Meaning | Example: a new company website |
|---|---|---|
| **Product scope** | The features, functions and characteristics of the product, service or result | Online ordering, a search function, pages that load in under two seconds |
| **Project scope** | The work performed to deliver the result with those features | Design, coding, testing, training, project management |
| **Requirement** | A condition or capability that must be present to satisfy a business need | "A customer can pay by card" |

- Completion of **product scope** is measured against the **product requirements**. Completion of **project scope** is measured against the **project management plan**.
- **Quality is part of scope.** A bridge project includes the bridge, and also how sturdy and long-lasting it must be.
- The **business case** explains why the project is worth doing and sets the success criteria that scope must serve.

## The six Scope processes

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.2.2; PMBOK 8, Guide Figure 2-13; Process Groups: A Practice Guide, Table 1-4; PMBOK 6, 1.2.4.6

| Focus Area | Process | Main output | Older name you may see |
|---|---|---|---|
| Planning | **Plan Scope Management** | Scope management plan, requirements management plan, quality management plan | Plan Scope Management, Plan Quality Management |
| Planning | **Elicit and Analyze Requirements** | Requirements documentation | Collect Requirements |
| Planning | **Define Scope** | Project scope statement | Define Scope |
| Planning | **Develop Scope Structure** | Scope baseline (WBS, WBS dictionary) or product backlog | Create WBS |
| Monitoring and Controlling | **Monitor and Control Scope** | Verified deliverables, change requests, work performance information | Control Scope, Control Quality |
| Monitoring and Controlling | **Validate Scope** | Accepted deliverables, change requests | Validate Scope |

The planning order is logical: decide **how** scope will be managed, find out what stakeholders **need**, describe the **scope**, then **break it down**.

## Plan Scope Management

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.2.2.1; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Scope management plan

Creates the plans that say **how** scope will be defined, developed, monitored, controlled and validated. It does not contain the scope itself.

- The **scope management plan** describes how the scope statement will be prepared, how the WBS will be created, how the scope baseline will be approved and maintained, and how formal acceptance will be obtained.
- The **requirements management plan** describes how requirements will be analyzed, documented and managed, including how they will be prioritized and traced.
- Both are components of the **project management plan**. Neither is part of the scope baseline.

## Elicit and Analyze Requirements

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.2.2.2; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Requirements documentation

Determines, documents and manages **stakeholder needs and requirements**. The main output is the **requirements documentation**. In adaptive work, requirements are collected as **user stories** and prioritized in a backlog.

Before requirements are baselined they should be **unambiguous (measurable and testable), traceable, complete, consistent and acceptable** to key stakeholders.

| Type of requirement | What it describes |
|---|---|
| **Business** | The high-level needs and strategic objectives of the organization |
| **Stakeholder** | The needs of a stakeholder or stakeholder group |
| **Solution: functional** | What the product does: its behaviors, actions, data and interactions |
| **Solution: nonfunctional** | How well it does it: reliability, security, performance, safety, supportability |
| **Transition and readiness** | Temporary capabilities needed to move to the future state, such as data conversion and training |
| **Project** | Conditions the project must meet: milestone dates, contractual obligations, constraints |
| **Quality** | Conditions that prove a deliverable is complete: tests, certifications, validations |

## Techniques for gathering requirements

@source PMBOK 8, Guide Figure 2-15; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Focus groups; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Nominal group

| Technique | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Interviews** | Talking with stakeholders directly, formally or informally |
| **Focus groups** | Prequalified stakeholders and experts brought together, guided by a moderator, to learn their expectations |
| **Questionnaires and surveys** | Written questions for collecting information quickly from a large number of people |
| **Brainstorming** | Generating many ideas in a short time in a group, led by a facilitator |
| **Benchmarking** | Comparing with the practices or products of comparable organizations to find best practices |
| **Observation** | Watching people do their work, also called job shadowing |
| **Document analysis** | Reviewing existing documents to find relevant requirements |
| **Nominal group technique** | Brainstorming followed by a **vote** to rank the ideas |
| **Affinity diagram** | Sorting a large number of ideas into groups |
| **Mind mapping** | Consolidating ideas into one map to show what they have in common and where they differ |

## The requirements traceability matrix

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Requirements traceability matrix

A grid that **links each requirement from its origin to the deliverable that satisfies it**.

- It shows that every requirement adds **business value**, by linking it to business and project objectives.
- It tracks requirements **throughout the project life cycle**, so that approved requirements are actually delivered at the end.
- It gives a structure for **managing changes** to product scope.

A requirement that cannot be traced to an objective is a warning sign of unneeded scope.

## Define Scope and the project scope statement

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.2.2.3; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Project scope statement

Develops a description of the project, the product and the value to be delivered. In a predictive project this is done in detail at the start; in adaptive work, at a high level and then at the start of each iteration.

The **project scope statement** contains:

- **Project scope description:** the characteristics of the product, service or result, elaborated from the charter and requirements.
- **Deliverables:** any unique and verifiable product, result or capability that must be produced.
- **Acceptance criteria:** the conditions that must be met before deliverables are accepted.
- **Project exclusions:** what is explicitly **out of scope**. Stating exclusions manages expectations and reduces scope creep.

| | Project charter | Project scope statement |
|---|---|---|
| Level of detail | High level | Detailed |
| Purpose | Authorizes the project | Describes the scope and its boundaries |
| Part of the scope baseline? | No | **Yes** |

## Develop Scope Structure: the WBS

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.2.2.4; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Scope baseline; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Decomposition; Practice Standard for Work Breakdown Structures, 2.3.1; Practice Standard for Work Breakdown Structures, 2.4.3

Subdivides deliverables and project work into smaller, more manageable components. The technique is **decomposition**. The output is the **work breakdown structure (WBS)**, which completes the **scope baseline**.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **WBS** | A hierarchical decomposition of the **total scope of work**. Each level down gives a more detailed definition of the work. It is organized around **deliverables**, not around dates. |
| **Work package** | The **lowest level** of the WBS. It has a unique identifier and is the level at which cost and duration can be estimated and managed. |
| **Control account** | A management control point where scope, budget and schedule are integrated and compared with earned value. It contains **two or more work packages**; each work package belongs to **only one** control account. |
| **Planning package** | Below the control account and above the work package: work content is known, detailed schedule activities are not yet. |
| **WBS dictionary** | The document that **describes each WBS component** in detail: description of work, responsible organization, milestones, resources, cost estimates, acceptance criteria. |
| **Code of accounts** | The numbering system that gives every WBS component its unique identifier. |

- **The 100 percent rule.** The WBS contains **all** product and project work, including project management work. The lower levels roll up to the higher levels so that nothing is left out and no extra work is done.
- **Do not decompose too far.** Excessive decomposition wastes management effort and makes the work less efficient.
- Work far in the future may not be decomposable yet. It is detailed later, which is **rolling wave planning**.
- Work packages are decomposed into **activities** later, when the schedule is developed. Activities are not part of the WBS: the WBS is the **what**, activities are the **how**.
- The WBS turns one large, uncertain job into smaller pieces that are easier to estimate, assign and control. It is the basis for defining activities, estimating costs and resources, and identifying risks.

**What a WBS does not show.** A WBS element describes scope only. It does **not** contain:

| Not in the WBS | Where it is found instead |
|---|---|
| **Time or sequence** | The schedule |
| **Relationships** between elements | The network diagram |
| **Costs** | Cost estimates and the budget |
| **Resources** | The responsibility assignment matrix and resource plans |
| **Importance** | Position in the WBS does not mean priority |

More rules for a sound WBS:

- Elements are **mutually exclusive**: no work appears in two places.
- If a parent would have **only one child**, do not decompose it.
- Elements are numbered by level: **1** for the project, **1.1, 1.2** for the next level, **1.1.1** below that.
- It can be built **top-down** (start from the final product and break it down) or **bottom-up** (list the deliverables, then group them). Either way, each level must add up to 100 percent of the level above.

## The scope baseline

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Scope baseline; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.2.1

**Scope baseline = project scope statement + WBS + WBS dictionary.**

- It is the **approved** version of the scope. It can be changed only through **formal change control** and is used for comparison with actual results.
- It is a component of the **project management plan**.
- Together with the **schedule baseline** and the **cost baseline** it forms the **performance measurement baseline (PMB)**.
- The requirements documentation and the scope management plan are **not** part of the scope baseline.

## Monitor and Control Scope

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.2.2.5; PMBOK 8, Guide Figure 2-18; PMBOK 6, Glossary (Scope Creep); The Project Management Answer Book, Chapter 4, Q16

The ongoing process of monitoring the status of project and product scope, **managing changes to the scope baseline**, and **measuring the quality of deliverables** against the required standards.

| Inputs | Tools and techniques | Outputs |
|---|---|---|
| Scope and quality management plans, requirements documentation, performance measurement baseline, quality metrics, **deliverables**, approved change requests | Variance analysis, trend analysis, root cause analysis, audits and **inspections**, testing and product evaluations | **Verified deliverables**, quality control measurements, **change requests**, work performance information |

Two problems this process is meant to prevent are commonly tested:

| | Meaning | Who causes it |
|---|---|---|
| **Scope creep** | Uncontrolled expansion of scope without adjusting time, cost and resources | Usually requests that skip change control |
| **Gold plating** | Adding extras the customer did not ask for | The project team |

Neither is acceptable. Any change to scope goes through **change control**.

## Validate Scope

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.2.2.6; PMBOK 8, Guide Figure 2-19

**Formalizes acceptance** of the completed deliverables by the customer or sponsor.

| Inputs | Tools and techniques | Outputs |
|---|---|---|
| **Verified deliverables**, scope baseline, requirements documentation, quality reports | **Inspection**, decision-making, customer talks and tests, review meetings | **Accepted deliverables**, **change requests**, work performance information |

| | Monitor and Control Scope (quality control) | Validate Scope |
|---|---|---|
| Question | Is the deliverable **correct**? Does it meet the requirements? | Does the customer **accept** it? |
| Done by | The project team | The customer or sponsor |
| Output | **Verified** deliverables | **Accepted** deliverables |
| Order | Usually first | Usually after, though the two can run in parallel |

- If the customer does **not** accept a deliverable, the reasons are documented and a **change request** (defect repair) is raised.
- Validate Scope happens **throughout** the project, as deliverables are completed, not only at the end.
- Accepted deliverables then go to **Close Project or Phase**.

## Scope in adaptive projects

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.2.1; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.2.2.4

| Predictive | Adaptive |
|---|---|
| Scope defined in detail up front | Scope kept high level and refined in each iteration |
| WBS | **Product backlog**, broken down into epics, features and **user stories** |
| Scope baseline, changed through formal change control | Backlog reprioritized by the **product owner**, without formal change control |
| Acceptance criteria | Acceptance criteria and the **definition of done (DoD)** |
| Acceptance at the end of a phase or project | Acceptance at the end of each iteration |

- The **product backlog** is a dynamic, prioritized list of work items and features.
- The **definition of done** is a checklist of all the criteria a deliverable must meet to be considered ready for customer use.
- A **value breakdown structure (VBS)** links deliverables to the value each one is expected to add, which helps prioritize them.

## Exam tips

- "What makes up the scope baseline?" **Scope statement, WBS and WBS dictionary.** The requirements documentation and the scope management plan are common wrong options.
- "Lowest level of the WBS?" The **work package**. Activities belong to the schedule, not the WBS.
- A WBS shows **no dates, no sequence, no costs and no resources**. An option that says the WBS shows the order of work is wrong.
- "Where do I find the details of a work package?" The **WBS dictionary**.
- **Verified** comes before **accepted**. The team verifies, the customer accepts.
- A customer rejects a deliverable: document why and raise a **change request**. Do not close the project or argue.
- A team member adds an unrequested feature to please the customer: this is **gold plating** and is not acceptable, even if the customer likes it.
- A stakeholder asks for more work: follow **change control**. First assess the impact.
- "Which document links requirements to deliverables?" The **requirements traceability matrix**.
- Exclusions are found in the **project scope statement**.
