## Key points

- All project management actions fall into **five Focus Areas**: Initiating, Planning, Executing, Monitoring and Controlling, and Closing.
- "Focus Areas" is the 8th edition's new name for what was called **Process Groups**.
- **Focus Areas are not phases.** They can all occur inside every phase, they overlap, and they are not strictly sequential.
- They apply to **every** project, whether predictive, adaptive or hybrid, and in every industry.
- **Monitoring and Controlling runs in parallel** with the other four. It is never a stand-alone step.

## What the Focus Areas are

@source PMBOK 8, Standard 4.5

The Focus Areas group the fundamental actions that take place over the course of any project. They are carried out through formal processes, informal practices, policies, procedures or techniques. They may be iterative, continuous or overlapping, and are performed when planned or when needed, in the project as a whole and within each phase.

> **Edition note.** Older editions, the PMI *Process Groups: A Practice Guide*, most exam-prep courses and many exam questions call these the **five Process Groups**. The five names are identical. If you see "Process Group", read "Focus Area".

| Focus Area | Purpose in one line | Typical artifacts |
|---|---|---|
| **Initiating** | Define and authorize a new project or phase | Project charter, stakeholder register |
| **Planning** | Establish scope, refine objectives, define the course of action | Project management plan, product backlog |
| **Executing** | Complete the work as agreed | Deliverables |
| **Monitoring and Controlling** | Track, measure, review and regulate progress; identify and initiate changes | Performance reports, change requests |
| **Closing** | Formally complete or close the project, phase or contract | Final acceptance, handover to operations |

## Initiating

@source PMBOK 8, Standard 4.5.1

Actions performed to **define a new project or a new phase** of an existing project. It often includes **formal authorization** to start.

What happens:

- the **initial scope** is defined
- **initial financial resources** are committed
- **stakeholders** who will interact with and influence the project are identified
- **project management roles** are assigned

This is captured in the **project charter** and the **stakeholder register**.

**Purpose:** align stakeholders' expectations with the project's purpose, and inform them about scope and objectives.

**Key benefit:** projects are authorized only when they align with the organization's strategic objectives, and the business case, benefits and stakeholders are considered from the very start.

**Business documents** generally originate *outside* the project and are used as inputs to it. The two main ones are the **business case** and the **benefits management plan**. In them the **sponsor** should give the project manager the minimum information needed to start. Who does the preliminary work varies: the project manager, a project management team, the sponsor, the PMO or a portfolio steering committee.

## Planning

@source PMBOK 8, Standard 4.5.2

Actions that **establish the intended scope, define and refine the objectives, and develop the course of action** to reach them.

- Output: the **project management plan** in a predictive setting, or artifacts such as a **product backlog** in an adaptive one.
- Planning is not done once. As more is learned, more planning is needed. This ongoing refinement is called **progressive elaboration**.
- Planning can be progressive, iterative or **rolling wave** (detailed for the near term, high-level for later work).

How planning differs by approach:

| | Predictive | Adaptive |
|---|---|---|
| When | Front-loaded: most planning up front | Brief high-level planning first (**roadmapping**) |
| Afterwards | Updates and refinements as needed | Frequent planning and replanning throughout |

**Key benefit:** defines the course of action for successfully completing the project or phase.

## Executing

@source PMBOK 8, Standard 4.5.3

Actions performed to **complete the work** in line with the currently agreed course of action. Includes coordinating resources, managing stakeholder engagement and performing the project's activities.

- This is where the chosen development approach is **most visible**.
- The plan can and should be **changed whenever a change would improve the project's value**.

**Key benefit:** drives focused execution to achieve the value represented by the integrated baseline.

## Monitoring and Controlling

@source PMBOK 8, Standard 4.5.4

Actions required to **track, measure, review and regulate** the progress and performance of the project, **identify** where changes to the plan are needed, and **initiate** those changes.

| Monitoring | Controlling |
|---|---|
| Collecting and comparing performance data | Analyzing variances |
| Comparing actual with planned performance | Assessing trends |
| Producing performance measures | Evaluating alternatives |
| Reporting performance information | Recommending corrective actions |

When a variance is found, the project management team determines what **corrective action** is needed. Those actions are then negotiated and agreed with the appropriate stakeholders.

- It is performed **in parallel with the other Focus Areas**, for each phase and for the project as a whole. It is **not** a separate, stand-alone area.
- With a stable plan, the effort is fairly constant. With frequent change, as in adaptive work, the effort is less even.

**Key benefit:** ensures the project is on track to meet expectations.

## Closing

@source PMBOK 8, Standard 4.5.5

Actions performed to **formally complete or close a project, phase or contract**, or to **terminate** a project before completion.

- Includes verifying that the work of the other Focus Areas is finished before declaring completion.
- Includes verifying that **outputs, outcomes and benefits** were achieved.
- Includes the **transition to operations**.
- Also covers suspension or **early closure**, for example when cancelling is the best way to limit a negative return.

**Key benefit:** phases, projects and contracts are closed out properly.

## Focus Areas are not phases

@source PMBOK 8, Standard 4.5.6

This is the most tested idea in this topic.

| | Focus Areas | Phases |
|---|---|---|
| What they are | Groupings of project management actions | Divisions of the project's work over time |
| How many | Always five | As many as the project needs |
| Names | Fixed: Initiating to Closing | Chosen by the project: design, build, test and so on |
| Order | Overlap and repeat | Sequential, transitional or overlapping |

If a project has phases, **the Focus Areas occur within each phase**. A design phase is initiated, planned, executed, monitored and closed. So is the build phase.

**In a predictive project** the level of effort shifts gradually: Initiating is heavy at the start, Planning rises early, Executing dominates the middle, Closing rises at the end, and Monitoring and Controlling runs across all of it.

**In an adaptive project** the Focus Areas are revisited in **every iteration**: each short cycle includes planning, executing and monitoring, with initiating at the very beginning and closing at the end.

## Exam tips

- "Which process group includes creating the project charter?" **Initiating.** Identifying stakeholders is also Initiating.
- "When is monitoring and controlling done?" **Throughout the project**, alongside all other Focus Areas.
- If a question asks whether closing applies to a cancelled project: **yes**. Terminated projects are still formally closed.
- The business case is an **input** to the project, created before or outside it. The charter is created **in** Initiating.
- Do not say "the planning phase" when the question is about Focus Areas. Planning happens in every phase.
