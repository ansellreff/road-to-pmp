## Key points

- A **risk** is an **uncertain** event that would affect objectives if it happens. It can be a **threat** (negative) or an **opportunity** (positive). An **issue** has **already happened**.
- Six processes: **Plan Risk Management, Identify Risks, Perform Risk Analysis, Plan Risk Responses, Implement Risk Responses, Monitor Risks**.
- **Qualitative** analysis ranks risks by **probability and impact**. **Quantitative** analysis puts **numbers** on the combined effect.
- **Expected monetary value (EMV) = probability × impact.**
- Threat strategies: **escalate, avoid, transfer, mitigate, accept**. Opportunity strategies: **escalate, exploit, share, enhance, accept**.
- Every risk has a **risk owner**. Risks and responses are recorded in the **risk register**.
- Risk management is **iterative**: new risks are identified throughout the project.

## Risk, issue and the kinds of risk

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.7.1; PMBOK 8, Guide Figure 2-46; The Standard for Risk Management, 2.1

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Risk** | An uncertain event or condition that, if it occurs, has a positive or negative effect on one or more objectives |
| **Threat** | A risk with a **negative** effect: delay, cost overrun, damaged reputation |
| **Opportunity** | A risk with a **positive** effect: cost saving, earlier finish, larger market share |
| **Issue** | A **current** condition that affects objectives. It has already occurred and needs action now. A threat that occurs becomes an issue. |
| **Individual risk** | One uncertain event or condition |
| **Overall project risk** | The effect of uncertainty on the project **as a whole**. If it is too high, the organization may cancel the project. |

A risk can be written as **cause, event, consequence**: "Because the supplier is new (cause), deliveries may be late (event), which would delay installation by two weeks (consequence)."

| | Meaning | How it is handled |
|---|---|---|
| **Known-known** | Facts and requirements | Managed as part of scope. **Not a risk.** |
| **Known-unknown** | A classic risk: it can be identified, with a probability and impact | **Contingency reserve** |
| **Unknown-known** | A hidden fact: others know it, the team does not | Finding the knowledge |
| **Unknown-unknown** | An emergent risk nobody could foresee | **Management reserve** and **project resilience** |

**Project resilience** is the ability to absorb impacts and recover quickly from setbacks, including rare, high-impact "black swan" events.

## Risk appetite, threshold and attitude

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.7.1; The Standard for Risk Management, 2.1; Risk Management: A Practice Guide, 2.1.4

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Risk appetite** | The degree of uncertainty an organization or person is **willing to accept** in anticipation of a reward |
| **Risk threshold** | The **measurable** amount of variation around an objective that is acceptable, for example ±5% on cost. Above it, action is taken. |
| **Risk attitude** | A disposition toward uncertainty, ranging from **risk averse** to **risk seeking** |
| **Risk exposure** | The combined potential impact of all risks at a point in time |

A threshold of ±5% reflects a **lower** risk appetite than ±10%.

A threshold is the level of risk **below which** the organization accepts a risk and **above which** it does not. Examples: the minimum exposure for a risk to be entered in the risk register, or the maximum exposure the project can manage before the risk must be **escalated**.

## The six Risk processes

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.7.2; PMBOK 8, Guide Figure 2-47; Process Groups: A Practice Guide, Table 1-4; PMBOK 6, 1.2.4.6

| Focus Area | Process | Main output | Older name you may see |
|---|---|---|---|
| Planning | **Plan Risk Management** | Risk management plan | Plan Risk Management |
| Planning | **Identify Risks** | Risk register, risk report | Identify Risks |
| Planning | **Perform Risk Analysis** | Updates to the risk register and risk report | Perform Qualitative Risk Analysis, Perform Quantitative Risk Analysis |
| Planning | **Plan Risk Responses** | Updates to the risk register; change requests | Plan Risk Responses |
| Executing | **Implement Risk Responses** | Change requests, updated documents | Implement Risk Responses |
| Monitoring and Controlling | **Monitor Risks** | Work performance information, change requests | Monitor Risks |

The aim of the domain is to **increase** the probability and impact of opportunities and **decrease** the probability and impact of threats.

> **Edition note.** Older material has seven risk processes, with qualitative and quantitative analysis as two separate processes. The 8th edition combines them in Perform Risk Analysis. Questions still ask about the two kinds of analysis.

## Plan Risk Management

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.7.2.1; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Risk management plan

Defines **how** risk management will be done on the project. It starts when the project is conceived and is completed early.

The output is the **risk management plan**, a component of the project management plan. It may include:

- **Risk strategy** and **methodology**: the general approach, tools and data sources.
- **Roles and responsibilities** for risk activities.
- **Funding**: the money for risk management, and the rules for using contingency and management reserves.
- **Timing**: when and how often the risk processes are performed.
- **Risk categories**, often shown as a **risk breakdown structure (RBS)**: a hierarchy of sources of risk, such as technical, management, commercial and external.
- Definitions of **probability and impact**, and the probability and impact matrix to be used.

The risk management plan does **not** list the risks. Those are in the risk register.

## Identify Risks

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.7.2.2; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Risk register; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Risk report

Identifies **both threats and opportunities**, and separates real risks from concerns and issues. Not all risks can be found at the start, so the process is **iterative**. Everyone can identify risks: the team, stakeholders and experts.

| Technique | How it helps |
|---|---|
| **Brainstorming, interviews** | Gathering ideas from the team, stakeholders and experts |
| **Checklists** | Lists built from past projects. Quick, but never complete. |
| **Root cause analysis** | Finding the underlying causes that lead to risks |
| **Assumption and constraint analysis** | Testing whether assumptions are valid; a false assumption is a risk |
| **SWOT analysis** | Strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats. Widens the search to risks generated **inside** the organization. |
| **Prompt lists** | A set list of risk categories used to trigger ideas, such as PESTLE or VUCA |
| **Document analysis** | Reviewing plans, contracts and files for uncertainty |

| Output | Contents |
|---|---|
| **Risk register** | The **individual** risks: identifier, description, cause and effect, category, **risk owner**, **triggers**, potential responses, status. Updated by every later risk process. |
| **Risk report** | The sources of **overall** project risk, plus summary information on the individual risks |

A **risk trigger** is an event or condition that indicates a risk is about to occur.

## Perform Risk Analysis: qualitative

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.7.2.3; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Probability and impact matrix; Process Groups: A Practice Guide, Glossary (Risk Data Quality Assessment); Process Groups: A Practice Guide, Glossary (Risk Categorization)

**Qualitative analysis** assesses each individual risk's **probability** and **impact** to **prioritize** the risks. It is quick, subjective, and done for **all** risks, throughout the project.

The main tool is the **probability and impact matrix**: a grid that maps each risk's probability against its impact. Threats and opportunities can be shown on the same matrix.

| Probability \ Impact | Low (0.1) | Medium (0.4) | High (0.8) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **High (0.7)** | 0.07 | 0.28 | **0.56** |
| **Medium (0.5)** | 0.05 | 0.20 | **0.40** |
| **Low (0.2)** | 0.02 | 0.08 | 0.16 |

**Risk score = probability × impact.** A risk with probability 0.5 and impact 0.4 scores **0.20**. The highest scores get attention first.

Other things considered: how **urgent** the risk is, how manageable it is, when the impact would be felt, and links with other risks. Risks can also be grouped by **category** to find areas of concentration.

> **On the exam.** Two more techniques from the process guide. A **risk data quality assessment** evaluates how far the data about risks is **useful** for risk management: an analysis built on poor data cannot be trusted. **Risk categorization** groups risks by their source (using the risk breakdown structure), by the area of the project affected (using the WBS) or by another useful category such as phase, to find the areas most exposed to uncertainty.

## Perform Risk Analysis: quantitative

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.7.2.3; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Decision tree analysis; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Simulation

**Quantitative analysis** numerically analyzes the **combined effect** of the identified risks on overall project objectives. It is **not always required**: it needs good data and is used mainly on large or complex projects.

| | Qualitative | Quantitative |
|---|---|---|
| Looks at | Each risk on its own | The combined effect on the whole project |
| Result | A ranking (high, medium, low) | Numbers (money, days, probability) |
| Done for | All risks | When required; usually the highest risks |
| Order | First | After qualitative |

| Technique | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Expected monetary value (EMV)** | Probability × impact, in money |
| **Decision tree analysis** | Compares alternative choices by the EMV of each branch |
| **Simulation (Monte Carlo)** | Runs the model many times to show the range of possible outcomes |
| **Sensitivity analysis** | Shows which risks have the most effect; displayed as a **tornado diagram** |
| **Influence diagram** | A picture of how decisions, uncertainties and outcomes affect each other |

**EMV example.** Threats are negative, opportunities positive.

| Risk | Probability | Impact | EMV |
|---|---|---|---|
| Key supplier is late | 20% | −$50,000 | −$10,000 |
| Material prices fall | 30% | +$20,000 | +$6,000 |
| **Total** | | | **−$4,000** |

The total suggests a contingency of about $4,000 for these two risks.

**Decision tree example.** Which supplier is the better choice?

| Option | Price | Risk | Expected cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Supplier A | $100,000 | 20% chance of a $50,000 delay cost | 100,000 + (0.20 × 50,000) = **$110,000** |
| Supplier B | $108,000 | 5% chance of a $20,000 delay cost | 108,000 + (0.05 × 20,000) = **$109,000** |

Supplier B has the lower expected cost, although its price is higher.

## Plan Risk Responses: threats

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.7.2.4; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Strategies for threats; The Standard for Risk Management, 4.6

Develops options and agrees actions for individual risks and for overall project risk. Each response has a **risk owner**, and reserves and activities are added to the plans as needed.

| Strategy | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Avoid** | **Eliminate** the threat or protect the project from it: probability becomes zero | Removing a risky feature from scope; extending the schedule |
| **Transfer** | Shift **ownership** to a third party, who bears the impact. Usually costs a risk premium. | **Insurance**, warranties, performance bonds, a fixed-price contract |
| **Mitigate** | **Reduce** the probability and/or the impact | More testing, a prototype, a more reliable supplier, a backup component |
| **Accept** | Acknowledge the threat and take **no proactive action** | **Active:** set aside a contingency reserve. **Passive:** only review it periodically. |
| **Escalate** | The threat is **outside the project's scope** or beyond the project manager's authority; pass it to the program, portfolio or organization | A change in company policy that affects many projects |

- Transferring does **not remove** the risk. It moves the responsibility.
- After escalation, the project team **no longer monitors** the risk, though it may stay in the register for information.
- Acceptance suits **low-priority** risks, or risks that cannot be dealt with cost-effectively.

## Plan Risk Responses: opportunities

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Strategies for opportunities; The Standard for Risk Management, 4.6; PMBOK 6, 11.5.2.5

| Strategy | What it means | Example | Threat equivalent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Exploit** | Make sure the opportunity **definitely happens**: probability becomes 100% | Assigning the most talented people to finish early | Avoid |
| **Share** | Give ownership to a third party who is best able to capture it, and share the benefit | A joint venture or partnership | Transfer |
| **Enhance** | **Increase** the probability and/or the impact | Adding resources to an activity to finish early | Mitigate |
| **Accept** | Take the benefit if it happens, with no proactive action | Not pursuing a small possible discount | Accept |
| **Escalate** | Outside the project's scope or authority | An opportunity that benefits the whole program | Escalate |

## Other response terms

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Contingent response strategies; The Standard for Risk Management, 4.6; PMBOK 6, 11.5; The Project Management Answer Book, Chapter 8, Q20

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Contingent response (contingency plan)** | A response prepared in advance and used **only if a trigger occurs** |
| **Fallback plan** | The backup used if the planned response **does not work** |
| **Residual risk** | The risk that **remains** after the responses have been implemented |
| **Secondary risk** | A **new** risk that arises as a direct result of implementing a response |
| **Workaround** | An **unplanned** response to a risk or issue that has already occurred |
| **Risk owner** | The person responsible for monitoring a risk and for selecting and carrying out its response |

Example: to mitigate the risk of a late supplier, a second supplier is hired. The chance that the two suppliers' parts do not fit together is a **secondary risk**. The small remaining chance that both are late is **residual risk**.

The same strategies apply to **overall project risk**. The most extreme form of avoidance is **cancelling the project**.

## Implement Risk Responses and Monitor Risks

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.7.2.5; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.7.2.6; The Standard for Risk Management, 4.8

**Implement Risk Responses** makes sure the agreed responses are **actually carried out**. A common failure is to plan responses and then do nothing. The risk owner leads the action.

**Monitor Risks** runs throughout the project. It:

- tracks the identified risks and watches for **triggers**,
- identifies and analyzes **new**, secondary and residual risks,
- checks whether the responses are **effective**,
- checks whether the **reserves** are still enough for the remaining risk (**reserve analysis**),
- closes risks that can no longer occur.

| Technique | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Risk review / reassessment** | Regularly re-examining the risks, usually in status meetings |
| **Risk audit** | Examining how effective the **risk management process** itself is |
| **Reserve analysis** | Comparing the remaining reserve with the remaining risk |

## Risk in adaptive projects

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.7.3; Risk Management: A Practice Guide, 4.1.1

The risk processes are **tailored** to the project: its size and complexity, the organization's risk appetite and thresholds, its strategic importance, and whether the approach is predictive, adaptive or hybrid. A small, simple project may use a simplified process.

| | Predictive | Adaptive |
|---|---|---|
| When risks are identified | Mostly **up front** in planning, then reviewed periodically | **Continuously**, in every iteration; only high-level identification at the start |
| How it is organized | Structured, often with dedicated risk roles and standard processes | Built into the team's regular events: daily standups, sprint planning, retrospectives |
| Feedback | At longer intervals, such as the end of a phase, so strategies are harder to adjust | **Frequent delivery and feedback** expose risks and issues early |
| Change | Controlled | Expected and embraced, which helps the team change course when a risk appears |

- A **spike** is a short piece of work done early to remove a specific, high uncertainty (early de-risking).
- A **hybrid** project does an initial risk identification and plan, as in predictive work, and then handles risk iteration by iteration.

## Exam tips

- Has it happened yet? **No = risk** (risk register). **Yes = issue** (issue log).
- "Buy **insurance**" or "use a **fixed-price** contract" is **transfer**. "Do more **testing**" or "build a **prototype**" is **mitigate**. "**Remove** the risky work" is **avoid**.
- "Set aside a **contingency reserve**" is **active acceptance**.
- **Exploit** = make it certain. **Enhance** = make it more likely or bigger. They are easy to confuse.
- "Outside the project manager's **authority**" = **escalate**.
- A new risk **caused by a response** is **secondary**. What is **left over** is **residual**.
- A newly identified risk: **add it to the risk register**, then analyze it, then plan a response. Do not act before analyzing.
- Qualitative comes **before** quantitative. The **probability and impact matrix** belongs to qualitative.
- **EMV:** threats are entered as negative values, opportunities as positive.
- Risks are identified **throughout** the project, by **everyone**.
