## Key points

- This course is built on the **8th edition**. Most exam-prep courses, practice questions and possibly the exam itself still use terms from the **7th edition** and from ***Process Groups: A Practice Guide***.
- The ideas are largely the same. The **names and counts** changed.
- **12 principles** (7th) became **6 principles** (8th).
- **8 performance domains** (7th) became **7 performance domains** (8th), with different names.
- **5 Process Groups, 10 Knowledge Areas, 49 processes** (Practice Guide) became **5 Focus Areas, 7 performance domains, 40 processes** (8th).
- When a question uses an older term, translate it and answer with the same reasoning.

## Why this module exists

@source PMBOK 8, Appendix X5.3

PMI states that the 8th edition was built mainly from two earlier sources: the **PMBOK Guide, 7th edition (2021)** and **Process Groups: A Practice Guide (2022)**. It combined the 7th edition's principles and performance domains with the practice guide's processes.

The instructor course you are following alongside this one still teaches "the 12 principles", "the 8 performance domains" and "the 49 processes". You need to recognize both vocabularies.

| | 7th edition + Process Groups guide | 8th edition |
|---|---|---|
| Principles | 12 | 6 |
| Performance domains | 8 | 7 |
| Groupings of management actions | 5 Process Groups | 5 Focus Areas (same names) |
| Knowledge Areas | 10 | None; replaced by the 7 performance domains |
| Processes | 49 | 40 |
| Tailoring | A principle and a section | A section only |

## The 12 principles and where they went

@source PMBOK 8, Appendix X5, Table X5-2; PMBOK 7, Standard section 3

| 7th edition principle | Short name | In the 8th edition |
|---|---|---|
| Be a diligent, respectful, and caring steward | Stewardship | Merged into **Be an Accountable Leader** |
| Create a collaborative project team environment | Team | Refined into **Build an Empowered Culture** |
| Effectively engage with stakeholders | Stakeholders | Merged into **Focus on Value**, plus the Stakeholders performance domain |
| Focus on value | Value | **Focus on Value** |
| Recognize, evaluate, and respond to system interactions | Systems thinking | Merged into **Adopt a Holistic View** |
| Demonstrate leadership behaviors | Leadership | Merged into **Be an Accountable Leader** |
| Tailor based on context | Tailoring | Moved to the **Tailoring section** |
| Build quality into processes and deliverables | Quality | Refined into **Embed Quality Into Processes and Deliverables** |
| Navigate complexity | Complexity | Merged into **Adopt a Holistic View** |
| Optimize risk responses | Risk | Moved to the **Risk performance domain** |
| Embrace adaptability and resiliency | Adaptability and resiliency | Merged into **Focus on Value** |
| Enable change to achieve the envisioned future state | Change | Merged into **Focus on Value** |

One 8th-edition principle has no 7th-edition ancestor in this table: **Integrate Sustainability Within All Project Areas** is new as a principle (the 7th edition touched on it under stewardship).

**Why PMI changed them.** Practitioners supported a principle-based standard but asked for principles that were more **actionable** and more **focused**, without the same topic appearing as both a principle and a performance domain (stakeholders) or as both a principle and a section (tailoring).

What each of the 12 means, in one line:

- **Stewardship:** act with integrity, care, trustworthiness and compliance.
- **Team:** build a collaborative environment with shared ownership.
- **Stakeholders:** engage them proactively and as much as needed.
- **Value:** keep evaluating and adjusting toward benefits and value.
- **Systems thinking:** see the project as a system of interacting parts.
- **Leadership:** anyone can show leadership; adapt the style to the situation.
- **Tailoring:** design the approach to fit the context.
- **Quality:** meet acceptance criteria and stakeholder expectations.
- **Complexity:** keep evaluating and navigating complexity from people, systems and ambiguity.
- **Risk:** address threats and opportunities to maximize positive and minimize negative effects.
- **Adaptability and resiliency:** respond to change and recover from setbacks.
- **Change:** prepare people to adopt the new state the project creates.

## The 8 performance domains of the 7th edition

@source PMBOK 7, Guide section 2

| 7th edition domain | What it covers | Closest home in the 8th edition |
|---|---|---|
| **Stakeholders** | Identifying, understanding and engaging stakeholders | Stakeholders domain |
| **Team** | Leadership, team culture, high-performing teams | Resources domain; Be an Accountable Leader; Build an Empowered Culture |
| **Development Approach and Life Cycle** | Predictive, adaptive, hybrid; phases; cadence | Standard section 4, Project Life Cycles |
| **Planning** | Estimating, scheduling, budgeting, planning for everything else | Planning Focus Area, across all domains |
| **Project Work** | Processes, physical resources, procurement, learning | Governance and Resources domains |
| **Delivery** | Requirements, scope, quality, delivering value | Scope domain |
| **Measurement** | Metrics, earned value, dashboards, forecasting | Monitoring and Controlling Focus Area; Schedule and Finance domains |
| **Uncertainty** | Risk, ambiguity, complexity, volatility | Risk domain |

The right-hand column is my own mapping by subject matter. PMI does not publish a one-to-one table for the domains.

## Process Groups, Knowledge Areas and the 49 processes

@source Process Groups: A Practice Guide, 1.7.5; Process Groups: A Practice Guide, section 4

The **five Process Groups** are the five Focus Areas under their old name: Initiating, Planning, Executing, Monitoring and Controlling, Closing.

The **ten Knowledge Areas** group processes by subject:

1. Integration
2. Scope
3. Schedule
4. Cost
5. Quality
6. Resource
7. Communications
8. Risk
9. Procurement
10. Stakeholder

How the 49 processes are distributed:

| Process Group | Processes | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Initiating | 2 | Develop Project Charter; Identify Stakeholders |
| Planning | 24 | Develop Project Management Plan; Create WBS; Develop Schedule; Determine Budget; Identify Risks |
| Executing | 10 | Direct and Manage Project Work; Manage Quality; Develop Team; Conduct Procurements |
| Monitoring and Controlling | 12 | Monitor and Control Project Work; Perform Integrated Change Control; Validate Scope; Control Quality |
| Closing | 1 | Close Project or Phase |

## Process names that changed

@source Process Groups: A Practice Guide, section 5; PMBOK 8, Guide Table 2-1

| Process Groups guide (49) | 8th edition (40) |
|---|---|
| Develop Project Charter | Initiate Project or Phase |
| Develop Project Management Plan | Integrate and Align Project Plans |
| Direct and Manage Project Work | Manage Project Execution |
| Monitor and Control Project Work | Monitor and Control Project Performance |
| Perform Integrated Change Control | Assess and Implement Changes |
| Collect Requirements | Elicit and Analyze Requirements |
| Create WBS | Develop Scope Structure |
| Define Activities, Sequence Activities, Estimate Activity Durations, Develop Schedule | Develop Schedule |
| Control Schedule | Monitor and Control Schedule |
| Determine Budget | Develop Budget |
| Control Costs | Monitor and Control Finances |
| Manage Quality | Manage Quality Assurance |
| Estimate Activity Resources | Estimate Resources |
| Develop Team, Manage Team | Lead the Team |
| Control Resources | Monitor and Control Resourcing |
| Perform Qualitative Risk Analysis, Perform Quantitative Risk Analysis | Perform Risk Analysis |
| Plan Procurement Management | Plan Sourcing Strategy |

Unchanged names include Identify Stakeholders, Plan Scope Management, Define Scope, Validate Scope, Plan Schedule Management, Estimate Costs, Plan Risk Management, Identify Risks, Plan Risk Responses, Implement Risk Responses, Monitor Risks, Acquire Resources, Manage Communications, Manage Stakeholder Engagement and Close Project or Phase.

This table is also my own matching by name and purpose, made by comparing the two books' process lists. It is a study aid, not an official PMI crosswalk. Domain 2 of this course teaches each 8th-edition process and notes its older name.

## Other terms that shifted

| Older term | 8th edition term |
|---|---|
| Process Groups | Focus Areas |
| Knowledge Areas | Performance domains (not identical, but the nearest equivalent) |
| Cost | Finance |
| Project: "temporary endeavor to create a unique product, service or result" | "Temporary initiative in a unique context undertaken to create value" |
| Technical project management, leadership, strategic and business management | Ways of working, power skills, business acumen |
| Build quality in | Embed quality |
| ITTOs listed as required parts of each process | ITTOs offered as illustrative samples |

## How to handle it in the exam

1. **Read the term, find its family.** "Knowledge Area", "Process Group", "49 processes": Process Groups guide. "12 principles", "Team performance domain", "Uncertainty domain": 7th edition. "Focus Area", "Governance domain", "Finance domain": 8th edition.
2. **Translate if needed**, using the tables above.
3. **Answer with the same logic.** Whatever the vocabulary, PMI's reasoning is constant: focus on value, engage stakeholders, be proactive about risk, build quality in, tailor to context, act ethically.

## Exam tips

- "Which process group has the most processes?" **Planning** (24 of 49 in the practice guide; also the most in the 8th edition).
- "In which process group is the project charter created?" **Initiating.**
- "How many knowledge areas?" **10.** "How many process groups?" **5.**
- If a question lists stewardship, tailoring or complexity as *principles*, it is using the 7th edition.
- Do not memorize both lists of processes yet. Learn to recognize the old names; Domain 2 will make them familiar.
