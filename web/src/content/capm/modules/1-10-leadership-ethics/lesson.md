## Key points

- **Management** directs work using position and process. **Leadership** guides people through influence, vision and example. A project manager needs both.
- **Emotional intelligence (EI)** has four areas: self-awareness, self-management, social awareness and social skills. It is the basis of all forms of leadership.
- There are **five conflict resolution techniques**. **Collaborate / problem-solve** is the one that produces a win-win.
- The **PMI Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct** rests on four values: **responsibility, respect, fairness and honesty**.
- Each value has **aspirational** standards (what we strive for) and **mandatory** standards (firm requirements, with disciplinary consequences).

## Leadership and management

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Leadership; Process Groups: A Practice Guide, 3.3.2; PMBOK 6, 3.4.5; PMBOK 7, Guide 2.2

**Leadership** is the knowledge, skills and behaviors needed to guide, motivate and direct a team toward its goals. It includes negotiation, resilience, communication, problem-solving, critical thinking and interpersonal skills. The 8th edition puts it plainly: the common denominator in all projects is people, and people can be counted, but they are not numbers.

The classic PMI comparison:

| Management | Leadership |
|---|---|
| Directs using positional power | Guides and influences using relational power |
| Maintains and administers | Develops and innovates |
| Focuses on systems and structure | Focuses on relationships with people |
| Relies on control | Inspires trust |
| Near-term goals | Long-range vision |
| Asks how and when | Asks what and why |
| "Do things right" | "Do the right things" |

Neither is better. Managing keeps the work organized; leading keeps people committed. Recall from module 1.5 that leadership is not tied to a job title and that authority and leadership are different things.

> This table comes from long-standing PMI teaching (it appeared in the 6th edition) and is still commonly tested. The 8th edition covers the same ground through the principle Be an Accountable Leader.

> **On the exam.** The 7th edition covers leadership, team culture and high-performing teams in its **Team performance domain**, and has a principle named "Demonstrate leadership behaviors".

## Leadership structures and styles

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Centralized management and leadership; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Servant leadership

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Centralized management and leadership** | Top-down. Decision-making and authority sit with top management |
| **Distributed management and leadership** | Decision-making and authority are spread among individuals and teams. A team may self-organize, with someone acting as facilitator instead of a designated project manager |
| **Servant leadership** | Leading by understanding and addressing the needs and development of team members, to enable the highest possible team performance, in service of the project's objectives |
| **Situational leadership** | Adapting the style to the team's needs, the urgency and the complexity (module 1.3) |

Leadership activities should be practiced by **all** team members, whether management is centralized or distributed.

## Emotional intelligence

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Interpersonal skills

**Emotional intelligence** is the ability to identify, assess and manage your own emotions and those of other people, as well as the collective emotions of groups.

| | Awareness | Management and skill |
|---|---|---|
| **Self** | **Self-awareness:** realistic self-assessment. Understanding your own emotions, goals, motivations, strengths and weaknesses | **Self-management** (self-regulation): controlling and redirecting disruptive feelings and impulses. Thinking before acting |
| **Social** | **Social awareness:** empathy. Understanding other people's feelings, reading non-verbal cues and body language, active listening | **Social skills:** managing groups, building rapport and networks, finding common ground. The culmination of the other three |

- Self-awareness and self-management keep you **calm and productive** in difficult circumstances.
- Social awareness and social skills build **better bonds** with the team and stakeholders.
- Some models add a fifth area, **motivation**: understanding what drives and inspires people.

## What makes a high-performing team

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.6.2.4.2

Effective leadership aims at these characteristics:

- **open communication** in a safe environment
- **shared understanding** of the project's purpose and benefits
- **shared ownership** of the outcomes
- **trust**
- **collaboration** instead of silos or competition
- **adaptability**
- **resilience**: recovering quickly from issues and failures
- **empowerment**, delegation and autonomy, as opposed to micromanagement
- **recognition**: even simple appreciation reinforces good behavior

Team development models (the Tuckman ladder) and motivation theories (Herzberg, McClelland, McGregor, Pink) are covered with the Resources domain in module 2.9.

## Conflict management

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Conflict management

**Conflict is inevitable** on projects. Common sources are **scarce resources, scheduling priorities and personal work styles**. Team ground rules, clear roles and good communication planning reduce it. Managed well, differences of opinion improve creativity and decisions.

How conflict should be handled:

1. Team members are **initially responsible** for resolving their own conflicts.
2. If it escalates, the **project manager helps facilitate** a resolution.
3. Address it **early**, usually **in private**, using a **direct, collaborative** approach.
4. Only if disruptive conflict continues are **formal procedures**, including disciplinary action, used.

The five techniques:

| Technique | What it means | Typical result |
|---|---|---|
| **Collaborate / problem-solve** | Combine viewpoints through open dialogue to reach consensus and commitment | Win-win |
| **Compromise / reconcile** | Each side gives something up; partial or temporary resolution | Can be lose-lose |
| **Smooth / accommodate** | Stress areas of agreement; concede to keep harmony | Relationship kept, issue may remain |
| **Force / direct** | Push one view at the expense of others, usually from a position of power; suited to emergencies | Win-lose |
| **Withdraw / avoid** | Retreat or postpone, to be better prepared or let others resolve it | Issue unresolved for now |

Which to use depends on the importance and intensity of the conflict, time pressure, the relative power of those involved, the importance of the relationship, and whether a long-term or short-term fix is needed.

## The PMI Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct

@source PMI Code of Ethics, 1.2; PMI Code of Ethics, 1.5

**Purpose:** to instill confidence in the project management profession and to help individuals become better practitioners.

**Who it applies to:**

- PMI members
- PMI certification holders
- applicants commencing a PMI certification process
- PMI volunteers

As a CAPM applicant, it already applies to you.

**Structure:** four values, each with two kinds of standards.

| | Aspirational standards | Mandatory standards |
|---|---|---|
| What they are | The conduct we strive to uphold | Firm requirements that may limit or prohibit behavior |
| Measurable? | Not easily | Yes |
| If not followed | A shared professional expectation is missed | Disciplinary procedures before PMI's **Ethics Review Committee** |

One act can violate both kinds at once.

## The four values

@source PMI Code of Ethics, 1.4

### Responsibility

@source PMI Code of Ethics, chapter 2

**Our duty to take ownership for the decisions we make or fail to make, the actions we take or fail to take, and the resulting consequences.**

Aspirational:

- decide and act in the best interests of society, public health and safety, and the environment
- take responsibility for how we lead, manage and deploy technology, including **artificial intelligence**
- accept only assignments consistent with our background, experience, skills and qualifications (and disclose any gaps for a stretch assignment)
- when we make or discover errors or omissions, take prompt corrective action and own the consequences
- fulfill commitments: do what we say we will do
- protect proprietary and confidential information
- uphold the Code and hold one another accountable

Mandatory:

- know and uphold the policies, rules, regulations and laws that govern our work
- **report unethical or illegal conduct** to appropriate management and, if necessary, to those affected
- bring violations of the Code to the appropriate authority
- file ethics complaints **only when substantiated by facts**
- **do not retaliate** against anyone who raises a good-faith ethical concern

### Respect

@source PMI Code of Ethics, chapter 3

**Our duty to show a high regard for ourselves, others and the resources entrusted to us** (people, money, reputation, the safety of others, and natural resources).

Aspirational:

- be inclusive leaders, be aware of our biases, build diverse teams
- learn the norms and customs of others and avoid behavior they might find disrespectful
- listen actively to others' points of view
- when in conflict, **approach the person directly**
- avoid gossip and remarks that undermine someone's reputation
- behave professionally even when it is not reciprocated

Mandatory:

- **negotiate in good faith**
- do not use the power of our expertise or position to benefit personally at others' expense
- do not act in an abusive manner
- respect the property rights of others, including intellectual property

### Fairness

@source PMI Code of Ethics, chapter 4

**Our duty to make decisions and act impartially and objectively.** Conduct must be free from competing self-interest, prejudice and favoritism.

Aspirational:

- be transparent in decision-making
- constantly re-examine our impartiality and objectivity
- give equal access to information to those authorized to have it
- make opportunities equally available to qualified candidates

Mandatory:

- **proactively and fully disclose any real or potential conflict of interest** to the appropriate stakeholders
- when a conflict of interest exists, **stay out of the decision** until there is full disclosure, an approved mitigation plan and the stakeholders' consent
- do not hire, fire, reward, punish or award contracts based on favoritism, nepotism or bribery
- do not discriminate
- apply the organization's rules without favoritism or prejudice

A **conflict of interest** arises when you can influence a decision for one party that could affect another party to which you also owe loyalty. The **appearance** of a conflict is treated as a conflict.

### Honesty

@source PMI Code of Ethics, chapter 5

**Our duty to understand the truth and act in a truthful manner, both in our communications and in our conduct.**

Aspirational:

- earnestly seek to understand the truth
- be truthful in communication and conduct
- provide accurate information in a timely manner, **including bad news**
- make commitments and promises in good faith
- create an environment where others feel safe to tell the truth

Mandatory:

- do not deceive: no false or misleading statements, **half-truths**, information out of context, or withholding information that would make a statement misleading
- do not behave dishonestly for personal gain or at another's expense

For all four values, **condoning** the wrong behavior in others is itself a violation.

## Exam tips

- Ethics questions usually have one answer that is uncomfortable but right: **disclose, report, tell the truth, deliver the bad news**. Choose it.
- Offered a gift or favor by a vendor during bidding? It is a **conflict of interest**: disclose it and step back from the decision.
- A colleague is breaking the law or the Code? **Report it** to the appropriate management or authority. But you need **facts**, not suspicion, to file a complaint.
- Asked to take work you are not qualified for? **Disclose the gap** so stakeholders can decide.
- You find your own mistake after a report was sent? **Correct it promptly** and inform those affected.
- For conflict between team members, the first step is that they try to resolve it themselves, then the project manager facilitates **privately and collaboratively**.
- "Which conflict technique gives the most lasting result?" **Collaborate / problem-solve.**
- Match the value: ownership and reporting = **responsibility**; listening, good-faith negotiation, no abuse = **respect**; conflict of interest, impartiality = **fairness**; truth, no half-truths = **honesty**.
