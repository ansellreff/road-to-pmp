## Key points

- The exam's process reference is ***Process Groups: A Practice Guide***. It describes **49 processes** in **5 process groups**.
- The five process groups: **Initiating, Planning, Executing, Monitoring and Controlling, Closing**. They are **not phases**.
- **Planning** has the most processes (**24**). **Initiating** has 2. **Closing** has 1.
- The same 49 processes are also sorted into **10 knowledge areas** by subject. That grouping comes from the **PMBOK Guide, Sixth Edition**, and exam material still uses its names.
- The **project charter** authorizes the project. The **project management plan** says how it will be executed, monitored, controlled and closed. **Project documents** are everything else.
- **Work performance data** becomes **information**, which becomes **reports**.
- The 8th edition lessons in this course use newer names. This module gives the names the exam uses.

## Why this module matters

@source Process Groups: A Practice Guide, 1.7.4; CAPM Exam Content Outline, Domain 2

The CAPM Exam Content Outline lists *Process Groups: A Practice Guide* among its references, and Domain 2 asks you to **"determine the activities within each process"**. The 8th edition regrouped these processes into 40 and renamed many. For the exam, learn the **49 names** and **where each belongs**.

A **process** is a systematic series of activities directed toward an end result: **inputs** are acted on with **tools and techniques** to create **outputs**. The output of one process is often the input of another.

## The five process groups

@source Process Groups: A Practice Guide, 1.7.5

A **process group** is a logical grouping of processes to achieve specific project objectives.

| Process group | What its processes do | Number |
|---|---|---|
| **Initiating** | Define a new project or phase by obtaining **authorization** to start | 2 |
| **Planning** | Establish the scope, refine the objectives, and define the **course of action** | 24 |
| **Executing** | **Complete the work** defined in the project management plan | 10 |
| **Monitoring and Controlling** | **Track, review and regulate** progress; identify and start needed changes | 12 |
| **Closing** | Formally **complete or close** the project, phase or contract | 1 |

- Process groups are **independent of project phases**. All five can occur inside one phase.
- They **overlap and repeat**. Planning continues during executing; monitoring and controlling runs throughout.

## The ten knowledge areas

@source PMBOK 6, 1.2.4.6

A **knowledge area** is an identified area of project management defined by its knowledge requirements. Each process belongs to one process group **and** one knowledge area.

| Knowledge area | About | 8th edition performance domain |
|---|---|---|
| **Integration** | Combining and coordinating all the other processes | Governance |
| **Scope** | All the work required, and only the work required | Scope |
| **Schedule** | Timely completion | Schedule |
| **Cost** | Completing within the approved budget | Finance |
| **Quality** | Meeting stakeholders' quality expectations | Scope and Governance |
| **Resource** | Identifying, acquiring and managing people and physical resources | Resources |
| **Communications** | Timely and appropriate handling of project information | Stakeholders |
| **Risk** | Identifying, analyzing and responding to risk | Risk |
| **Procurement** | Buying products and services from outside the team | Governance, and the Procurement appendix |
| **Stakeholder** | Identifying and engaging the people affected | Stakeholders |

*Process Groups: A Practice Guide* itself lists the processes by process group only. The knowledge areas come from the Sixth Edition, where each has a chapter. "Integration management" and "quality management plan" in the exam outline are knowledge-area terms.

Patterns that make the table easier to remember:

- **Integration** is the only knowledge area with a process in **every** process group.
- Only **Integration** and **Stakeholder** have an Initiating process.
- Only **Integration** has a Closing process.
- **Scope, Schedule and Cost** have **no Executing process**: their work is done under Direct and Manage Project Work.
- Every knowledge area starts planning with a **"Plan ... Management"** process (Integration's is Develop Project Management Plan; Stakeholder's is Plan Stakeholder Engagement).
- Monitoring and Controlling processes start with **Control**, **Monitor** or **Validate**.

## Executing, Monitoring and Controlling, and Closing

@source Process Groups: A Practice Guide, section 6; Process Groups: A Practice Guide, section 7; Process Groups: A Practice Guide, section 8

**Executing (10):**

| Process | What happens |
|---|---|
| Direct and Manage Project Work | The planned work is performed; **deliverables** are produced |
| Manage Project Knowledge | Existing knowledge is used and new knowledge created; lessons learned are recorded |
| Manage Quality | The quality plan is turned into quality activities (quality assurance) |
| Acquire Resources | Team members and physical resources are obtained |
| Develop Team | Competencies, interaction and team environment are improved |
| Manage Team | Performance is tracked, feedback given, conflicts resolved |
| Manage Communications | Information is created, distributed and stored |
| Implement Risk Responses | Agreed risk responses are carried out |
| Conduct Procurements | Sellers are selected and contracts awarded |
| Manage Stakeholder Engagement | Stakeholders' needs and issues are worked on |

**Monitoring and Controlling (12):**

| Process | What happens |
|---|---|
| Monitor and Control Project Work | Overall progress is tracked and reported |
| Perform Integrated Change Control | **Change requests** are reviewed, approved or rejected |
| Validate Scope | The **customer formally accepts** completed deliverables |
| Control Scope | Scope status is monitored; changes to the scope baseline are managed |
| Control Schedule | Schedule status is monitored; changes to the schedule baseline are managed |
| Control Costs | Cost status is monitored; changes to the cost baseline are managed |
| Control Quality | Deliverables are **checked for correctness** |
| Control Resources | Physical resources are checked against the plan |
| Monitor Communications | Information needs are checked as being met |
| Monitor Risks | Risks and responses are tracked; new risks identified |
| Control Procurements | Seller performance and contracts are managed |
| Monitor Stakeholder Engagement | Engagement is tracked and strategies adjusted |

**Closing (1):** **Close Project or Phase** finalizes all activities, hands over the final product, and archives the records and lessons learned.

Two pairs the exam likes:

| Pair | Difference |
|---|---|
| **Control Quality** and **Validate Scope** | Control Quality checks that the deliverable is **correct** (done by the team). Validate Scope gets it **accepted** (done by the customer). Control Quality usually comes first. |
| **Manage Quality** and **Control Quality** | Manage Quality is about the **process** (executing). Control Quality is about the **product** (monitoring and controlling). |

## Charter, project management plan and project documents

@source Process Groups: A Practice Guide, 1.11; Process Groups: A Practice Guide, Table 1-6

| | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Project charter** | Issued by the **sponsor**. Formally **authorizes** the project and gives the project manager authority to use resources. |
| **Project management plan** | Describes **how** the project will be **executed, monitored and controlled, and closed** |
| **Project documents** | Other documents used to manage the project. They are **not part of the plan**. |

**The project management plan has 18 components:**

| Group | Components |
|---|---|
| **Subsidiary management plans (10)** | Scope, requirements, schedule, cost, quality, resource, communications, risk, procurement, stakeholder engagement |
| **Additional plans (2)** | Change management plan; configuration management plan |
| **Baselines (4)** | Scope baseline, schedule baseline, cost baseline, performance measurement baseline |
| **Other (2)** | Project life cycle description; development approach |

**Examples of project documents** (33 are listed): assumption log, issue log, risk register, stakeholder register, lessons learned register, change log, project schedule, requirements documentation, requirements traceability matrix, team charter.

The classic trap: the **risk register, issue log and stakeholder register are project documents**. The **risk management plan** and **stakeholder engagement plan** are parts of the project management plan.

## Data, information and reports

@source Process Groups: A Practice Guide, Table 1-4; Process Groups: A Practice Guide, 1.8

| Term | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Work performance data** | **Raw** observations and measurements from the work | 12 of 20 activities are finished; $40,000 spent |
| **Work performance information** | Data **analyzed in context** by the controlling processes | The project is 10% behind schedule |
| **Work performance reports** | Information **compiled for a purpose**: to raise awareness or prompt decisions | The monthly status report; a dashboard |

Data comes from **executing**. Information comes from the **controlling** processes. Reports come from **Monitor and Control Project Work**.

## Projects drive change

@source Process Groups: A Practice Guide, 1.2.1; Process Groups: A Practice Guide, 1.2

A project is a **temporary endeavor undertaken to create a unique product, service or result**.

**Projects drive change in organizations.** A project moves an organization from its **current state** to a desired **future state** in order to achieve a specific objective. This is what the exam outline means by a project being a "**vehicle for change**".

A project ends when one of these is true:

- its objectives have been **achieved**
- its objectives **will not or cannot** be met
- **funding** is exhausted
- the **need** no longer exists
- people or physical **resources** are no longer available
- it is **terminated** for legal cause or convenience

## Exam tips

- "Which process group?" Look at the verb. **Plan, define, estimate, identify, develop (a plan or schedule)**: Planning. **Direct, manage, acquire, conduct, implement**: Executing. **Control, monitor, validate**: Monitoring and Controlling.
- **Identify Stakeholders** is **Initiating**. **Identify Risks** is **Planning**.
- **Develop Project Charter** is Initiating. **Develop Project Management Plan** and **Develop Schedule** are Planning. **Develop Team** is Executing.
- **Create WBS** is in **Planning**, in the **Scope** knowledge area.
- A change request is decided in **Perform Integrated Change Control**.
- Customer acceptance of a deliverable is **Validate Scope**.
- The risk register is a **project document**, not part of the project management plan.
- **Integration** is the knowledge area the project manager cannot delegate: it ties the others together.
- The 8th edition calls the process groups **focus areas**. They have the same five names.
