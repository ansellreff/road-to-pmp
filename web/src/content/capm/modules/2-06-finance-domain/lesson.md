## Key points

- The **Finance** domain has four processes: **Plan Financial Management**, **Estimate Costs**, **Develop Budget** and **Monitor and Control Finances**.
- **Estimate Costs** approximates the cost of the resources needed. **Develop Budget** adds the estimates up and produces the **cost baseline**.
- **Cost baseline** = cost estimates + **contingency reserve**. **Project budget** = cost baseline + **management reserve**.
- **Contingency reserve** is for **identified risks** (known-unknowns). **Management reserve** is for **unforeseen work** (unknown-unknowns) and is not part of the cost baseline.
- Estimates get **more accurate** as the project progresses.
- **Cost of quality** = cost of conformance (prevention, appraisal) + cost of nonconformance (internal and external failure).
- Changing the cost baseline needs **formal change control**.

## What the Finance domain covers

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.4; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.4.1

The Finance domain covers planning, estimating, budgeting, financing, funding, managing and controlling costs so that the project **optimizes value** for the organization. It is not only about spending less.

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Value** | May be financial (return on investment, payback) or non-financial (compliance, social impact, customer satisfaction) |
| **Funding** | Where the money comes from: internal budgets, customer contracts, grants |
| **Financial constraints** | The budget, and also rules about the kind of money available and when it must be spent (for example within a fiscal year) |
| **CapEx** (capital expenditure) | Money invested to acquire, upgrade or maintain **physical assets**: buildings, equipment, technology |
| **OpEx** (operational expenditure) | Ongoing costs of **running the business**: wages, rent, utilities, advertising |
| **Life cycle cost** | The cost of using, maintaining and supporting the result after the project. A cheaper project can create a more expensive product. |

> **Edition note.** Older material calls this area Cost Management. The 8th edition widens it to Finance, adding funding and the value of the project.

## The four Finance processes

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.4.2; PMBOK 8, Guide Figure 2-26

| Focus Area | Process | Main output | Older name you may see |
|---|---|---|---|
| Planning | **Plan Financial Management** | Financial management plan, funding strategy | Plan Cost Management |
| Planning | **Estimate Costs** | Cost estimates, basis of estimates | Estimate Costs |
| Planning | **Develop Budget** | **Cost baseline**, project funding requirements | Determine Budget |
| Monitoring and Controlling | **Monitor and Control Finances** | Work performance information, revenue and cost forecasts, change requests | Control Costs |

## Plan Financial Management

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.4.2.1; PMBOK 8, Guide Figure 2-27

Defines **how** project revenues and expenses will be estimated, budgeted, managed, monitored and controlled. It is done once up front or at set points.

- Output: the **financial management plan** (older name: cost management plan), a component of the project management plan, and the **funding strategy**.
- It sets things such as units of measure, level of accuracy, control thresholds and reporting formats.
- It contains **no cost figures**. Those come from the next two processes.

## Estimate Costs

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.4.2.2; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Cost estimates; Practice Standard for Project Estimating, 2.4; PMBOK 6, 7.2

Develops an **approximation of the cost of the resources** needed to complete the work. It is repeated through the project as more becomes known.

- A cost estimate is a **prediction** based on what is known at a point in time.
- Costs are estimated for **everything charged to the project**: labor, materials, equipment, services, facilities, and special items such as inflation or the cost of financing.
- Estimates consider **alternatives**: make or buy, buy or lease, sharing resources.
- Outputs: **cost estimates** and the **basis of estimates** (how each estimate was produced, with assumptions and range).
- **Accuracy increases** as the project progresses.

| Estimate type | When | Typical range |
|---|---|---|
| **Rough order of magnitude (ROM)** | Early, during initiating | −25% to +75% |
| **Definitive estimate** | Later, when the work is well defined | −5% to +10% |

The narrowing of the range over time is called the **cone of uncertainty**. As the range narrows, less contingency reserve is needed for estimating uncertainty.

> **Note on the figures.** The ROM range of −25% to +75% is given in PMI's estimating standard. For later estimates that standard gives −10% to +15% as an example and says organizations set their own guidelines. The −5% to +10% "definitive" range is given in the PMBOK Guide, Sixth Edition, section 7.2, and is the one most often used in exam preparation material.

## Cost estimating techniques

@source PMBOK 8, Guide Figure 2-28; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Estimation techniques

The same techniques used for durations are used for costs.

| Technique | How it works | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Analogous** | Uses the actual cost of a similar past project. Quick, less accurate. | "The last warehouse cost $2 million, so this one will be about the same." |
| **Parametric** | Uses a rate: quantity × cost per unit | 200 m² × $1,500 per m² = $300,000 |
| **Bottom-up** | Estimates each work package or activity and adds them up. Most accurate, most effort. | Summing 40 work package estimates |
| **Three-point** (multipoint) | Uses optimistic, most likely and pessimistic costs | (O + M + P) ÷ 3, or (O + 4M + P) ÷ 6 |
| **Reserve analysis** | Works out how much contingency is needed for the remaining risk | Adding 10% for known risks |
| **Cost of quality** | Considers what will be spent on preventing and fixing defects | See below |

## Types of cost

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Cost estimates; The Project Management Answer Book, Chapter 6, Q5; The Project Management Answer Book, Chapter 6, Q7; The Project Management Answer Book, Chapter 6, Q8

| Type | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct cost** | Charged directly to this project | Team wages, project materials |
| **Indirect cost** | Shared across several projects (overhead) | Office rent, electricity, management salaries |
| **Fixed cost** | Does not change with the amount of work | Equipment rental, a one-time license |
| **Variable cost** | Changes with the amount of work | Materials, hourly labor |
| **Sunk cost** | Money **already spent** that cannot be recovered | $300,000 spent so far on a failing project |

**Sunk costs should not be considered** when deciding whether to continue a project. Only future costs and benefits matter.

## Cost of quality

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Cost of quality (CoQ); PMBOK 8, Guide Figure 5-3

The **cost of quality (CoQ)** is all the money spent over the life of the product on preventing defects, checking for them and dealing with failures.

| Group | Category | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Cost of conformance**: money spent **to avoid failures** | **Prevention costs** (build a quality product) | Training, documenting processes, proper equipment, time to do it right |
| | **Appraisal costs** (assess the quality) | Testing, inspections, destructive testing loss |
| **Cost of nonconformance**: money spent **because of failures** | **Internal failure costs** (found by the project) | Rework, scrap |
| | **External failure costs** (found by the customer) | Warranty work, liabilities, lost business |

- **Prevention is cheaper than correction.** Money spent on conformance reduces the much higher cost of failures.
- External failures are the most expensive, because they also damage reputation.

## Develop Budget

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.4.2.3; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Cost aggregation; PMBOK 8, Guide Figure 2-25

Adds up the estimated costs of activities or work packages to establish an authorized **cost baseline**.

**Cost aggregation** rolls the numbers up the WBS:

1. Activity cost estimates are added into **work package** estimates.
2. Work packages are added into **control accounts**.
3. Control accounts plus the **contingency reserve** give the **cost baseline**.
4. The cost baseline plus the **management reserve** gives the **project budget**.

**Example.**

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Work package cost estimates | $100,000 |
| + Contingency reserve | $10,000 |
| **= Cost baseline** | **$110,000** |
| + Management reserve | $5,000 |
| **= Project budget** | **$115,000** |

> **Edition note.** The 8th edition shows a second possible arrangement, in which an organization keeps the contingency reserve outside the cost baseline. The arrangement above (contingency inside, management reserve outside) is the standard one and matches the Guide's own definition of the cost baseline.

## The cost baseline and funding

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Cost baseline; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Project funding requirements; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Funding limit reconciliation; PMBOK 6, 7.3.3.1

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Cost baseline** | The approved, **time-phased** project budget, **excluding management reserve**. Changed only through formal change control; used for comparison with actual results. |
| **Budget at completion (BAC)** | The sum of all budgets established for the work to be performed |
| **Project funding requirements** | The total and periodic (for example quarterly) funding needed, derived from the cost baseline. Total funds = cost baseline + management reserve. |
| **Funding limit reconciliation** | Comparing planned spending with the limits on available funds. If spending would exceed a limit, **work is rescheduled** to level out the spending. |

- "Time-phased" means the budget is spread over time. Plotted as cumulative cost, it forms an **S-curve**.
- Money usually arrives in **steps** (for example each quarter), while spending rises gradually.
- The cost baseline is one of the three parts of the **performance measurement baseline**, with the scope and schedule baselines.

## Contingency reserve and management reserve

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.4.1; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Reserve analysis; PMBOK 6, 7.3.3.1; The Project Management Answer Book, Chapter 8, Q11

| | **Contingency reserve** | **Management reserve** |
|---|---|---|
| For | **Identified risks** with planned responses (**known-unknowns**) | **Unforeseen work** within the project scope (**unknown-unknowns**) |
| Part of the cost baseline? | **Yes** | **No** |
| Part of the project budget? | Yes | Yes |
| Who controls it | Usually the **project manager** | Usually **management**; the project manager needs approval to use it |
| How it is set | A percentage, a fixed amount or quantitative analysis | Set aside by management |

- Example of a known-unknown: some rework is expected, but nobody knows how much.
- As the project progresses and risks pass, contingency reserve may be **used, reduced or eliminated**.
- When management reserve is released for unforeseen work, it is **added to the cost baseline**, which requires an approved change.

## Monitor and Control Finances

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.4.2.4; PMBOK 8, Guide Figure 2-30

Monitors the project's financial status, updates the finances and **manages changes to the cost baseline**. It runs throughout the project.

| Inputs | Tools and techniques | Outputs |
|---|---|---|
| Financial management plan, cost baseline, project funding requirements, **work performance data** | **Earned value analysis**, trend analysis, reserve analysis, to-complete performance index | **Work performance information**, revenue and **cost forecasts**, **change requests** |

- **Variance analysis** compares actual costs with planned costs to find and address deviations.
- Knowing only what was spent is not enough. Spending must be compared with the **work actually completed**, which is what earned value does (next module).
- The process is also about acting **early** on the causes of overruns, not just reporting them.
- A change to the cost baseline goes through **Assess and Implement Changes**.

## Measures used to judge a project's value

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.4.1; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Cost-benefit analysis; PMBOK 6, 1.2.6.4; PMI Guide to Business Analysis, Glossary (Payback Period); PMI Guide to Business Analysis, 4.4.2.7

These measures are used in the business case and when selecting projects.

| Measure | Meaning | Better when |
|---|---|---|
| **Return on investment (ROI)** | Gain compared with the money invested | Higher |
| **Net present value (NPV)** | Value today of future cash inflows minus outflows | Higher (positive means worthwhile) |
| **Internal rate of return (IRR)** | The interest rate the investment is expected to earn | Higher |
| **Payback period** | Time needed to earn back the investment | **Shorter** |
| **Cost-benefit analysis** | Compares the benefits of an option against its costs | Benefits exceed costs |

You are not asked to calculate NPV or IRR. You are asked to **pick the better project**: for payback period choose the **smaller** number, for the others the **larger**.

## Finance in adaptive projects

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.4.1; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.4.3

- Budgets may be allocated for a short period, such as a **quarter**, and revised based on the work achieved.
- Spending is **flatter** over time, because a stable team works in regular iterations.
- Detailed estimates are made only for the **near-term** work, since later scope may change.
- Contracts should allow the budget to follow the iterative progress of the project.

## Exam tips

- "Which process produces the cost baseline?" **Develop Budget** (older name: Determine Budget), not Estimate Costs.
- Cost baseline **includes contingency** reserve and **excludes management** reserve.
- "Known risk" or "identified risk" means **contingency reserve**. "Unforeseen" or "unknown-unknown" means **management reserve**.
- Early, rough estimate: **ROM, −25% to +75%**. Detailed estimate: **definitive, −5% to +10%**.
- Training and process documentation are **prevention** costs. Testing and inspection are **appraisal** costs. Rework and scrap are **internal failure**. Warranty claims are **external failure**.
- "Money already spent" is a **sunk cost** and should be ignored in the decision.
- Planned spending exceeds the funds available this quarter: **funding limit reconciliation**, then reschedule work.
- The financial management plan says **how** costs are managed. It has no numbers.
