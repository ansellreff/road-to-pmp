## Key points

- The **Resources** domain covers **people** (the project team) and **physical or virtual resources** (equipment, materials, software, facilities).
- Five processes: **Plan Resource Management**, **Estimate Resources**, **Acquire Resources**, **Lead the Team**, **Monitor and Control Resourcing**.
- **Lead the Team** is about **people**. **Monitor and Control Resourcing** is about **physical and virtual** resources.
- In a **RACI** chart, exactly **one person is accountable** for each task.
- The **Tuckman ladder**: forming, storming, norming, performing, adjourning.
- Five ways to resolve conflict. **Collaborate/problem-solve** gives a **win-win** and is usually the best answer.
- **Herzberg:** hygiene factors (such as salary) only prevent dissatisfaction; **motivational factors** (achievement, growth) create satisfaction.

## What the Resources domain covers

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.6; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.6.1

The domain is about how well the project plans for and uses its resources, so that the **right resources are available at the right time and place**.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Human resources (team members)** | People with assigned roles and responsibilities working toward the shared project goal |
| **Physical, material or virtual resources** | Anything that is not a person: equipment, materials, supplies, facilities, software, licenses, test environments |
| **Project manager** | Leads the team and is responsible for forming it and making it work as an effective group: acquiring, managing, motivating and empowering |
| **Resource manager** | A person with management authority over one or more resources, such as a functional manager |

- The project manager often does **not** control who is assigned. In a matrix organization, people are obtained from **functional or resource managers**, which takes **negotiation**.
- **Internal** resources are assigned by functional or resource managers. **External** resources come through **procurement**.
- Involving team members in planning and decisions adds expertise and strengthens their **commitment**.

## The five Resources processes

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.6.2; PMBOK 8, Guide Figure 2-40; Process Groups: A Practice Guide, Table 1-4; PMBOK 6, 1.2.4.6

| Focus Area | Process | Main output | Older name you may see |
|---|---|---|---|
| Planning | **Plan Resource Management** | Resource management plan, team charter | Plan Resource Management |
| Planning | **Estimate Resources** | Resource requirements, basis of estimates, resource breakdown structure | Estimate Activity Resources |
| Executing | **Acquire Resources** | Project team assignments, physical resource assignments, resource calendars | Acquire Resources |
| Executing | **Lead the Team** | Team performance assessments, change requests | Develop Team, Manage Team |
| Monitoring and Controlling | **Monitor and Control Resourcing** | Work performance information, change requests | Control Resources |

## Plan Resource Management

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.6.2.1; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Resource management plan; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Team charter

Defines **how** to estimate, acquire, lead and use team and physical resources. Good planning considers the availability of, and competition for, **scarce** resources.

| Output | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Resource management plan** | The component of the project management plan that describes how resources are acquired, allocated, monitored and controlled. It covers roles and responsibilities, how the team will be developed, and how people will be recognized and released. |
| **Team charter** | A document that sets the team's **values, agreements and operating guidelines**: communication guidelines, decision-making and conflict resolution processes, meeting guidelines. It works best when **the team writes it**. |

**Ground rules** are the expectations for acceptable behavior, set in the team charter. All team members share responsibility for following them.

## Showing who does what: the RACI chart

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Responsibility assignment matrix; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Hierarchical charts

| Tool | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **Responsibility assignment matrix (RAM)** | A grid of work packages or activities against people, showing who is assigned to what |
| **RACI chart** | A common type of RAM: **R**esponsible, **A**ccountable, **C**onsulted, **I**nformed |
| **Organizational breakdown structure (OBS)** | Arranged by the organization's departments, with the work listed under each |
| **Resource breakdown structure (RBS)** | A hierarchy of resources by **category and type**: labor, materials, equipment, supplies |

Example of a RACI chart:

| Activity | Ana | Ben | Carla | Dev |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Write the test plan | **A** | R | C | I |
| Run the tests | I | **A** | R | R |
| Sign off the results | **A** | C | I | R |

- **Responsible** does the work. **Accountable** answers for it. **Consulted** gives input (two-way). **Informed** is told the result (one-way).
- **Only one person is accountable** for each task, so there is no confusion about who is in charge.
- A RACI chart is especially useful when the team mixes internal and external people.

## Estimate Resources and Acquire Resources

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.6.2.2; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.6.2.3; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Resource calendar

**Estimate Resources** works out the **type, quantity and characteristics** of the resources needed. It is closely tied to scheduling, because the number of people affects duration.

- Outputs: **resource requirements**, **basis of estimates** and the **resource breakdown structure**.
- A **resource calendar** shows the working days and shifts when each specific resource is **available**.

**Acquire Resources** obtains the team members and physical resources. It is performed **periodically** as needed.

| Technique | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Negotiation** | With functional managers, other project teams or suppliers, for the people and resources needed |
| **Preassignment** | Resources decided **in advance**, for example named in a proposal or in the charter |
| **Virtual teams** | People with a shared goal who work with little or no face-to-face time. Widens the choice of people and reduces travel cost, but makes **communication planning** more important. |
| **Multicriteria decision analysis** | Scoring candidates against criteria such as availability, cost, experience, skills and attitude |

- If the needed resources cannot be obtained, schedule, budget, quality and risk are all affected. The project manager may have to use **alternative resources**, as long as legal and mandatory criteria are not violated.
- Acquiring from **inside** the organization takes negotiation and influence; from **outside**, procurement.

## Lead the Team

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.6.2.4; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Team performance assessments

Guides, develops and manages the team: improving competencies, interaction and the team environment, tracking performance, giving feedback, and resolving issues.

| | Focus | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Management activities** | The **means** of meeting objectives | Processes, planning, coordinating, measuring, monitoring |
| **Leadership activities** | **People** | Influencing, motivating, listening, enabling |

Techniques for developing the team:

| Technique | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Colocation** | Placing the most active team members in the **same physical location** to improve teamwork |
| **Team building** | Activities that improve the team's social relations and build a cooperative environment |
| **Training** | Activities that improve the competencies of team members |
| **Coaching and mentoring** | Guiding people to develop their skills and reach their goals |
| **Recognition and rewards** | Recognizing desirable behavior **throughout** the project, not only at the end. Intangible rewards can work as well as money. |
| **Individual and team assessments** | Tools that give insight into strengths and weaknesses, such as attitude surveys |

The output **team performance assessments** judges how effective the **team as a whole** is: better skills, better teamwork, lower staff turnover, more cohesiveness.

**High-performing teams** show open communication, shared understanding, shared ownership, **trust**, collaboration, adaptability, resilience, **empowerment** and recognition. People who are empowered perform better than people who are **micromanaged**.

## The Tuckman ladder

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Tuckman ladder; PMBOK 6, 9.4

| Stage | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Forming** | Members meet and learn about the project and their roles. They are independent and not yet open. |
| **Storming** | The team starts on the work and the decisions. **Conflict** appears as ideas and working styles clash. |
| **Norming** | Members begin to work together, adjust their habits and learn to **trust** one another. |
| **Performing** | The team works as a well-organized, **interdependent** unit and handles issues smoothly. |
| **Adjourning** | The work is complete and the team is released. |

- The stages usually happen in order, but a team can get **stuck**, **go back** to an earlier stage, or **skip** one if the members have worked together before.
- A **new member joining** usually sends the team back toward forming.

## Resolving conflict

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Conflict management

**Conflict is inevitable** on projects. Common sources are **scarce resources, scheduling priorities and personal work styles**. Managed well, it can improve creativity and decisions.

- Team members are **first responsible** for resolving their own conflicts. If it escalates, the project manager helps.
- Address conflict **early**, usually **in private**, with a **direct, collaborative** approach.
- Focus on the **issue, not the person**, and on the present and future, not the past.

| Technique | What it is | Typical result |
|---|---|---|
| **Collaborate / problem-solve** | Bringing in several viewpoints through open dialogue to reach consensus and commitment | **Win-win** |
| **Compromise / reconcile** | Each side gives something up; everyone is partly satisfied | Partial or temporary; sometimes lose-lose |
| **Smooth / accommodate** | Stressing areas of agreement; giving way to keep harmony | Temporary |
| **Force / direct** | Pushing one viewpoint at the expense of others, usually from a position of power | **Win-lose**; suited to emergencies |
| **Withdraw / avoid** | Retreating from the conflict or postponing it | Not resolved |

## What motivates people

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Motivation; PMBOK 6, 9.4.2.5; The Project Management Answer Book, Chapter 12, Q26

| Model | Main idea |
|---|---|
| **Herzberg: hygiene and motivational factors** | **Hygiene factors** (salary, company policies, physical environment) cause dissatisfaction if missing, but do **not** motivate when present. **Motivational factors** (achievement, growth, advancement) create satisfaction. |
| **Pink: intrinsic and extrinsic motivation** | Once people are paid fairly, more money stops motivating. Lasting motivation comes from **autonomy, mastery and purpose**. |
| **McClelland: theory of needs** | People are driven by needs for **achievement, power and affiliation**, in different strengths. |
| **McGregor: Theory X and Theory Y** | **Theory X:** people work only for income and need hands-on, top-down management. **Theory Y:** people are motivated to do good work and respond to coaching. |
| **Theory Z** | Maslow: people are motivated by self-realization, values and a higher calling. Ouchi: motivation through a job for life and care for employees' well-being. |
| **Maslow's hierarchy of needs** | Needs are met from the bottom up: physiological, safety, social, esteem, self-actualization. |

## Monitor and Control Resourcing

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.6.2.5

Ensures that the **physical and virtual** resources assigned to the project are **available as planned**, compares planned and actual use, and takes corrective action. **Team members are handled in Lead the Team, not here.**

It is concerned with:

- monitoring resource spending,
- dealing with **shortages and surpluses** in time,
- making sure resources are used and **released** according to the plan,
- informing stakeholders of resource issues.

Examples of what goes wrong without it: critical equipment arrives late and delays the product; low-quality materials cause rework; too much inventory raises costs.

## Resources in adaptive projects

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.6.3

- Teams are **self-organizing**, with decentralized decision-making.
- Members are often **generalizing specialists**: a main skill plus the ability to help in other areas.
- The leader acts as a **servant leader**, removing obstacles and supporting the team, instead of directing each task.
- Teams with experience in a type of project need less oversight; a project that is new to the organization tends to get a more direct leadership style.

## Exam tips

- "Who resolves a conflict between two team members first?" **The team members themselves.** The project manager steps in if it escalates.
- "Best conflict technique?" **Collaborate / problem-solve.** Force is the answer only for an emergency.
- A team that is **arguing** is **storming**. A team beginning to **trust** is **norming**.
- Salary and working conditions are **hygiene factors**. They do not motivate; they only stop dissatisfaction.
- "Who sets the ground rules?" The **team**, in the **team charter**.
- One **accountable** person per task in a RACI chart.
- A team member is unavailable because a functional manager needs them: **negotiate** with the functional manager.
- Problems with **equipment or materials** belong to **Monitor and Control Resourcing**. Problems with **people** belong to **Lead the Team**.
- Reward the team **throughout** the project, and prefer rewards that do not set team members against each other.
