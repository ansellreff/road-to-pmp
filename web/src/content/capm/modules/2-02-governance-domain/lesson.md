## Key points

- The **Governance** domain has **nine processes** and is the only domain with processes in all five Focus Areas. It integrates all the others.
- **Initiate Project or Phase** produces the **project charter**. **Integrate and Align Project Plans** produces the **project management plan**.
- **Manage Project Execution** produces **deliverables** and **work performance data**, and implements **approved change requests**.
- **Quality assurance** is about the **process**. **Quality control** is about the **deliverables**.
- **Explicit knowledge** can be written down. **Tacit knowledge** lives in people's heads and is shared through interaction.
- Once a baseline exists, every change goes through **formal change control**, often decided by a **change control board (CCB)**. In adaptive work, changes are handled through the **backlog**.
- **Close Project or Phase** applies to every project, including cancelled ones.

## The nine Governance processes

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6; Process Groups: A Practice Guide, Table 1-4; PMBOK 6, 1.2.4.6

| Focus Area | Process | Main output | Older name you may see |
|---|---|---|---|
| Initiating | **Initiate Project or Phase** | Project charter, assumption log | Develop Project Charter |
| Planning | **Integrate and Align Project Plans** | Project management plan | Develop Project Management Plan |
| Planning | **Plan Sourcing Strategy** | Sourcing strategy plan | Plan Procurement Management |
| Executing | **Manage Project Execution** | Deliverables, work performance data, issue log, change requests | Direct and Manage Project Work |
| Executing | **Manage Quality Assurance** | Quality reports | Manage Quality |
| Executing | **Manage Project Knowledge** | Lessons learned register | Manage Project Knowledge |
| Monitoring and Controlling | **Monitor and Control Project Performance** | Work performance reports, change requests | Monitor and Control Project Work |
| Monitoring and Controlling | **Assess and Implement Changes** | Approved change requests, change log updates | Perform Integrated Change Control |
| Closing | **Close Project or Phase** | Final product transition, final report | Close Project or Phase |

Governance is not a one-time activity. It runs from initiation to closure, and it includes the **authority to cancel a project** when necessary.

> **On the exam.** The exam's references call most of this the **Integration** knowledge area, with seven processes: **Develop Project Charter** (Initiating); **Develop Project Management Plan** (Planning); **Direct and Manage Project Work** and **Manage Project Knowledge** (Executing); **Monitor and Control Project Work** and **Perform Integrated Change Control** (Monitoring and Controlling); **Close Project or Phase** (Closing). An "integration management plan" in the exam outline means the **project management plan**, which integrates all the subsidiary plans. Module 1.12 has the full table of 49 processes.

## Initiate Project or Phase

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.1

Officially **authorizes the start** of a project and gives the project manager **authority to allocate organizational resources**, by creating a **project charter** or similar document.

| Inputs | Tools and techniques | Outputs |
|---|---|---|
| Business case, benefits management plan, agreements, EEFs, OPAs | Expert judgment, brainstorming, focus groups, interviews, facilitation, meetings | **Project charter**, **assumption log** |

- The charter links the project to the **business case** and the organization's **strategic goals**. It is the official record that the organization is committed.
- It forms a partnership between the **performing** and **requesting** organizations.
- For work between two companies, a **contract** sets the agreement between them, and each uses a charter internally.
- In **adaptive** projects the charter can be much lighter: a worksheet or a short vision statement answering why, who, what, where, when, how, and who the customer is.
- The process is done once, or at set points such as the start of each phase.

## Integrate and Align Project Plans

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.2

Consolidates all the plan components from the other domains into **one unified project management plan**, and checks that they agree with each other.

| Inputs | Tools and techniques | Outputs |
|---|---|---|
| Project charter, outputs from other processes, EEFs, OPAs | Expert judgment, brainstorming, checklists, interviews, facilitation, meetings | **Project management plan** |

- At the start, this process sets the **tailoring**, the **development approach** and the **life cycle**. These guide planning in every other domain.
- Then each domain's management plan and baseline is integrated, along with the **change management plan** and **configuration management plan**.
- The plan can be high-level or detailed, and it is refined by **progressive elaboration**.
- Plan enough to move forward, **but not more detail than necessary**.

## Plan Sourcing Strategy

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.3; PMBOK 8, Guide Table 2-3

Decides whether work is done with **internal or external resources**, and documents how outside sources will be selected and contracted. It defines **what to acquire, how and when**.

Key tools: **make-or-buy analysis**, market research, source selection analysis. Output: the **sourcing strategy plan** with insourcing or outsourcing decisions and **source selection criteria** (cost, technical capability, past performance, financial stability, compliance, alignment with values).

| Advantages of insourcing (make) | Advantages of outsourcing (buy) |
|---|---|
| Uses internal expertise when available | Brings in expertise that is missing internally |
| Often cheaper when substantial new innovation is needed | Often cheaper when the goods or services are common |
| Tighter integration with strategy | Frees capacity and capital for competitive strengths |
| More control and oversight of deliverables | **Transfers delivery risk** to the supplier |

The purchasing department and the legal department often take part. Procurement is covered further in module 2.11.

## Manage Project Execution

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.4

**Leading and performing the work** defined in the project management plan, and **implementing approved changes**.

| Inputs | Tools and techniques | Outputs |
|---|---|---|
| Project management plan, project documents, **approved change requests**, EEFs, OPAs | Expert judgment, **PMIS**, meetings | **Deliverables**, **work performance data**, **issue log**, change requests, plan and document updates |

- Includes allocating resources, managing their use, and adjusting plans based on work performance data.
- Approved changes implemented here include **corrective actions, preventive actions and defect remediation**.
- Work performance data collected here is passed to the controlling processes for analysis.
- Quality during execution has two sides: **process quality** (quality assurance) and **deliverable quality** (handled through scope management and control).

## Manage Quality Assurance

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.5

Ensuring that **project processes** are performed in a way consistent with stakeholder expectations. It turns the quality management plan into executable activities that apply the organization's standards and policies.

| | Quality assurance | Quality control |
|---|---|---|
| Focus | The **process** | The **deliverables** |
| Question | Are we working the right way? | Does the result meet the specification? |
| Related concepts | Standards, regulations, compliance, **audits** | Product design, **testing**, **defects** |
| 8th edition home | Governance domain | Scope domain |

Tools: **audits**, checklists, affinity diagrams, cause-and-effect diagrams, flowcharts, problem-solving, process improvement. Output: **quality reports**.

Benefits: builds confidence that future outputs will meet requirements, improves process efficiency, and identifies ineffective processes and causes of poor performance. Quality assurance is usually **formal** in predictive projects and may be informal in adaptive ones. It can be performed by the project management team, an external entity, or both.

## Manage Project Knowledge

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.6

**Using existing knowledge and creating new knowledge** to achieve the project's objectives and contribute to organizational learning.

| | Explicit knowledge | Tacit knowledge |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Formal and systematic; can be **codified** in words, pictures or numbers | Personal, **embedded in a person's mind**; experience, insight, know-how |
| Examples | Manuals, procedures, documented processes | An expert's intuition about when a design will fail |
| Shared through | Databases, registers, documents | **Conversation and interaction**: mentoring, team building, face-to-face work, storytelling |
| Weakness | Lacks context, can be misread | Hard to articulate and transfer |

- An important objective is to **convert tacit knowledge into explicit knowledge** where possible.
- Knowledge management is **more than documenting lessons at the end**. Lessons should be captured throughout the project.
- Key output: the **lessons learned register**.
- AI can help capture and share knowledge, but brings risks such as exposing sensitive information and **AI hallucinations**. Projects should have responsible AI policies.

## Monitor and Control Project Performance

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.7

**Tracking, reviewing and reporting** overall progress against the performance objectives in the project management plan.

| Inputs | Tools and techniques | Outputs |
|---|---|---|
| Project management plan, project documents, **work performance information**, agreements | Data analysis (earned value, root cause, trend and variance analysis), decision-making, dashboards, information radiators | **Work performance reports**, change requests, updated cost and schedule forecasts |

- **Monitoring:** collecting, measuring and assessing data and trends.
- **Controlling:** deciding corrective or preventive actions, replanning, and following up.

It lets stakeholders understand the **current state**, see what was done about performance issues, and get **cost and schedule forecasts**. If a beneficial decision would change a baseline, it must go through integrated change control.

Measure only what is relevant and actionable. Measuring costs time and effort.

## Measurement pitfalls

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.7.1

| Pitfall | What goes wrong |
|---|---|
| **Hawthorne effect** | The act of measuring something changes behavior. Measure only output volume and the team will produce volume, not value |
| **Vanity metric** | Shows data but gives no useful information for decisions (page views instead of new viewers) |
| **Demoralization** | Unachievable targets lower morale as the team keeps failing |
| **Misusing the metrics** | Chasing less important or short-term measures, or doing easy out-of-sequence work to look good |
| **Confirmation bias** | Seeing in the data what supports your existing view |
| **Correlation versus causation** | Assuming that because two things move together, one causes the other |

## Assess and Implement Changes

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.8

Managing changes from the start of the project to its end. A change can be proposed by **any stakeholder at any time**, and can affect scope, the plan or project documents.

**Predictive approach**

- Changes are **not formally controlled until baselines are established**. After that, every change goes through the formal process.
- A change may be raised verbally, but it must be **documented in writing** and entered in the change system.
- The request should include its estimated **schedule and cost impact** before it can be approved.
- Each request is **approved, deferred or rejected** by a designated person or body, as defined in the plan.
- The **change control board (CCB)** is a formally established group that reviews, evaluates, approves, defers or rejects changes, and records and communicates the decisions.
- While waiting for the CCB, the project manager **keeps executing planned work** and analyzes the impact of the change.
- After CCB approval, **customer or sponsor approval** may still be needed, unless they sit on the CCB.
- The **change management plan** sets up the CCB and its authority. The **configuration management plan** says which artifacts are under control.
- Decisions go in the **change log**.

The usual sequence: **change request → impact analysis → decision → update plans and log → implement → monitor the result**.

**Adaptive approach**

- Changes are handled by **backlog management**, not a formal change request process.
- A proposed change becomes a **product backlog item**. Its impact is analyzed and it is given a **priority**.
- There is usually no formal approval. A **low priority** means the change is effectively deferred. **Not adding it**, or removing it, means it is rejected.

## Close Project or Phase

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.9

Finalizing all activities for a project, phase, release, iteration or contract, **whether it succeeded or not**.

| Inputs | Tools and techniques | Outputs |
|---|---|---|
| Project charter, project management plan, project documents, **accepted deliverables**, business documents, agreements, procurement documentation | Expert judgment, document analysis, trend and variance analysis, meetings | **Final product, service or result transition**, **final report**, lessons learned register updates, OPA updates |

Closing activities in a predictive project:

- confirm all deliverables are **delivered and formally accepted** by the customer
- confirm the **measurable value** was delivered and there is a plan to sustain it in operations
- resolve all issues; update documents
- confirm all costs are charged; **close project accounts**
- **close contracts:** confirm acceptance of suppliers' work, settle claims and warranties, pay final invoices
- identify and document **lessons learned**; archive project information
- **transfer** the product to the next phase or to operations
- measure **stakeholder satisfaction**
- **release** people, facilities, equipment and leftover materials
- write the **final report**

If a project is **terminated early**, closing still applies, and includes investigating and documenting the reasons.

In **adaptive** work, closing wraps up each release or iteration: confirm backlog items meet their acceptance criteria, get stakeholder acceptance, hold a **retrospective**, and share knowledge.

## Tailoring governance

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.7

Governance should fit the project's context. Agile projects may replace traditional governance with **guided self-governance**: light governance with clear boundaries, where the team decides how it works. The daily coordination meeting is itself a self-governing mechanism. Predictive projects may need heavier governance because of internal and external constraints. The aim in every case is **"just enough"** process to achieve the outcomes.

## Exam tips

- "What is the output of initiating?" The **project charter** (and the assumption log).
- "Where are approved changes implemented?" **Manage Project Execution** (older name: Direct and Manage Project Work).
- A customer asks a team member directly for a small extra feature. The answer is never "just do it". **Submit a change request** and assess the impact.
- When a change is requested: **assess the impact first**, then take it to the change control board. Do not go to the sponsor or reject it before analyzing.
- **Audit** points to quality **assurance**. **Inspection, testing, defect** point to quality **control**.
- Lessons learned are captured **throughout** the project, not only at closing.
- Before closing, deliverables must be **accepted**. Releasing the team comes near the **end** of closing, after lessons learned and archiving.
- A cancelled project still goes through **Close Project or Phase**.
