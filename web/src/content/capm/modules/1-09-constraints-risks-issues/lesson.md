## Key points

- An **assumption** is something treated as true without proof. A **constraint** is a limiting factor.
- A **risk** is an *uncertain future* event that would affect objectives if it happens. An **issue** is a *current* condition that is already affecting them.
- Risks can be negative (**threats**) or positive (**opportunities**).
- Assumptions and constraints go in the **assumption log**, risks in the **risk register**, issues in the **issue log**.
- **Risk appetite** is how much uncertainty the organization will accept for a reward. A **risk threshold** puts a number on it.
- **Contingency reserve** covers known risks and is inside the baseline. **Management reserve** covers unforeseen work and sits outside it.

## The four terms side by side

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.7.1

| | Assumption | Constraint | Risk | Issue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| What it is | A factor considered true, real or certain without proof | A limiting factor that affects execution | An uncertain event or condition that, if it occurs, affects objectives | A current condition or situation that may affect objectives |
| Certain? | Believed, not proven | Yes, it is a fact | No, it may or may not happen | Yes, it has happened or is happening |
| Time | Now, about the future | Now | Future | Present |
| Recorded in | Assumption log | Assumption log | Risk register | Issue log |
| What you do | Validate it | Work within it | Plan a response | Resolve it |
| Example | "The supplier will deliver in six weeks" | "The budget cannot exceed $2 million" | "The supplier might deliver late" | "The supplier has delivered late" |

The four are linked. **An assumption that turns out false becomes a risk or an issue.** A constraint can create risks. **A risk that occurs becomes an issue.** An issue may arise from a poorly managed risk.

## Assumptions

@source PMBOK 8, Glossary (assumption)

An **assumption** is a factor in the planning process considered to be true, real or certain **without proof or demonstration**. Planning is impossible without them: you assume staff will be available, prices will hold, a permit will be granted.

Because they are unproven, every assumption carries risk. Good practice is to write them down and check them as the project proceeds.

## Constraints

@source PMBOK 8, Glossary (constraint)

A **constraint** is a **limiting factor** that affects the execution of a portfolio, program, project or process. Examples: a fixed deadline, a budget cap, a required technology, a regulation, limited staff.

Projects must balance **competing constraints**. The classic set is **scope, schedule and cost**, often called the triple constraint, with **quality, resources and risk** commonly added. Changing one usually affects at least one other: add scope, and time or cost must give.

> In an adaptive project the usual choice is to fix time and cost and let scope vary. In a predictive project scope is fixed and time or cost absorb the change. See module 1.6.

## The assumption log

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Assumption log

The **assumption log** is a project document that records **all assumptions and constraints** throughout the project. Entries are added, updated or closed as the project goes on.

- **High-level** (strategic and operational) constraints are normally identified in the **business case** before the project starts, and flow into the **project charter**.
- **Lower-level** activity and task assumptions arise throughout the project, for example when defining specifications, estimates and schedule activities.

## Risk

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.7.1; PMBOK 7, Guide 2.8

A **risk** is an uncertain event or condition that, if it occurs, has a **positive or negative** effect on one or more objectives.

| | Threat | Opportunity |
|---|---|---|
| Effect | Negative | Positive |
| Examples | Delays, cost overruns, reputation damage | Cost savings, more market share, positive environmental impact |
| Goal | Reduce probability and impact | Increase probability and impact |

A risk can be written in a **cause, event, consequence** structure: "Because the design is new (cause), the prototype may fail testing (event), which would delay launch by a month (consequence)."

**Individual vs overall risk.** *Individual* risks are specific events. **Overall risk** is the effect of uncertainty on the project as a whole. If overall risk is too high, the organization may cancel the project.

The **risk register** is the repository where the outputs of the risk processes are recorded: identified risks, their analysis, the planned responses and their status. It is updated regularly throughout the project.

> **On the exam.** The 7th edition covers risk in its **Uncertainty performance domain**, together with ambiguity, complexity and volatility. The exam's process reference has seven risk processes (module 1.12).

## Risk classification

@source PMBOK 8, Guide Figure 2-46

| | What it means | Label |
|---|---|---|
| **Known-known** | Facts and requirements. Managed as part of scope. **Not a risk** | Facts |
| **Known-unknown** | You know it could happen and can estimate probability and impact | Classic risk |
| **Unknown-known** | The knowledge exists somewhere, but not with the team | Hidden fact |
| **Unknown-unknown** | The knowledge does not exist within reach. Cannot be foreseen | Emergent risk |

## Issue

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.7.1; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Issue log

An **issue** is a current condition or situation that may have an impact on one or more objectives. It **has already occurred** and may need immediate action or management attention.

The **issue log** is the project document where issues are recorded and monitored, usually with an owner and a target resolution date.

**Risk or issue?** Ask one question: *has it happened?*

- "The lead developer might resign" is a **risk**. Plan a response.
- "The lead developer resigned this morning" is an **issue**. Act now.

## Risk appetite, threshold and exposure

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.7.1; PMBOK 8, Glossary (risk appetite)

- **Risk appetite:** the degree of uncertainty an organization or individual is willing to accept in anticipation of a reward.
- **Risk threshold:** the measure of acceptable variation around an objective, reflecting the risk appetite. A threshold of ±5% around a cost objective shows a *lower* appetite than ±10%.
- **Risk exposure:** an aggregate measure of the potential impact of all risks at a given point in time.

## Risk responses

@source PMBOK 8, Glossary (risk acceptance)

A **risk response** is an action, planned or implemented, to address a threat or opportunity.

| For threats | Meaning | For opportunities | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Avoid** | Eliminate the threat or protect the project from its impact | **Exploit** | Act to ensure the opportunity occurs |
| **Mitigate** | Reduce the probability or impact | **Enhance** | Increase the probability or impact |
| **Transfer** | Shift the impact and the ownership of the response to a third party (insurance, a contract) | **Share** | Give ownership to a third party best able to capture it |
| **Escalate** | Pass ownership upward when the risk is outside project scope or the team's authority | **Escalate** | Same |
| **Accept** | Acknowledge the risk and take no action unless it occurs | **Accept** | Same |

## Reserves

@source PMBOK 8, Glossary (contingency reserve); PMBOK 8, Glossary (management reserve)

A **reserve** is a provision in the plan to deal with cost or schedule risk.

| | Contingency reserve | Management reserve |
|---|---|---|
| For | **Known** risks with active response strategies | **Unforeseen** work that is within project scope |
| Sometimes described as | Known-unknowns | Unknown-unknowns |
| Where it sits | **Inside** the schedule or cost baseline | **In addition to** the baseline |
| Controlled by | Project manager | Management |

## Baseline

@source PMBOK 8, Glossary (baseline)

A **baseline** is the approved version of a work product. It can be changed only through **formal change control**, and it is used as the basis for comparison with actual results. Constraints are often expressed as baselines: the scope, schedule and cost baselines.

## Resilience, uncertainty and related terms

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.7.1

- **Uncertainty:** a lack of understanding and awareness of issues, events, the path to follow or the solutions to pursue.
- **Ambiguity:** a state of being unclear, where the cause of events is hard to identify or there are many options to choose from.
- **Complexity:** a characteristic of a project or its environment that is difficult to manage because of human behavior, system behavior and ambiguity.
- **Volatility:** the possibility of rapid and unpredictable change.
- **Project resilience:** the ability to absorb impacts and recover quickly from setbacks. It is how a project prepares for emergent risks and rare, high-impact **black swan** events.

Ambiguous and uncertain situations do not always become risks. Often they are resolved as information arrives and experts get involved.

## Exam tips

- The fastest way to answer most questions here: **future and uncertain = risk; present and real = issue; believed true = assumption; limit = constraint.**
- **Risk is not only negative.** If an option says risks should be "eliminated" as a general rule, it ignores opportunities.
- A risk that happens is an issue. Record it in the **issue log** and carry out the planned response.
- A fixed deadline imposed by the customer is a **constraint**. "We assume the customer will approve designs within three days" is an **assumption**.
- Insurance and fixed-price contracts are **transfer**. Doing extra testing to reduce the chance of defects is **mitigate**. Changing the plan to remove the cause is **avoid**.
- Contingency reserve is **in** the baseline; management reserve is **not**.
