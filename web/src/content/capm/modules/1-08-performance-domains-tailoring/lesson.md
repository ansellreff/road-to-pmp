## Key points

- A **performance domain** is a group of related processes that are critical for the effective delivery of project value.
- There are **seven**: Governance, Scope, Schedule, Finance, Stakeholders, Resources and Risk. They have no weighting or order, run **concurrently** through the whole project, and work as one **integrated system**.
- The 8th edition describes **40 processes** inside the domains. They are illustrative, not a mandatory method.
- **Tailoring** is the deliberate adaptation of the approach, governance and processes to fit the project's environment and objectives.
- Tailoring has **four steps**: select the initial development approach, tailor for the organization, tailor for the project, implement ongoing improvement.

## What a performance domain is

Performance domains are **interactive, interrelated and interdependent** areas of focus that work together to achieve the desired results. They are not handled as separate silos. A project leader pays attention to stakeholders, schedule, risk and the rest from the first day to the last, however and whenever value is delivered.

**Principles and domains.** Principles guide mindset and behavior. Performance domains are the broad areas of focus in which that mindset and behavior are shown. The principles are the foundation; the domains hold the processes.

Each domain is presented the same way in the Guide:

| Part | What it contains |
|---|---|
| Key concepts | The most important ideas for that domain |
| Processes | The domain's processes, each with sample inputs, tools and techniques, and outputs |
| Tailoring considerations | What to adjust for the project's context |
| Interactions with other domains | Overlaps and dependencies |
| Check results | Target outcomes and how to verify them |

**Check results** carries an important message: project management activities add no value by themselves. A schedule is not valuable because it exists. It is valuable because it aligns stakeholders around when value will be delivered. Every output should serve a higher-order outcome.

## The seven domains

| Domain | What it is about |
|---|---|
| **Governance** | The framework, functions and processes that guide decisions and activities to optimize value delivery. It integrates everything else: strategic alignment, decision-making, change, quality assurance, knowledge and success criteria |
| **Scope** | Defining and controlling what the project will and will not deliver. Includes requirements and **quality** |
| **Schedule** | Planning and controlling when the work is done |
| **Finance** | Estimating costs, building the budget and controlling money |
| **Stakeholders** | Identifying and engaging the people affected, and managing communications |
| **Resources** | Planning, acquiring and managing people and physical resources, and leading the team |
| **Risk** | Anticipating, preparing for and responding to uncertainty, both threats and opportunities |

Governance can be **lightweight** for adaptive work, **moderate** for hybrid, and **comprehensive** for large predictive projects.

## The 40 processes

The Guide presents 40 processes to describe the mechanics of the domains. Each lists a **sampling** of inputs, tools and techniques, and outputs (ITTOs). They are **illustrative, not comprehensive, and not prescriptive**: a technical description, not a required methodology.

This table maps the processes to the five Focus Areas. You do not need to memorize it yet. Domain 2 of this course works through the processes one domain at a time.

| Domain | Initiating | Planning | Executing | Monitoring and Controlling | Closing |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Governance** | Initiate Project or Phase | Integrate and Align Project Plans; Plan Sourcing Strategy | Manage Project Execution; Manage Quality Assurance; Manage Project Knowledge | Monitor and Control Project Performance; Assess and Implement Changes | Close Project or Phase |
| **Scope** | | Plan Scope Management; Elicit and Analyze Requirements; Define Scope; Develop Scope Structure | | Monitor and Control Scope; Validate Scope | |
| **Schedule** | | Plan Schedule Management; Develop Schedule | | Monitor and Control Schedule | |
| **Finance** | | Plan Financial Management; Estimate Costs; Develop Budget | | Monitor and Control Finances | |
| **Stakeholders** | Identify Stakeholders | Plan Stakeholder Engagement; Plan Communications Management | Manage Stakeholder Engagement; Manage Communications | Monitor Stakeholder Engagement; Monitor Communications | |
| **Resources** | | Plan Resource Management; Estimate Resources | Acquire Resources; Lead the Team | Monitor and Control Resourcing | |
| **Risk** | | Plan Risk Management; Identify Risks; Perform Risk Analysis; Plan Risk Responses | Implement Risk Responses | Monitor Risks | |

Patterns worth noticing:

- Only **two** processes are in Initiating: *Initiate Project or Phase* and *Identify Stakeholders*.
- Only **one** process is in Closing: *Close Project or Phase*.
- **Planning** has the most processes.
- Only **Governance** has processes in all five Focus Areas.

> **Edition note.** The 6th edition and the *Process Groups* practice guide have **49 processes** in **10 knowledge areas** (integration, scope, schedule, cost, quality, resource, communications, risk, procurement, stakeholder). The 7th edition has **8 performance domains** (stakeholders, team, development approach and life cycle, planning, project work, delivery, measurement, uncertainty) and no processes. The 8th edition combines both ideas: 7 domains containing 40 processes. Exam-prep courses still commonly teach the 49. Module 1.11 compares them.

## Tailoring

**Tailoring** is the deliberate adaptation of the project management approach, governance and processes to make them suitable for the environment and fit for the work at hand. It is driven by the principles, and by organizational values and culture.

**Why tailor?** No single approach fits all projects. Too few processes makes management ineffective; too many is costly and wasteful. A nuclear reactor project needs far more rigor than an office building, and a team of 200 needs far more coordination than a team of 10. Projects must balance competing demands: speed, cost, value, quality, compliance and sustainability, stakeholder expectations, and adapting to change.

Benefits of tailoring:

- more **commitment** from the team members who helped tailor the approach
- a stronger **customer focus**
- more **efficient use of resources**

Limits: organizational policy or a contract may **mandate** a particular approach.

## What can be tailored

**1. Life cycle and development approach.** Choosing the phases and choosing predictive, adaptive or hybrid.

**2. Processes.** Each element can be:

| Action | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Added** | Address unique conditions or increase rigor |
| **Modified** | Fit the project or team better |
| **Removed** | Cut unnecessary cost or effort |
| **Blended** | Combine elements for added value |
| **Aligned** | Keep definitions and application consistent |

**3. Engagement.** *People* (skills and capabilities), *empowerment* (which decisions are left to the team) and *integration* (making one team from staff, contractors and partners).

## The four-step tailoring process

1. **Select the initial development approach.** Use knowledge of the product and the delivery cadence. A **suitability filter** helps: a decision-making tool, not a rigid procedure, that evaluates culture, team and project factors to see whether predictive, adaptive or hybrid fits best.
2. **Tailor for the organization.** Add, remove and reconfigure elements to fit organizational requirements and policy.
3. **Tailor for the project.** Adjust for size, criticality and other factors, looking at three attributes: the **product or deliverable**, the **project team** and the **culture**.
4. **Implement ongoing improvement.** Inspect and adapt. Tailoring is **not a one-time exercise**. Review points, phase gates and retrospectives are opportunities to adjust.

**Who approves?** Tailoring that affects only the project team usually needs just the **project manager's** approval. Tailoring that affects **external groups** may need the **PMO's** approval. The PMO can also help by sharing solutions from other projects.

Questions to ask when tailoring for the project:

| Attribute | Examples |
|---|---|
| Product or deliverable | Compliance needs, type of product, market, technology stability, timeframe, stability of requirements, security, possibility of incremental delivery |
| Project team | Size, geography, experience, access to the customer, diversity |
| Culture | Buy-in, trust, empowerment, organizational culture |

## Diagnostics: when to tailor further

Retrospectives and lessons learned show where the current way of working needs adjusting.

| Situation | Tailoring suggestion |
|---|---|
| Deliverables are of poor quality | Find the root causes; improve the process instead of only adding verification steps |
| Team members are unsure how to proceed | Add guidance, mentoring, training, knowledge sharing |
| Team members work in silos | Team building; individual meetings to find the cause |
| Long delays waiting for approvals | Authorize people to decide up to set value thresholds |
| Too much work in progress or waste | Value stream mapping, kanban boards |
| Stakeholders disengaged or negative | Check that communication is timely and relevant; adapt the methods |
| Progress is not transparent | Collect, share and discuss the right data; agree the measures |
| Unexpected issues and risks keep appearing | Look for gaps in project processes |

## Exam tips

- Performance domains run **concurrently**, not in sequence. Any answer that orders them is wrong.
- "Adapting the approach to the project's context" is always **tailoring**. It is expected, not a deviation.
- The team **should be involved** in tailoring. Their involvement increases commitment.
- If quality is poor, PMI's answer is to fix the **process** (root cause), not to add more inspection.
- A question that says "the methodology requires a document that adds no value for this small project" is asking you to tailor: **remove** it, with appropriate approval.
- Know which edition a question is using: 7 domains and 40 processes (8th), 8 domains (7th), or 49 processes and 10 knowledge areas (6th and the Process Groups guide).
