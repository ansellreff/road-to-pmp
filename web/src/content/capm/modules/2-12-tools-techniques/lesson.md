## Key points

- The same tools and techniques appear in many processes. Learn them **once, by group**, instead of process by process.
- The groups: **expert judgment**, **data gathering**, **data analysis**, **data representation**, **decision-making**, **interpersonal and team skills**, **meetings**, and the **project management information system (PMIS)**.
- **Data gathering** collects information. **Data analysis** makes sense of it. **Data representation** shows it.
- Voting: **unanimity** (everyone), **majority** (more than 50%), **plurality** (largest group).
- **Variance analysis** compares actual with planned. **Trend analysis** forecasts from past results. **Root cause analysis** finds the underlying reason.
- In a question, pick the tool that **fits the purpose**: what is the person trying to find out or decide?

## Why this module groups the tools

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 5

The 8th edition lists its tools and techniques **alphabetically** in Section 5, and each process figure shows a sample of them. Many repeat: **expert judgment**, **meetings** and **data analysis** appear in most processes.

Exam questions rarely ask "which tools belong to process X". They describe a situation and ask **which tool fits**. So this module sorts the common tools by what they are **for**. Tools tied to one topic (critical path, earned value, risk responses, contract types) are in their own modules.

| Group | Purpose | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Expert judgment** | Use someone's specialized knowledge | Consulting a specialist, the PMO, a consultant |
| **Data gathering** | **Collect** information and ideas | Brainstorming, interviews, focus groups, surveys, benchmarking, checklists |
| **Data analysis** | **Understand** the information | Alternative, root cause, variance, trend, what-if, cost-benefit analysis |
| **Data representation** | **Show** the information | Charts, matrices, diagrams, mind maps |
| **Decision-making** | **Choose** between options | Voting, multicriteria decision analysis, prioritization |
| **Interpersonal and team skills** | Work with **people** | Facilitation, conflict management, negotiation, active listening |
| **Meetings** | Bring people together to share, decide or plan | Kickoff, status, daily coordination, retrospective |
| **PMIS** | The **software and systems** that support the work | Scheduling tools, configuration management, reporting |

## Expert judgment and the PMIS

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Expert judgment; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Project management information system (PMIS)

**Expert judgment** is judgment based on expertise in an application area, discipline or industry. It can come from **any person or group** with specialized education, knowledge, skill, experience or training: team members, other departments, consultants, the PMO, customers, professional associations.

- It is the most frequently listed tool, because almost every process benefits from experience.
- The project manager does not have to be the expert. Knowing **whom to ask** is the skill.

A **project management information system (PMIS)** is the set of tools used to **gather, integrate and distribute** the outputs of the project management processes: scheduling software, work authorization systems, **configuration management** systems, information collection and distribution, and automated reporting of key performance indicators.

## Data gathering

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Data gathering; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Brainstorming; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Interviews; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Benchmarking

| Technique | What it is | Best when |
|---|---|---|
| **Brainstorming** | A group generates many ideas in a short time, led by a facilitator, **without judging** them at first | You need a wide range of ideas |
| **Interviews** | Talking directly with stakeholders, formally or informally | You need detail, or the subject is confidential |
| **Focus groups** | Prequalified stakeholders and experts in a guided, conversational discussion | You want to learn expectations and attitudes |
| **Questionnaires and surveys** | Written questions sent to many people | The audience is **large or spread out** and a quick turnaround is needed |
| **Benchmarking** | Comparing with the practices or products of others to find best practices | You want a basis for measuring performance or ideas for improvement |
| **Checklists** | A list of items to consider, built from past projects | You want to make sure nothing known is forgotten |
| **Observation** (job shadowing) | Watching people do their work | People find it hard to describe what they do |

## Data analysis

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Data analysis; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Alternative analysis; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Root cause analysis; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Variance analysis; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Trend analysis

| Technique | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| **Alternative analysis** | Which of the available options or approaches should we use? |
| **Root cause analysis** | What is the **basic underlying reason** for this variance, defect or risk? |
| **Variance analysis** | How far is **actual** performance from the **baseline**, and why? |
| **Trend analysis** | Based on past results, where is performance **heading**? |
| **What-if analysis** | What would happen to the objectives **if** this scenario occurred? |
| **Cost-benefit analysis** | Do the benefits of this option outweigh its costs? |
| **Document analysis** | What relevant information is in the existing documents? |
| **Assumption and constraint analysis** | Are our assumptions valid, and what do the constraints limit? |
| **SWOT analysis** | What are the strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats? |
| **Stakeholder analysis** | Who are the stakeholders, and what are their interests and influence? |
| **Reserve analysis** | Is the remaining reserve enough for the remaining risk? |
| **Simulation** (Monte Carlo) | What is the range of possible outcomes, and how likely is each? |

- **Variance** looks **back** (where are we against the plan?). **Trend** looks **forward** (where are we going?).
- A root cause can lie behind several problems. Remove the root cause and they do not come back.

## Data representation

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Data representation; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Affinity diagram; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Mind mapping; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Information radiators

| Tool | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **Affinity diagram** | A large number of ideas sorted into **groups** |
| **Mind map** | Ideas arranged around a central topic, showing what they have in common and where they differ |
| **Flowchart** | The steps and decision points of a process |
| **Cause-and-effect diagram** | Causes branching off a problem |
| **Histogram, pie chart, scatter diagram** | Distributions, proportions and relationships in data |
| **Responsibility assignment matrix** (RACI) | Who does what |
| **Probability and impact matrix** | The priority of each risk |
| **Stakeholder engagement assessment matrix** | Current and desired engagement levels |
| **Hierarchical charts** | Breakdowns: WBS, organizational and resource breakdown structures |
| **Burndown chart** | Work **remaining** against time left in a timebox |
| **Burnup chart** | Work **completed** toward a milestone |

An **information radiator** is a **visible, physical display** placed where everyone can see it, updated often, and usually kept by hand ("low tech, high touch"). A task board is a common example.

## Decision-making

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Decision-making; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Voting; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Multicriteria decision analysis; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Prioritization/ranking; PMBOK 6, 6.4.2.7

| Method | How the decision is reached |
|---|---|
| **Unanimity** | **Everyone** agrees on one course of action |
| **Majority** | **More than 50%** of the group agrees. An odd number of people avoids a tie. |
| **Plurality** | The **largest block** decides, even without a majority. Used when there are more than two options. |
| **Autocratic** | **One person** decides for the group |
| **Multicriteria decision analysis** | Options are scored in a **decision matrix** against **weighted criteria**, then ranked by score |

Example: 10 people vote on three options and the result is 4, 3, 3. No option has a majority (that would need 6). By **plurality**, the option with 4 votes wins.

Agile teams often vote in ways that reveal everyone's view at once, such as **fist of five**, where each person shows zero to five fingers for their level of support.

**Prioritization** methods rank requirements or stakeholders. The best known is **MoSCoW**:

| Letter | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **M** | **Must have** |
| **S** | **Should have** |
| **C** | **Could have** |
| **W** | **Will not have** (this time) |

## Interpersonal and team skills

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Interpersonal and team skills; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Facilitation; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Active listening; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Negotiation

| Skill | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Facilitation** | Guiding a group event to a successful decision or conclusion: everyone takes part, all contributions are considered, the result has full buy-in |
| **Active listening** | Acknowledging, clarifying and confirming, to reduce misunderstanding |
| **Conflict management** | Resolving disagreement; collaborating is usually best |
| **Negotiation** | Discussion aimed at reaching agreement |
| **Influencing** | Getting cooperation without direct authority, which project managers often lack |
| **Emotional intelligence** | Recognizing and managing your own emotions and those of others |
| **Nominal group technique** | A structured method that collects everyone's input before discussion, so vocal members do not dominate |
| **Cultural and political awareness** | Understanding differences between people and the formal and informal power in the organization |

## Meetings

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Meetings; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Meeting management; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Daily coordination meeting; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Retrospectives; PMBOK 6, 4.2.2.4; PMBOK 7, Guide 4.4.3

| Meeting | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Kickoff** | Start the project or phase: share objectives and gain the team's commitment |
| **Status update** | Review progress, issues and risks |
| **Daily coordination meeting** (daily standup) | A brief daily meeting: progress since yesterday, intentions for today, obstacles |
| **Sprint or iteration planning** | Decide what the team will deliver in the next iteration |
| **Sprint review** | Demonstrate completed work to stakeholders and get feedback |
| **Retrospective** | The team looks at how it worked, to improve the **process**. Held at least at the end of each iteration. |
| **Lessons learned** | Capture what went well and what did not, for future work |
| **Change control board meeting** | Decide on change requests |

Running a good meeting:

1. Prepare and distribute an **agenda** stating the objectives.
2. Invite the **right participants**.
3. **Start and finish on time**, and stay on topic.
4. Manage expectations, issues and conflicts during the meeting.
5. **Record the actions** and who is responsible for each.

> **On the exam.** The Seventh Edition lists sixteen meetings and events. The outline asks for the purpose of each kind, so learn what sets them apart:

| Meeting | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Kickoff** | At the outset of a project, phase or iteration: set expectations, gain a common understanding and start the work |
| **Planning meeting** | Create, elaborate or review a plan, and secure commitment to it |
| **Status meeting** | Regularly scheduled: exchange and analyze information on current progress and performance |
| **Daily standup** (daily scrum) | Brief: progress since yesterday, intentions for today, obstacles met or expected |
| **Backlog refinement** | Elaborate and reprioritize the backlog to find the work for an upcoming iteration |
| **Iteration planning** (sprint planning) | Clarify backlog items, acceptance criteria and effort for the coming iteration |
| **Iteration review** (sprint review) | At the end of an iteration: demonstrate the work accomplished |
| **Retrospective** | Regularly: explore work and results to improve process and product. A form of lessons learned meeting. |
| **Lessons learned meeting** | Identify and share knowledge gained in a project, phase or iteration, good and bad |
| **Release planning** | A high-level plan for releasing a product, deliverable or increment |
| **Risk review** | Check the status of existing risks, whether responses work, and identify new risks |
| **Change control board** | The people accountable for reviewing, approving, delaying or rejecting changes; decisions are recorded and communicated |
| **Steering committee** | Senior stakeholders give direction and support, and make decisions **outside the team's authority** |
| **Bidder conference** | With prospective sellers before bids, so that all have the same understanding of the procurement |
| **Project review** | At the end of a phase or project: assess status and value delivered, and decide whether to move to the next phase or to operations |
| **Project closeout** | Obtain **final acceptance** of the delivered scope from the sponsor, product owner or client |

## Choosing the right tool

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 5

| The situation | The tool |
|---|---|
| "We need ideas, quickly, from the whole group" | **Brainstorming** |
| "We have 80 ideas on sticky notes and need to make sense of them" | **Affinity diagram** |
| "Why does this defect keep coming back?" | **Root cause analysis**, cause-and-effect diagram |
| "How far are we from the plan?" | **Variance analysis** |
| "Will we still be on budget in three months?" | **Trend analysis** |
| "We must choose between three suppliers on several criteria" | **Multicriteria decision analysis** |
| "We need the views of 2,000 users in five countries" | **Questionnaires and surveys** |
| "How do leading companies handle this?" | **Benchmarking** |
| "Which requirements are essential?" | **Prioritization** (MoSCoW) |
| "Nobody on the team has done this before" | **Expert judgment** |
| "The loudest person always wins the discussion" | **Nominal group technique**, or voting revealed at the same time |
| "Everyone should see progress at a glance" | **Information radiator**, burndown chart |

## Exam tips

- Read for the **purpose** in the question: collect, analyze, show, or decide.
- **More than 50%** is a **majority**. The **largest group** without 50% is a **plurality**.
- "Large or geographically spread audience" points to **surveys**. "Small group, guided discussion" points to a **focus group**.
- "Baseline versus actual" is **variance analysis**. "Forecast from history" is **trend analysis**.
- A **retrospective** improves the **process**. A **sprint review** shows the **product**.
- An **affinity diagram groups** ideas. A **mind map connects** them. **Brainstorming generates** them.
- **Expert judgment** is a safe answer only when the question is really about lacking knowledge. If a more specific tool fits, choose that.
- **Burndown** = work remaining. **Burnup** = work completed.
