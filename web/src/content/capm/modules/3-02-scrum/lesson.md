## Key points

- **Scrum** is a single-team framework for developing a product in fixed-length cycles called **sprints**, each **one month or less**.
- **Three roles:** **product owner** (what to build, in what order), **development team** (how to build it), **scrum master** (the process, and removing impediments).
- **Five events:** the **sprint**, **sprint planning**, **daily scrum**, **sprint review**, **sprint retrospective**.
- **Three artifacts:** **product backlog**, **sprint backlog**, **increment**.
- The **sprint review** inspects the **product**. The **retrospective** inspects the **process**.
- The **daily scrum** lasts at most **15 minutes** and is for the team to coordinate, not to report status or solve problems.
- Work counts only when it meets the **definition of done**.

## What Scrum is

@source Agile Practice Guide, A3.2

Scrum is a **single-team process framework** used to manage product development. It consists of **roles, events, artifacts and rules**, and delivers working product **iteratively**.

- Work is done in **sprints**: timeboxes of **one month or less**, all of the **same length**.
- Each sprint produces a **potentially releasable increment** of the product.
- The short cycle lets the team **inspect, adapt** and deliver value frequently.

Scrum rests on three ideas, often called its pillars:

| Pillar | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Transparency** | The work and its progress are visible to everyone |
| **Inspection** | The product and the process are checked frequently |
| **Adaptation** | What is learned is used to adjust quickly |

Scrum is the most widely used agile framework, so it is the one most often tested.

## The three roles

@source Agile Practice Guide, A3.2; Agile Practice Guide, 4.4.2

| Role | Responsible for | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| **Product owner** | **Maximizing the value** of the product | Owns and **orders the product backlog** by business value. Works with stakeholders and customers to set direction. Works with the team daily. **Accepts or declines** finished work. One person, not a committee. |
| **Development team** | **Delivering the working product** | **Cross-functional:** has all the skills needed, with no dependence on people outside the team. **Self-organizing:** decides how to do the work. Small, fewer than 10 people. |
| **Scrum master** | Making sure the **Scrum process** is followed | **Coaches** the team, **removes impediments**, facilitates. A **servant leader**, not a boss: does not assign tasks. |

- There is **no project manager** role in Scrum. The traditional duties are shared: the product owner manages scope and priorities, the team manages its own work, the scrum master manages the process.
- In PMI's wider wording, the scrum master is a **team facilitator** or supportive leader.
- **Strong product ownership** is a critical success factor. Without it the team may build features nobody values.

## The three artifacts

@source Agile Practice Guide, A3.2; Agile Practice Guide, 5.2.1

| Artifact | What it is | Owned by |
|---|---|---|
| **Product backlog** | The **ordered list of all the work** for the product. It is never finished; it changes as more is learned. | Product owner |
| **Sprint backlog** | The items the team **selected for this sprint**, plus its plan for delivering them | Development team |
| **Increment** | The **finished, usable** work produced in the sprint, added to all earlier increments | The whole Scrum team |

- The backlog does not need every item written before work starts: only enough to understand the first release in outline, and enough detail for the **next** sprint.
- The product owner may use a **product roadmap** to show the expected sequence of deliverables over time.
- Items near the top of the backlog are small and detailed. Items lower down are larger and vaguer.

## The sprint and its events

@source Agile Practice Guide, A3.2; Agile Practice Guide, 5.2.3

| Event | Purpose | Who | Timebox for a one-month sprint |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sprint** | The container for all the work and the other events | Whole Scrum team | One month or less |
| **Sprint planning** | Decide **what** will be delivered this sprint and **how** | Whole Scrum team | Up to 8 hours |
| **Daily scrum** | Coordinate the next 24 hours | Development team | **15 minutes** |
| **Sprint review** | Show the **increment** to stakeholders and get **feedback** | Scrum team and stakeholders | Up to 4 hours |
| **Sprint retrospective** | Improve **how the team works** | Scrum team | Up to 3 hours |

The order inside a sprint: **planning → daily work with a daily scrum → review → retrospective**, then the next sprint starts immediately.

- A **timebox** is a fixed maximum time. When it ends, the event ends.
- Shorter sprints have proportionally shorter events.
- During a sprint, the **sprint goal** and the sprint length do not change. If something urgent appears, it goes into the product backlog for a later sprint.

## Sprint planning and backlog refinement

@source Agile Practice Guide, 5.2.3; Agile Practice Guide, 5.2.2

**Sprint planning** opens the sprint.

- The **product owner** presents the highest-priority items.
- The **team** decides **how much it can take on**, based on its **capacity**. Nobody assigns work to the team.
- Capacity changes: if people are on holiday, the team plans less.
- The result is the **sprint backlog** and a sprint goal.

**Backlog refinement** (once called grooming) prepares items for future sprints.

- The product owner works with the team to clarify stories, split large ones and estimate their relative size.
- It usually happens in the **middle of a sprint**, to get ready for the next one.
- A common guide is **no more than about one hour a week**, so that most time goes to doing the work.
- A **definition of ready (DoR)** lists what an item needs before the team will take it into a sprint.
- If the team cannot judge an item because of an unknown, it can run a **spike**: a short piece of research.

## The daily scrum

@source Agile Practice Guide, 5.2.4

A meeting of **no more than 15 minutes**, held every day, in which the team coordinates and commits to each other. Each person answers three questions:

1. What have I **completed** since the last meeting?
2. What do I plan to **complete** before the next one?
3. What are my **impediments**?

- It is the **team's** meeting. Anyone on the team can facilitate it; it does not have to be the scrum master.
- It is **not a status meeting** for a manager. That is a common mistake by teams coming from predictive work.
- It is for **finding** problems, **not solving** them. Problems go to a "**parking lot**" and are handled right afterwards by the people involved.
- The scrum master then works on **removing the impediments** raised.

## Sprint review and sprint retrospective

@source Agile Practice Guide, 5.2.6; Agile Practice Guide, 5.2.7

| | **Sprint review** | **Sprint retrospective** |
|---|---|---|
| Looks at | The **product**: what was built | The **process**: how the team worked |
| Who attends | Scrum team **and stakeholders** | Scrum team only |
| What happens | The team **demonstrates** the working increment; the product owner **accepts or declines** each item; feedback changes the backlog | The team discusses what went well and what did not, finds root causes and chooses improvements |
| Result | An updated product backlog | A **small number** of improvement actions for the next sprint |
| Order | First | Last event of the sprint |

About the review:

- A team should demonstrate working product **at least every two weeks**.
- A team that never demonstrates or releases cannot learn fast enough and is probably not really working in an agile way.

About the retrospective:

- It is **not about blame**. It needs **psychological safety**: everyone can speak openly without fear.
- It uses both feelings and measurements.
- **Limit the number of actions** to what the team can actually do. Finishing two improvements beats starting ten.
- It is often called the most important agile practice, because it is how the team gets better.

## Definition of done and the increment

@source Agile Practice Guide, 5.2.8

The **definition of done (DoD)** is the team's shared checklist of what "finished" means. Typically an increment is:

- **integrated** with related work and passing its tests or reviews,
- **compliant** with quality, safety or regulatory standards,
- **documented**, with any training or instructions needed,
- **accepted** by the product owner.

Why it matters:

- An item that does not meet the definition of done is **not done**. It is not shown as complete in the review and goes back to the product backlog.
- There is no "90% done". **Done or not done.**
- Releasing increments often **confirms value**, exposes hidden work such as deployment and documentation, and **reduces risk**.
- The **product owner** decides when to release: after every item, or in batches on a regular cadence.

| | Definition of ready | Definition of done |
|---|---|---|
| Question | Can the team **start** this item? | Is this item **finished**? |
| Applies | Before an item enters a sprint | Before an item counts as complete |

## Exam tips

- "Who prioritizes the backlog?" The **product owner**. "Who decides how much work fits in a sprint?" The **development team**. "Who removes impediments?" The **scrum master**.
- The scrum master **does not assign tasks** and is **not** the team's manager.
- A stakeholder brings a new request mid-sprint: it goes to the **product owner** and the **product backlog**, not straight into the sprint.
- Daily scrum: **15 minutes**, three questions, **no problem-solving**.
- **Review = product, with stakeholders. Retrospective = process, team only.**
- Unfinished work at the end of a sprint: **not demonstrated as done**, returned to the product backlog and re-prioritized. The sprint is **not extended**.
- Sprint length is **fixed and consistent**, one month or less.
- Team members who try to solve a problem during the daily scrum: move it to the **parking lot**.
- Older or other material uses different words for the same things: **developers** for the development team, **iteration** for sprint, **daily standup** or **daily coordination meeting** for the daily scrum.
