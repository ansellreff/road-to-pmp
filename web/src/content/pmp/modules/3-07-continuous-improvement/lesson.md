## Key points

- **Lessons learned** are knowledge gained during a project that shows how events were, or should be, handled, to improve future performance. Use them **from the start**, not only at the end.
- The **lessons learned register** is created early and updated throughout; at closure its content goes into the **lessons learned repository**, part of the organizational process assets.
- Ways to learn: **retrospectives** (at least every iteration), **after-action reviews**, **in-progress postmortems**, **storytelling**, knowledge and information management.
- **Continuous improvement** (PDCA, Lean, TQM, Six Sigma, Five Focusing Steps) makes small, measured improvements; retrospectives limit actions to what the team can finish.
- Keep improvement processes **current**: review checklists, templates and procedures, and remove what is obsolete.
- **Update OPAs**: knowledge repositories are updated by the project; policies and procedures change through the organization, so forward suggestions to the owners.

## Lessons learned in the Eighth Edition

@source PMBOK 8, Glossary (lessons learned); PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Lessons learned register; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.6; PMBOK 8, Standard 2.2.2; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.7.2.2; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.4.2.2

The first enabler of Business Environment task 6 is to **utilize lessons learned**.

- **Lessons learned:** the knowledge gained during a project that shows how project events were addressed, or should be addressed, to improve future performance.
- The **lessons learned register** records knowledge gained during a project, phase or iteration: the **category and description** of the situation, its **impacts, recommendations and proposed actions**, challenges, problems, realized risks and opportunities.
- It is created **early** (in Manage Project Knowledge) and updated throughout; the people involved in the work help capture the lessons.
- Lessons from **previous projects** feed the current project; the current project's lessons go into the **lessons learned repository** for others.
- **Manage Project Knowledge** uses existing knowledge and creates new knowledge, to achieve the project's objectives and contribute to **organizational learning**.

| Where to use past lessons | Example |
|---|---|
| **Risk identification** | Checklists built from completed projects; the lessons learned register is an input to Identify Risks |
| **Estimating** | Historical information for analogous and parametric estimates |
| **Planning** | Plans adjusted to avoid problems seen before; lessons learned register as an input to Estimate Costs and Develop Schedule |
| **Every iteration** | The last retrospective's improvements tried in the next iteration |

## Ways of capturing lessons

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, After-action reviews (AARs); PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, In-progress postmortems; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Retrospective meetings; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Storytelling; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Knowledge management; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Information management

| Technique | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Retrospective** | A lessons learned meeting held **frequently** (at least at the end of each iteration): what worked, what did not, recommendations. The whole team takes part. The term can apply to any approach |
| **After-action review (AAR)** | From the U.S. Army: at the end of a project or after major milestones. Four questions: **what was supposed to happen, what actually happened, why were there differences, what can we learn?** All involved take part; findings are documented and shared |
| **In-progress postmortem** | Structured reviews at **various stages**, not only at the end, so problems are fixed in time |
| **Storytelling** | Narratives that make lessons relatable and memorable; effective for **tacit** knowledge |
| **Knowledge management** | Connects people: networking, **communities of practice**, work shadowing, discussion forums, knowledge fairs, interactive training |
| **Information management** | Connects people to **explicit** knowledge: codified lessons, the register, library services, information gathering |

## Retrospectives that lead to improvement

@source Agile Practice Guide, 5.2.7; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Retrospective meetings

- The retrospective may be **the most important agile practice**: the team learns about, improves and adapts its **process**. ("At regular intervals, the team reflects on how to become more effective, then tunes and adjusts its behavior accordingly.")
- Hold one after a release, when weeks have passed, when the team is **stuck**, or at any milestone; iterations are not required.
- It is **not about blame**: create a psychologically safe space where everyone can speak candidly.
- Use **qualitative** (feelings) and **quantitative** (measurements) data to find **root causes**, design countermeasures and agree actions.
- **Limit the actions** to the team's capacity: finishing a few improvements beats starting many. Rank them, decide **how to measure** each outcome, and check the results next time.

> **On the exam.** Lessons are captured **throughout** the project, by everyone, and used at the **start** of new work. A single lessons learned meeting on the last day is too late.

## Keeping continuous improvement processes current

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Continuous improvement; PMBOK 8, Standard 3.5; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Checklists; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Process improvement; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Audits

The enabler: **help ensure continuous improvement processes are updated**.

- **Continuous improvement** identifies, analyzes and implements **incremental** improvements to processes, products, systems or services, using **PDCA**, **Lean**, **Total Quality Management**, **Six Sigma**, the **Five Focusing Steps** and similar methods.
- A **continuous improvement mindset** optimizes processes and grows the **maturity** of the whole organization, not only the project.
- **Checklists** should be **reviewed from time to time** to add new information and remove or archive what is obsolete.
- **Process improvement** analyzes current processes, finds areas to improve and changes them for better performance and lower waste.
- **Quality audits** share good practices from similar projects and add each audit's contributions to the lessons learned repository.
- If an improvement changes how the team works, update the **project management plan** and the team's working agreements so the improvement sticks.

## Updating organizational process assets

@source PMBOK 8, Standard 2.2.2; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Organizational process asset (OPA) updates; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.9.1; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.6

The enabler: **update organizational process assets (OPAs)**.

| OPA group | Who updates it, and how |
|---|---|
| **Organizational knowledge repositories** (lessons learned, historical information, metrics, issues and defects) | Updated **throughout the project** by the project team |
| **Policies, processes and procedures** (templates, life cycles, methods, checklists) | Usually owned by the **PMO** or another function; updated only **through organizational policy**; some organizations let teams tailor templates |

- **Any OPA** can be updated as a result of a process; Manage Project Knowledge and Close Project or Phase both list **OPA updates** as outputs.
- At closure: collect and audit records, document lessons learned, **archive** project information, and **forward suggestions** for improving policies and procedures to the appropriate unit.
- Make lessons **findable**: historical information and lessons learned should be available to the current and future projects.

## Predictive and agile

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Retrospective meetings; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.9.2; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, After-action reviews (AARs)

| | Predictive | Agile |
|---|---|---|
| When | At phase ends and milestones, and through the project; final lessons at closure | Every iteration and release |
| How | Lessons learned register, after-action reviews, in-progress postmortems | Retrospectives, small experiments |
| Where it goes | Lessons learned repository, OPA updates | Team working agreements, backlog of improvements, and the repository |

## Exam tips

- Use lessons learned at the start; capture them throughout; finalize them at closure.
- Everyone involved contributes to lessons learned.
- Retrospectives are blame-free and end with a few measurable actions.
- Review checklists and templates; archive obsolete content.
- Teams update knowledge repositories; policies change through the organization.
