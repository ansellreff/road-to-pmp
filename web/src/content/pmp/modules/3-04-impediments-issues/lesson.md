## Key points

- An **impediment** (blocker) is an obstacle that stops the team from reaching its objectives. An **issue** is a current condition that may affect objectives: it **has already happened**. A **risk** is uncertain and **in the future**.
- A risk that occurs **becomes an issue**: it moves from the risk register to the **issue log**, and its planned response (or a **workaround**) is put into action.
- **Evaluate** each impediment's impact, **prioritize** and **make it visible** (impediment list, issue log, board), and choose an **intervention**: the team solves it, the leader removes it, or it is **escalated**.
- Supportive leaders **remove organizational impediments**, such as slow approval, release or documentation processes.
- **Reassess continually**: daily coordination meetings, the issue log and retrospectives keep impediments moving.
- **Resolve issues with the relevant stakeholders**, with an owner, a target date and a recorded final solution.

## Impediments, issues and risks

@source PMBOK 8, Glossary (impediment); PMBOK 8, Guide 2.7.1; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Issue log

| Term | Meaning | Recorded in |
|---|---|---|
| **Impediment** | An obstacle that prevents the team from achieving its objectives; also called a **blocker** | Impediment list, task board, issue log |
| **Issue** | A current condition or situation that may affect one or more objectives; it has **already occurred** and may need **immediate action** or management attention | **Issue log** |
| **Risk** | An uncertain event or condition that, **if it occurs**, has a positive or negative effect on objectives | **Risk register** |

- Risks and issues are closely related: an issue may arise from a **poorly managed risk**. But issues have occurred or are still occurring, while risks have not occurred yet.
- **Ambiguous or uncertain** situations do not always become risks; often they are resolved through **collaborative problem-solving** as information arrives and experts get involved.
- Reviewing the **issue log** and **risk register** together can show challenges with individual stakeholders.

## Evaluating the impact of impediments

@source Agile Practice Guide, 5.4; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Visual controls; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.7.3

The first enabler of Business Environment task 4 is to **evaluate the impact of impediments**.

- Ask what the impediment blocks: which work items, which goal, which dates, which stakeholders.
- Look across domains: an impediment may hit the schedule, the budget, the scope and stakeholders at once, so view its impact **holistically**.
- For unexpected delays, the Agile Practice Guide suggests: **visualize blockers**, run **"delay diagnostics"**, limit work in process and shorten feedback loops.
- Note how long an item has been blocked: dots on a task board show how many days it has sat in place.

## Prioritizing and highlighting impediments

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Visual controls; Agile Practice Guide, 5.2.4; Agile Practice Guide, 4.2.3; PMBOK 6, 4.3.3.3

The enabler: **prioritize and highlight impediments**.

- An **impediment list** on the visual controls shows each impediment, its **severity**, and the **actions** being taken to resolve it.
- In the **daily coordination meeting**, each member names **"my impediments (or risks or problems)"**; flow-based teams ask whether there are **bottlenecks or blockers** to the flow.
- In a psychologically safe team, members **raise impediments immediately**, ask for help and swarm on blocked work **without fear of blame**; a **visible board** shortens the time to remove them.
- The **issue log** gives each issue a **priority**, so the most damaging ones get attention first.

## Choosing an intervention strategy

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Problem-solving; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Root cause analysis; Agile Practice Guide, 4.3.1.1; Agile Practice Guide, 4.3.1.2; Agile Practice Guide, 5.4; Agile Practice Guide, 4.3.2

The enabler: **determine and apply an intervention strategy to remove or minimize impediments**.

| Who acts | Intervention |
|---|---|
| **The team** | **Problem-solving**: gather information, think critically, try creative, quantitative or logical approaches; **swarm** on the blocked work; **timebox a spike** to learn |
| **The leader as facilitator** | Expose and communicate **bottlenecks** inside and between teams, so the teams resolve them; act as a bridge builder, not the decision-maker |
| **The leader removing organizational impediments** | Work with departments whose processes slow the team: extensive documentation, finance, **change control boards**, audits, long release queues |
| **Escalation** | **Empower impediment escalation** when the obstacle is beyond the team; in larger settings the project manager escalates and removes **organization-level** impediments |

- Find the **root cause**: when all root causes are removed, the problem does not recur.
- Minimizing an impediment is sometimes the best available step; removing it fully may take longer.

> **On the exam.** In agile questions, the servant leader **removes impediments** so the team can work; the leader does not take over the team's work or make the team's decisions. In predictive questions, the project manager logs the issue, assigns an owner and resolves or escalates it.

## Reassessing continually

@source Agile Practice Guide, 5.2.4; Agile Practice Guide, 5.2.7; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.7.2.6; PMBOK 6, 4.3.3.3

The enabler: **reassess continually to help ensure impediments, obstacles and blockers for the team are being addressed**.

- The **daily coordination meeting** (15 minutes) surfaces new impediments and checks the old ones.
- The **issue log** is **updated throughout the project** by monitoring and controlling, until each issue is investigated and resolved.
- **Retrospectives** may produce many action items to remove impediments; the team picks the most important and follows through.
- **Monitor Risks** and **Implement Risk Responses** update the issue log as risks turn into issues.

## Recognizing when a risk becomes an issue

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.7.1; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.7.2.5; Practice Standard for Scheduling, Glossary (workaround)

The enabler: **recognize when a risk becomes an issue**.

- When an identified risk **occurs**, it is no longer uncertain: it is now an **issue**, recorded in the **issue log** and handled now.
- If a response was planned (for example a **contingency plan** with a trigger), carry it out: **Implement Risk Responses** executes the agreed responses and updates the issue log and risk register.
- A **workaround** is a response to a negative risk that has occurred.
- An issue that came from a poorly managed risk is a lesson: record it, and check whether other risks are being managed the same way.

## Resolving issues with stakeholders

@source PMBOK 6, 4.3.3.3; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.7.1; PMI Code of Ethics, 3.2; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.4

The enabler: **collaborate with relevant stakeholders on an approach to resolve the issues**.

The **issue log** may record: issue type; **who raised it and when**; description; **priority**; **who is assigned**; **target resolution date**; **status**; and the **final solution**.

- Involve the stakeholders who are affected or who can solve the issue; **subject matter experts** help turn ambiguity into a clear decision.
- When the issue is a **conflict or disagreement**, approach the people involved **directly** first, and choose the best way to resolve it.
- **Escalate** when the solution needs authority beyond the team or project manager, along the agreed path (module 3.1).
- Close the issue only when the solution is in place, and record it.

## Predictive and agile

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.7.1; Agile Practice Guide, 4.3.1.2; Agile Practice Guide, 5.2.4

| | Predictive | Agile |
|---|---|---|
| Where it is tracked | Issue log, with owner, priority and target date | Impediment list, task board, daily meeting |
| Who removes it | The assigned owner; the project manager resolves or escalates | The team swarms; the supportive leader removes organizational impediments |
| How often reviewed | Status meetings and monitoring | Daily, and in retrospectives |

## Exam tips

- Issue = happening now; risk = might happen. A risk that occurs becomes an issue.
- Make impediments visible; prioritize by impact.
- The servant leader removes impediments; the team solves its own work problems.
- Find the root cause, not just the symptom.
- Every issue gets an owner, a target date and a recorded solution.
- Escalate only what is beyond your authority, along the agreed path.
