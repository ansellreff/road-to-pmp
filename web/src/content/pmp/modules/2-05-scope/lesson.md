## Key points

- The Scope domain makes sure the project includes **all the work required, and only the work required**. Scope is "the most important component of any project's baseline", because the project's value comes from it.
- **Requirements** are conditions or capabilities needed to satisfy a business need. Before baselining they should be **unambiguous (measurable and testable), traceable, complete, consistent and acceptable**.
- **Predictive**: scope statement + **WBS** + WBS dictionary = **scope baseline**, changed only through formal change control. **Adaptive**: a prioritized **product backlog**, refined into epics, features and user stories; the baseline is set at the start of each iteration and the product owner approves changes.
- The **WBS** decomposes the total scope into **work packages** (the lowest level, where cost and duration are estimated). The **100 percent rule**: all the work, including project management work, and nothing extra.
- **Monitor and Control Scope** produces **verified deliverables**; **Validate Scope** turns them into **accepted deliverables** through formal acceptance by the authorized stakeholder.
- Stakeholder agreement on scope comes from shared understanding (scope statement, exclusions, acceptance criteria) and from early involvement of whoever accepts the deliverables.

## Scope and value

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.2; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.2.1

**The Scope performance domain** covers the processes that ensure the project encompasses **all the work required** and that **no unnecessary work** is performed, optimizing cost and schedule to maximize value. It also covers **quality**: meeting stakeholder expectations and fulfilling project and product requirements, with deliverables meeting **acceptance criteria**.

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Project scope** | The work performed to deliver a product, service or result with the specified features and functions. It encapsulates the expected value, so it is "the most important component of any project's baseline" |
| **Product scope** | The features, functions and characteristics of what the project delivers: what it should look like and do, including quality and performance specifications |
| **Requirement** | A condition or capability that must be present to satisfy a business need |
| **Quality as a feature** | Quality is part of scope: a bridge's scope includes target thresholds for how sturdy, long-lasting and maintainable it is |
| **Value breakdown structure (VBS)** | Links the scope to its intended value: each major deliverable carries a value estimate (or a percentage of total value, 100% if mandatory), used to prioritize deliverables and work |
| **Definition of done (DoD)** | The criteria a deliverable must meet to be ready for customer use; in adaptive work, the shared criteria before a story, feature or increment counts as complete |

The six processes: **Plan Scope Management**, **Elicit and Analyze Requirements**, **Define Scope**, **Develop Scope Structure** (Planning), **Monitor and Control Scope** and **Validate Scope** (Monitoring and Controlling).

## Defining scope: requirements and the scope statement

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.2.2.1; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.2.2.2; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.2.2.3; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Requirements documentation; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Project scope statement

The first enabler of Process task 2 is to **define scope**.

**Plan Scope Management** creates the **scope management plan**: how scope will be defined, developed, monitored, controlled and validated, establishing all the required work and **eliminating work that adds no value**. It is more iterative and collaborative in adaptive projects.

**Elicit and Analyze Requirements** defines and documents stakeholders' needs for the features and functions. In adaptive work the requirements are collected as **user stories** prioritized in a backlog. Tools include benchmarking, **brainstorming**, **focus groups**, **interviews**, questionnaires and surveys, document analysis, the **nominal group technique**, **design thinking**, prioritization and meetings.

**Requirements documentation** describes how requirements meet the business need. Requirements become more detailed over time; **before being baselined** they should be:

- **unambiguous** (measurable and testable),
- **traceable**,
- **complete**,
- **consistent**, and
- **acceptable to key stakeholders**.

The **requirements traceability matrix** links each requirement from its origin to the deliverable that satisfies it, and to the business and project objectives, so every requirement adds value and none is lost.

**Define Scope** develops a detailed or high-level description of the project, product and value, and identifies the **quality requirements and standards** and how compliance will be shown. Predictive: at the start, structured in the WBS. Adaptive and hybrid: at the start of each iteration, progressively defined in the backlog; in agile, scope is often represented at a high level by the **product roadmap** and its releases.

The **project scope statement** describes the project scope, **major deliverables**, assumptions and constraints, and **acceptance criteria**; it may contain explicit **exclusions**, which help manage stakeholder expectations.

## Obtaining stakeholder agreement on scope

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.2.2.3; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Project scope statement; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Accepted deliverables; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.2.3

The second enabler is to **obtain stakeholder agreement on project scope**.

- Define Scope's key benefit is that **stakeholders and the project team understand the value** that will be delivered.
- The scope statement gives a **common understanding** of the scope among stakeholders; stated **exclusions** prevent false expectations.
- The **authorized stakeholder who will accept** the deliverables should be **involved early** and give feedback on quality as the work progresses.
- With **external partners**, scope must be integrated into the **contractual agreements** early, so all parties' commitments are synchronized.
- In **dynamic environments** (market volatility, fast technology change), scope management should be particularly **flexible**.

> **On the exam.** Stakeholders disagree about what is in or out of scope. The answer is to clarify and agree: requirements, exclusions and acceptance criteria written down and approved, not to start work and sort it out later. In agile, the product owner's prioritized backlog is where this agreement lives.

## Breaking down scope: the WBS and the backlog

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.2.2.4; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Decomposition; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.2.1; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Scope baseline

The third enabler is to **break down scope**: **Develop Scope Structure**.

**Predictive and hybrid: the WBS.** A **work breakdown structure** is a hierarchical decomposition of the **total scope** of work into smaller, manageable **work packages** that can be assigned, tracked and measured, giving clear ownership of each deliverable.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Decomposition** | Dividing and subdividing scope and deliverables into smaller, more manageable parts |
| **Work package** | The lowest level of the WBS, for which **cost and duration** can be estimated and managed |
| **100 percent rule** | The WBS covers **all product and project work, including project management work**; the lowest levels roll up so nothing is left out and **no extra work** is done |
| **Rolling wave planning** | Deliverables far in the future are decomposed later, once they are agreed |
| **WBS dictionary** | Details for each WBS component: scope description, milestones, responsible parties, resources, acceptance criteria |

The level of decomposition follows the **degree of control** needed; too much detail wastes effort and makes data hard to aggregate.

**The scope baseline** = approved **scope statement** + **WBS** + **WBS dictionary**. It is part of the **performance measurement baseline**, with the schedule and cost baselines.

**Adaptive: the backlog.** The **product backlog** is a dynamic, prioritized list of work items and features, focused on value. Its decomposition (product backlog breakdown) goes from **epics** to **features** to **user stories**. The baseline is defined at the **start of each iteration**, aligned with the prioritized requirements, and the **product owner** approves changes dynamically, without a formal change control procedure.

## Monitoring, controlling and validating scope

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.2.2.5; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.2.2.6; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Verified deliverables; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Accepted deliverables; PMBOK 8, Standard 3.4

| Process | What it does | Key outputs |
|---|---|---|
| **Monitor and Control Scope** | Monitors scope status, **manages changes to the scope baseline**, measures the quality of deliverables against standards | **Verified deliverables** (checked for correctness), quality reports, change requests |
| **Validate Scope** | Checks the quality processes and **formalizes acceptance** of the deliverables through an objective process | **Accepted deliverables**, change requests |

The sequence matters: a deliverable is first **verified** (correct, by quality control inside Monitor and Control Scope), then **validated** (accepted by the authorized stakeholder against the criteria). Validate Scope's tools include inspection, decision-making, customer talks and tests, and review meetings.

**Scope creep** (uncontrolled additions) and **gold plating** (extras nobody asked for) both work against value: a value-focused approach uses continuous scope management to prevent them.

## Predictive and agile

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.2.1; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.2.2.3; PMBOK 8, Standard 3.4

| | Predictive | Agile |
|---|---|---|
| Scope description | Detailed scope statement at the start | High-level roadmap and releases; detail added each iteration |
| Breakdown | WBS to work packages | Backlog: epics, features, user stories |
| Baseline | Scope baseline, formal change control | Set each iteration; the product owner approves changes |
| Done | Accepted deliverables through Validate Scope | Definition of done; product owner accepts stories |

## Exam tips

- All the work and only the work: no scope creep, no gold plating.
- Requirements must be measurable and testable, traceable, complete, consistent and acceptable.
- WBS: decompose to work packages; 100 percent rule includes project management work.
- Scope baseline = scope statement + WBS + WBS dictionary.
- Verified (by quality control) comes before validated (accepted by the customer).
