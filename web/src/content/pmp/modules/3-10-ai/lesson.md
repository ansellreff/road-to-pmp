## Key points

- **Artificial intelligence (AI)** describes technologies that simulate human behavior on computers: they learn from experience and adapt. **Machine learning** is part of AI, **deep learning** part of machine learning, and **generative AI** (built on **large language models**) part of deep learning.
- AI is an **emerging technology** in the external environment: it changes professions and projects, so project professionals need to understand it and adapt.
- Adopt AI by task complexity: **automation** (simple tasks, little human input), **assistance** (drafts a human reviews), **augmentation** (complex, strategic work with AI as a thinking partner). The more complex the task, the more **human intervention**.
- AI helps across domains: data-driven decisions, automated reporting, real-time monitoring and early warnings, risk identification, dynamic scheduling, stakeholder sentiment, meeting minutes.
- **Responsible use:** watch for **bias**, protect **privacy**, keep a **human accountable**, check **reliability**, ensure **safety** and **transparency**, respect **copyright**, consider **sustainability**.
- AI output can be wrong (**hallucinations**) and can expose sensitive information: projects need **responsible AI policies**, and the PMI Code makes us responsible for how we deploy AI.

## What AI is

@source PMBOK 8, Appendix X3.1; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Artificial intelligence (AI)

**AI** lets machines perform tasks they were not directly programmed to do, learn from experience and adapt to new situations: pattern recognition, fraud detection, robotics, drug discovery.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Artificial intelligence (AI)** | The broad term: systems that can reason, learn and act autonomously |
| **Machine learning (ML)** | A subfield of AI that trains models on data to predict outputs from previous inputs |
| **Deep learning (DL)** | Advanced ML using multilayered neural networks; needs large data sets and computing power |
| **Generative AI (GenAI)** | A subset of deep learning that uses **large language models (LLMs)** to generate new text, speech, audio, pictures and video |
| **Natural language processing (NLP)** | Software that processes human language: chatbots, speech recognition, summarization, sentiment analysis |

**Large language models** brought AI into the mainstream: it is now accessible with minimal manual effort, so every professional should understand it to stay productive.

## AI as a change in the business environment

@source PMBOK 8, Appendix X3.1; PMBOK 8, Standard 2.2.1; Agile Practice Guide, 6.1.1; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.7.3

Business Environment task 8 asks project managers to **survey changes in technology**, **assess their impact on scope or the backlog**, and **keep reviewing**. AI is the clearest current example.

- AI is creating a **major shift in most professions**: raising productivity, creating new jobs and possibly removing others. Its fast changes require **great adaptability** to keep up with new tools and ways of working.
- **Emerging technologies** such as AI, automation, blockchain and the Internet of Things are **external enterprise environmental factors**.
- **Technology and operating model shifts** (platforms, automation) are strategic drivers of organizational change.
- For a project, a new AI capability may be an **opportunity** (a cheaper, faster way to deliver) or a **threat** (a competitor's product, a new risk), and may add or remove scope. Assess it like any external change (module 3.9).
- Risk tailoring can include **GenAI and data analytics** for more complete risk identification and analysis.

## Strategies for adopting AI

@source PMBOK 8, Appendix X3.1.1; PMBOK 8, Appendix X3.2; PMBOK 8, Appendix X3.1.2

Classify tasks by **complexity** and the **need for human supervision**:

| Strategy | Tasks | Examples | The human's role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Automation** | Low complexity, little human input in the final output | Report generation, document analysis, call summaries; reusable standard prompts | Set up and spot-check |
| **Assistance** | AI complements analysis and builds ideas iteratively | A first draft of a **risk register** or a schedule with buffers | **Review** for accuracy and completeness; first drafts are never final |
| **Augmentation** | Strategic, complex work: enhancing and exploring capabilities | Balancing a portfolio to maximize ROI; risk forecasting from external variables | Use AI as a **brainstorming partner** over many iterations |

**Choosing tools:** free tools usually limit the number of prompts; paid versions often let users **stop their data being used to retrain models**, protecting **privacy and intellectual property**.

## Use cases across the project

@source PMBOK 8, Appendix X3.2; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.7.2.2; Agile Practice Guide, 5.2.7

| Area | AI use cases |
|---|---|
| **Governance** | Data-driven project selection and prioritization; multicriteria decision analysis; idea generation for business cases and charters; **automated reporting** on status and compliance; process automation; **real-time monitoring** against baselines; **early warning** signals from patterns in similar projects; baseline optimization; chatbots; task management; **automated meeting minutes** |
| **Risk** | **Risk identification and assessment** from historical data and benchmarks; suggested or automated mitigation actions; schedule risk impact analysis. AI is a tool of **Identify Risks** |
| **Stakeholders** | Stakeholder **sentiment analysis**; personalized communication (channels and frequency) |
| **Schedule** | **Dynamic scheduling** that adjusts to changes; schedule conflict resolution across projects |
| **Agile teams** | GenAI reads notes and boards, anonymizes text, suggests small experiments and tracks retrospective actions |

> **On the exam.** AI supports the project manager's judgment; it does not replace it. A schedule, risk register or report drafted by AI is reviewed by people before it is used or shared.

## Responsible use and ethical concerns

@source PMBOK 8, Appendix X3.3; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.6; PMI Code of Ethics, 2.2

AI shifts work from repetitive tasks to more creative ones, but **people** must weigh the risks and decide how AI is used.

| Concern | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Bias** | Diversify training data, test periodically for bias, involve different teams |
| **Privacy** | Secure sensitive data; collect it under a **privacy policy**; avoid uses that violate privacy and ethics |
| **Accountability** | A **human is accountable** for each decision, clearly defined |
| **Reliability** | **Check and validate** AI output: it may be biased, incorrect or irrelevant |
| **Safety** | Design, test and monitor AI systems to the required level of safety |
| **Transparency** | Share how data is used and handled, how algorithms work and how decisions are made |
| **Copyright** | Know the laws on the data used, and on ownership of AI-generated content |
| **Sustainability** | Each request uses electricity, water and other resources; weigh that when choosing to use AI |

- In knowledge management, AI risks include **unintentionally exposing sensitive information** and **hallucinations**; projects should have **responsible AI policies**.
- The **PMI Code of Ethics**: we are responsible for our leadership, management and **deployment of technology, including AI**.

## Exam tips

- AI > machine learning > deep learning > generative AI.
- Automation for simple tasks, assistance for drafts, augmentation for strategic thinking; more complexity, more human involvement.
- Always validate AI output; a human is accountable.
- Protect privacy: secure data, follow a privacy policy, choose tools that do not reuse your data.
- AI is an external technology change: assess its impact on scope and risk like any other.
