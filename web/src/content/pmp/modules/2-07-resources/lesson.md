## Key points

- The Resources domain covers **team resources** (people) and **physical or virtual resources** (equipment, materials, facilities, software, licenses, test environments). Five processes: **Plan Resource Management, Estimate Resources, Acquire Resources, Lead the Team, Monitor and Control Resourcing**.
- **Plan resources from the requirements**: the scope baseline, schedule, quality plan and risks drive what is needed. The **resource management plan** says how resources are acquired, allocated, monitored and controlled.
- **Estimate Resources** gives **resource requirements** per work package, a **basis of estimates** and the **resource breakdown structure**.
- **Acquire Resources** needs **negotiation and influence**: resources often belong to resource or functional managers. Alternatives are allowed if risks are acknowledged and no legal or mandatory criteria are broken.
- **Resource leveling** may move the **critical path** and the end date; **resource smoothing** stays within float and does not.
- **Monitor and Control Resourcing** keeps physical and virtual resources available at the right time and place, and **releases** them when no longer needed.

## The Resources performance domain

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.6; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.6.1; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.6.2

The domain covers planning, estimating and acquiring the resources needed, leading the team, and controlling resources, so that **the right resources are available at the right time and place**.

| Role or resource | In the Eighth Edition |
|---|---|
| **Project manager** | Responsible for forming and running the team; acquires, manages, motivates and empowers it, aware of location, politics, culture and organizational change |
| **Resource manager** | Has management authority over one or more resources. The project team may not control selection (collective agreements, subcontractors, a matrix) |
| **Internal resources** | Assigned by functional or resource managers |
| **External resources** | Obtained through partnerships, joint ventures or **procurement** (module 2.8) |
| **Physical, material or virtual resources** | Anything that is not a person: equipment, materials, supplies, facilities, infrastructure, software, test environments, licenses, services |

| Process | Focus area | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Plan Resource Management** | Planning | How to estimate, acquire, lead and use team, physical and virtual resources |
| **Estimate Resources** | Planning | Type and quantity of resources needed for the work |
| **Acquire Resources** | Executing | Obtain the team and the physical or virtual resources |
| **Lead the Team** | Executing | Guide, develop and manage the team (modules 1.3 and 1.4) |
| **Monitor and Control Resourcing** | Monitoring and Controlling | Physical and virtual resources available as planned; planned versus actual use |

## Defining and planning resources from the requirements

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.6.2.1; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Resource management plan; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Resource-based view; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Green human resource management

The first enabler of Process task 4 is to **define and plan resources based on requirements**.

**Plan Resource Management** establishes the approach and the **level of management effort** for resources, based on the project's **type and complexity**. It is performed once or at set points, and should plan for the **availability of, or competition for, scarce resources**.

| Inputs (examples) | Tools and techniques | Outputs |
|---|---|---|
| Project charter; quality management plan; **scope baseline**; project schedule; **requirements documentation**; risk register; stakeholder register | Interviews; SWOT analysis; hierarchical charts; **responsibility assignment matrix**; text-oriented formats; organizational theory; **green human resource management**; **resource-based view**; meetings | **Resource management plan**; **team charter**; updates to the assumption log and risk register |

**The resource management plan** describes how resources are **acquired, allocated, monitored and controlled**.

Two tools named for the first time in the Eighth Edition:

- **Resource-based view**: identifying the **strategic resources** that give a competitive advantage, using **VRIO** (value, rarity, imitability, organization).
- **Green human resource management**: people practices that support the **sustainable use of resources** and environmental performance.

## Estimating resource needs

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.6.2.2; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Resource requirements; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Resource calendars

**Estimate Resources** identifies the **type, quantity and characteristics** of resources (module 2.3), and is closely tied to the schedule.

| Inputs (examples) | Tools and techniques | Outputs |
|---|---|---|
| Resource management plan; schedule management plan; scope baseline; activity list and attributes; cost estimates; **resource calendars**; risk register | Expert judgment; **bottom-up**, **analogous** and **parametric** estimating; alternatives analysis; PMIS; interviews; AI and predictive analytics; virtual and augmented reality; optimization methods | **Resource requirements**; **basis of estimates**; **resource breakdown structure**; updates |

- **Resource requirements** list the types and quantities needed per work package or activity; they roll up to each WBS branch and the whole project.
- **Resource calendars** show **when and for how long** each resource is available, possibly with skill level and location.
- The **resource breakdown structure** is a hierarchy of resources by category and type.

## Acquiring resources

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.6.2.3; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Preassignment

**Acquire Resources** guides the selection of resources and assigns them to activities. It is performed periodically, as needed.

- The project manager or team should **collaborate, negotiate and influence** those who can provide the resources.
- **Failing to acquire** the needed resources can hurt schedule, budget, customer satisfaction and quality, raise risk, and in the worst case cancel the project.
- If resources are unavailable, **alternative resources** with different competencies or costs may be used, **if risks are acknowledged** and legal, regulatory or mandatory criteria are not violated.

| Tools and techniques | Outputs |
|---|---|
| Multicriteria decision analysis; **negotiation**; problem-solving; **preassignment**; virtual teams | Physical or virtual resource assignments; **project team assignments**; **resource calendars**; change requests; updates to the resource plan and cost baseline |

**Preassignment**: resources fixed in advance, for example named in a competitive proposal, or needed expertise assigned before the resource plan is complete.

> **On the exam.** A functional manager will not release the specialist the plan needs. The project manager's first move is to negotiate (show the impact, offer options such as part-time or later dates), not to escalate at once or hire someone without approval. If an alternative resource is used, record the risk.

## Managing and optimizing resources

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Resource optimization technique; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.6.2.5

The second enabler is to **manage and optimize resource needs and availability**.

**Resource optimization** adjusts start and finish dates to balance resource demand with supply:

| Technique | What it does | Effect on the critical path |
|---|---|---|
| **Resource leveling** | Moves activities when resources are **overallocated** (for example, one person on two activities at once), limited or shared, or to keep usage constant | **May change** the critical path and delay the end date |
| **Resource smoothing** | Keeps resource needs under set limits, moving activities **only within their free and total float** | **Not changed**; the end date is not delayed, but not every resource can be optimized |

**Monitor and Control Resourcing** ensures that physical and virtual resources are available **at the right time and place, in the right amount**, and **released when no longer needed**. (Team members are handled in Lead the Team.) It is concerned with:

- monitoring resource expenditures;
- identifying and dealing with **shortages and surpluses** in time;
- using and releasing resources according to the plan;
- informing stakeholders of resource issues;
- influencing the factors that change resource use, and managing actual changes.

Tools include alternatives analysis, cost-benefit analysis, performance reviews, trend analysis, problem-solving, negotiation and influencing, **value stream mapping**, continuous improvement, the **theory of constraints** and control charts. Outputs: work performance information, change requests, and updates to the resource plan and the schedule and cost baselines.

**What goes wrong without it:** critical equipment arrives late and delays the product; low-quality materials cause recalls or rework; **too much inventory** raises costs, while **too little** cannot meet demand.

## Tailoring and agile teams

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.6.3; Agile Practice Guide, 4.7.1

- Leadership and resource processes are tailored to the approach: experienced teams may be **more self-managing** and need less direction.
- Agile teams prefer **dedicated** members: part-time assignment and multitasking cost 20% to 40% of productivity (module 1.4).
- In industries where **critical resources are scarce**, resource planning and acquisition need more lead time and attention.

## Predictive and agile

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.6.3; Agile Practice Guide, 4.7.1; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Resource optimization technique; Agile Practice Guide, A3.4; Agile Practice Guide, 5.2.5

| | Predictive | Agile |
|---|---|---|
| Planning | Resource requirements per work package; resource calendars; leveling against the schedule | A stable, dedicated, cross-functional team; capacity per iteration |
| Acquiring | Negotiated with resource managers, often for set periods | Team formed once and kept together; specialists' commitments made explicit |
| Optimizing | Leveling and smoothing | Limit WIP, swarm on bottlenecks, pull work as capacity allows |

## Exam tips

- Plan resources from the scope, schedule, quality and risk requirements.
- Resource manager controls the resource; the project manager negotiates and influences.
- Leveling can extend the end date; smoothing cannot.
- Release resources when they are no longer needed, and watch for shortages and surpluses.
- Alternative resources are fine if the risk is acknowledged and no rule is broken.
