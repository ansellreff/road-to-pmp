## Key points

- The **Finance** domain covers the money side: planning, estimating, budgeting, funding, and monitoring and controlling costs, so the project **optimizes value** for the organization. Four processes: **Plan Financial Management, Estimate Costs, Develop Budget, Monitor and Control Finances**.
- **Analyze the financial needs** from the value definition, the funding sources and the financial constraints (budget, CapEx or OpEx, fiscal year).
- **Estimates get more accurate** as the project progresses: a rough order of magnitude estimate may be **−25% to +75%**, a definitive one **−5% to +10%**.
- **Contingency reserve** is for **known risks** (known-unknowns) and is usually **inside** the cost baseline. **Management reserve** is for **unknown-unknowns**, sits **outside** the cost baseline, and is usually released by **management**.
- **Cost baseline + management reserve = project budget.** Funding requirements come from the cost baseline.
- **Monitor and Control Finances** tracks spending against the cost baseline, forecasts, and sends **change requests** through governance when variances pass the thresholds.

## The Finance performance domain

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.4; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.4.1; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.4.2

The domain covers the use and allocation of monetary resources: costs, funding and, in some cases, the value proposition. **Financial measures** are used to evaluate performance against the plan, track resource use and spending, show accountability, inform stakeholders, **forecast** overruns, shortages or savings, evaluate the return on investment, and support risk management.

> The value of financial measurement is not in collecting the data, but in the **discussions and decisions** it leads to.

| Process | Focus area | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Plan Financial Management** | Planning | Defines how revenues and expenses will be estimated, budgeted, managed, monitored and controlled |
| **Estimate Costs** | Planning | Approximates the cost of the resources needed for the work |
| **Develop Budget** | Planning | Aggregates the estimates into an **authorized cost baseline** |
| **Monitor and Control Finances** | Monitoring and Controlling | Tracks spending, updates finances, manages changes to the cost baseline and revenue forecasts |

## Analyzing the financial needs

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.4.1; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.4.2.1; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Funding strategy; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Financing

The first enabler of Process task 6 is to **analyze project financial needs**.

| Concept | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Value definition** | Tangible value, often measured with **ROI, IRR, payback period, return on assets (ROA)** or ROACE; also social or environmental value; intangible value such as customer satisfaction and innovation |
| **Value maximization** | Not only cost control: the project should deliver the **most value**, aligned with strategy |
| **Funding** | Money from internal budgets, customer contracts, grants or crowdfunding; the project manager may lead or support funding work |
| **Financial constraints** | The budget is the main one, but also the **type** of money: **capital expenditure (CapEx)** for physical assets, **operational expenditure (OpEx)** for running costs; money tied to a purpose or a **fiscal year** |
| **Cost management** | Most costs come from resources. Consider **life cycle cost**: fewer design reviews may cut project cost but raise the product's operating cost |
| **Cost measurement** | Stakeholders measure cost at different moments: when committed, ordered, delivered or recorded |

**Plan Financial Management** is done once or at set points. Its tools are expert judgment, alternatives analysis and meetings; its outputs are the **financial management plan** and the **funding strategy**.

**Funding strategies:** fixed or reallocated internal budgets; **lump sum** (all at once); **incremental disbursement** (by phase or milestone); external investment; government or NGO grants. **Financing** means acquiring funding, common for long-term infrastructure and public projects; an external funder may set its own requirements.

## Estimating costs

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.4.2.2; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Cost estimates; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Analogous estimating; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Parametric estimating; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Bottom-up estimating; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Multipoint estimating; Practice Standard for Project Estimating, 2.4; PMBOK 6, 7.2

**Estimate Costs** is performed periodically. Its outputs are **cost estimates**, the **basis of estimates** and document updates.

| Technique | How it works | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| **Analogous** | Uses a previous, similar project's actual values | Quick and cheap, but less accurate; used when detail is limited |
| **Parametric** | An algorithm based on historical data and parameters (cost per square meter, hours per drawing) | As good as the data and the model |
| **Bottom-up** | Estimates the lowest-level work and **aggregates** it up the WBS | Most detailed, takes the most effort |
| **Multipoint (three-point)** | Uses optimistic, most likely and pessimistic values; triangular: **(O + M + P) / 3** | Shows the range of uncertainty |

**Example.** A data migration costs 80,000 at best, 100,000 most likely and 150,000 at worst. Triangular estimate: (80,000 + 100,000 + 150,000) / 3 = 110,000.

Other tools: reserve analysis, **cost of quality**, the PMIS and voting. Cost estimates cover all charged resources (labor, materials, equipment, services, facilities) and special categories such as **inflation**, the **cost of financing** and contingency. They consider trade-offs such as **make versus buy** and **buy versus lease**.

**Accuracy improves over time.** A rough order of magnitude (ROM) estimate at the start may be **−25% to +75%**; later, a definitive estimate may narrow to **−5% to +10%** (the Sixth Edition's figures).

> **Edition note.** The Eighth Edition gives no ranges. The Practice Standard for Project Estimating has the same ROM range but says later estimates "could narrow to a range of −10%/+15%". Remember the ROM range and the rule behind both: the range narrows as information grows.

## Quantifying risk and contingency allocations

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Reserve analysis; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.4.1; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.4.3.1

The enabler: **quantify risk and contingency financial allocations**.

**Reserve analysis** evaluates the amount of risk and the schedule and budget reserve, to decide whether the reserve is **sufficient for the remaining risk**.

- **Contingency reserves** (contingency allowances) cover **identified risks**: the **known-unknowns**. Example: some rework is expected, but not how much.
- A contingency reserve can be set at any level, from one activity to the whole project, as a **percentage**, a **fixed amount**, or by **quantitative analysis**.
- As more is known, contingency may be **used, reduced or eliminated**. It is clearly identified in the cost documents.
- Small projects with little financial flexibility need a **conservative** approach to reserves; government projects may need a **bigger buffer** because of public accountability.

## Building the budget and planning spend tracking

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.4.2.3; PMBOK 8, Guide Figure 2-25; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Cost baseline; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Project funding requirements; PMBOK 8, Guide Figure 4-1; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Cost aggregation; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Funding limit reconciliation

The enabler: **plan spend tracking throughout the project life cycle**.

**Develop Budget** aggregates the cost estimates into an **authorized cost baseline**, against which performance is monitored and controlled. Tools: **cost aggregation** (work packages roll up to **control accounts** and the project), reserve analysis, historical information review, **funding limit reconciliation** and financing. Outputs: the **cost baseline**, the **project funding requirements** and document updates.

| Layer | What it holds |
|---|---|
| **Work cost estimates** | The sum of the work package estimates |
| **+ Contingency reserve** | For known risks. In the usual case it is **inside** the cost baseline |
| **= Cost baseline** | The approved, **time-phased** budget, changed only through **formal change control** |
| **+ Management reserve** | For unknown-unknowns, **outside** the cost baseline |
| **= Project budget** | The total amount |

> Figure 2-25 shows two scenarios: reserves managed **implicitly** (contingency inside the cost baseline, as above) or **explicitly** (both reserves outside it). Organizations differ; the exam usually assumes the first.

**Example.** Work packages total 400,000, contingency reserve is 40,000 and management reserve is 30,000. Cost baseline: 400,000 + 40,000 = 440,000. Project budget: 440,000 + 30,000 = 470,000.

- **Project funding requirements** are total and periodic (quarterly, yearly) funding needs **derived from the cost baseline**. Funding often comes in **steps**, not evenly; total funds = cost baseline plus management reserve.
- **Budget at completion (BAC)** is the sum of all budgets for the work.
- **Funding limit reconciliation**: when planned spending exceeds a funding limit for a period, work is **rescheduled** to level the rate of spending, using imposed date constraints.

## Planning financial reporting

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Financial management plan; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.4.5

The enabler: **plan financial reporting**. The **financial management plan** describes how costs are planned, structured and controlled. It can set:

| Item | Example |
|---|---|
| **Units of measure** | Staff hours, meters, tons, lump sum |
| **Level of precision** | Round US$995.59 to US$1,000 |
| **Level of accuracy** | An acceptable range such as ±10% |
| **Organizational procedures links** | The **control account** in the WBS, with a code linking to the accounting system |
| **Control thresholds** | The variance allowed, as a **percentage**, before action is needed |

**Checking the outcomes (Table 2-8):** value maximization through ROI, NPV, IRR, KPIs and OKRs; spending within budget through **cost variance (CV)** and **cost performance index (CPI)**; financial visibility through **trend analysis** and forecasts. The calculations are in module 2.10.

## Anticipating future finance challenges

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.4.3; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.4.1; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.4.4

The enabler: **anticipate future finance challenges**.

- **Resource availability**: scarce people or equipment raise costs (higher wages, training, rush shipping, rental surcharges, late-delivery penalties).
- **Procurement strategy**: the contract type (fixed price or T&M) changes who carries the cost risk (module 2.8).
- **Regulation**: regulated industries may need formal financial controls, such as **SOX** or **GDPR** compliance.
- **Budget rules**: an annual budget may have to be used **within the fiscal year**; OpEx can affect the whole organization's results.
- **Forecasting**: revenue and cost forecasts warn early of overruns or shortages.
- Finance interacts with **Governance, Scope and Schedule**, and is affected by **Stakeholders** and **Risk**.

## Monitoring variations and managing reserves

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.4.2.4; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.4.1; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Variance analysis; PMBOK 6, 7.3.2.3

Two enablers: **monitor financial variations and work with the governance process**, and **manage financial reserves**.

**Monitor and Control Finances** tracks expenditures, updates financial records, adjusts the cost baseline and revenue forecasts **through change control**, and takes **corrective action**. It includes **variance analysis**: actual against planned costs.

| Tools | Outputs |
|---|---|
| Expert judgment; **earned value analysis**; **trend analysis**; **reserve analysis**; **to-complete performance index (TCPI)**; PMIS | Work performance information; **revenue and cost forecasts**; **change requests**; **funding proposals**; updates to the financial plan, cost baseline and performance measurement baseline |

| | Contingency reserve | Management reserve |
|---|---|---|
| **Covers** | Known risks with active responses (known-unknowns) | Unforeseen work within scope (unknown-unknowns) |
| **Where** | Usually **inside** the cost baseline | **Outside** the cost baseline, inside the budget |
| **When it is used** | When an identified risk occurs, through its planned response | For unforeseen work, at the discretion of **senior management**; in some organizations the project manager |
| **Effect on the baseline** | Already in the cost baseline | The amount used is **added** to the cost baseline, which needs an **approved change** |

> **On the exam.** A variance passes the control threshold: analyze the cause, forecast the impact, and raise a **change request** through governance. Do not quietly use management reserve, and do not hide the overrun.

## Predictive and agile

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.4.3; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.4.3.1; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.4.1

| | Predictive | Agile |
|---|---|---|
| Budget | A cost baseline for the whole project | Allocated per **quarter** and revised by the work achieved |
| Spending curve | Follows the planned cost baseline | **Flatter**, spread over the iterations |
| Control | Earned value against the baseline | Time-phased budgets and **continuous funding assessment** |
| Contracts | Fixed to the plan | Structured to match the iteration cadence |

## Exam tips

- Contingency reserve: known risks, inside the cost baseline. Management reserve: unknown risks, outside it, released by management.
- Cost baseline + management reserve = project budget.
- ROM −25% to +75%; definitive −5% to +10%.
- Analogous is quick but less accurate; bottom-up is the most detailed.
- A variance past the threshold goes to governance as a change request.
- In agile, budgets are often set per quarter and revised.
