## Key points

- **Quality** is the degree to which the inherent characteristics of a deliverable or process help meet or exceed the project's objectives. It is measured by **acceptance criteria**, the **definition of done**, **fitness for use** and efficiency.
- **Quality assurance** is about using the **processes** well (standards, compliance, **audits**). **Quality control** is about **deliverables** meeting specifications (design, **testing**, defects).
- The Eighth Edition has no quality domain: quality planning sits in **Plan Scope Management**, quality assurance in **Manage Quality Assurance** (Governance), quality control in **Monitor and Control Scope** and **Validate Scope**.
- **Cost of quality:** **prevention** and **appraisal** (cost of conformance) against **internal** and **external failure** (cost of nonconformance). Prevention costs less than finding defects late.
- **Control charts** show whether a process is **stable**. Control limits are statistical; **specification limits** come from the requirements.
- **Continuous improvement** (PDCA, Lean, Six Sigma, retrospectives) and **root cause analysis** keep problems from coming back.

## Quality in the Eighth Edition

@source PMBOK 8, Standard 3.5; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.5; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.2.2

The principle **"Embed quality into processes and deliverables"** asks for a consistent focus on target quality thresholds. Quality covers both the **deliverables** and the **processes** that produce them. Its dimensions:

| Dimension | The question it asks |
|---|---|
| **Performance** | Does it function as intended? |
| **Conformity** | Does it match the specifications and is it fit for use? |
| **Reliability** | Is it consistent enough to meet the desired outcomes? |
| **Resilience** | Can it cope with unforeseen failures and recover quickly? |
| **Satisfaction** | Does it bring good feedback from customers and users? |
| **Uniformity** | Is it even across similar outputs or workflows? |
| **Efficiency** | Does it give the greatest output for the least input? |
| **Sustainability** | Does it help economic, social and environmental outcomes? |
| **Compliance** | Does it meet regulations and industry and organizational standards? |

| Effort | Focus | Related concepts |
|---|---|---|
| **Quality assurance** | Using the project's **processes** effectively, following standards, to assure stakeholders | Regulations, compliance, **audits** |
| **Quality control** | **Deliverables** that meet the defined specifications and thresholds | Product design, **testing**, defects |

Where quality sits in the 40 processes: **Plan Scope Management** updates the **quality management plan** (with test and inspection planning); **Manage Quality Assurance** is in the Governance domain; **Monitor and Control Scope** measures deliverable quality; **Validate Scope** checks the processes and formalizes acceptance (module 2.5).

## Gathering quality requirements

@source PMBOK 8, Standard 3.5; PMBOK 8, Standard 3.5.2; PMBOK 7, Guide 2.6.3; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Quality metrics

The first enabler of Process task 7 is to **gather quality requirements for project deliverables**.

- A **specification** is an attribute a deliverable must have to meet a target objective. Quality is linked to the **acceptance criteria** in the charter, the **statement of work** or other key documents.
- Quality requirements may appear in the **completion criteria**, the **definition of done**, the SOW or the requirements documentation.
- Thresholds usually address scope, but can also apply to **schedule and cost** when the value depends on them.
- In experimental projects, the criteria are **regularly updated** as the project evolves.
- **Look beyond the minimum:** in the Eighth Edition's example, a shipping company entering a new market finds that its high-value customers have **stricter standards than the regulators**. Meeting those wins the market.

**Quality metrics** describe the attributes and how compliance will be checked, for example: tasks completed on time, CPI, failure rates, defects per day, downtime per month, errors per line of code, customer satisfaction scores, test coverage.

## Planning quality processes and tools

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Quality management plan; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.2.2.1; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Checklists; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Cause-and-effect diagram; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Benchmarking; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Flowchart; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Affinity diagram; PMBOK 8, Glossary (histogram)

The enabler: **plan quality processes and tools**.

The **quality management plan** describes how the organization's policies, procedures and guidelines will be applied to reach the quality objectives. It may be formal or informal, and is **reviewed early**. It may include:

- the **quality standards** and **quality objectives**;
- quality **roles and responsibilities**;
- the deliverables and processes subject to **quality reviews**;
- the **quality control** and quality management activities planned;
- the **tools and methods**, with procedures for **nonconformance** and **continuous improvement**.

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Checklists** | Lists of items to check, built from history; quick, but never exhaustive |
| **Cause-and-effect (fishbone, Ishikawa) diagram** | Traces an effect back to its root cause, branch by branch |
| **Flowchart** | Maps the steps and branches of a process, to find where to improve |
| **Affinity diagram** | Groups many ideas into categories |
| **Histogram** | A bar chart of numerical data |
| **Control chart** | Shows whether a process is stable (see below) |
| **Benchmarking** | Compares products, processes and practices with comparable organizations, for ideas and a basis for measurement |

## Executing the quality management plan

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.5; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Audits; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Quality reports

The enabler: **execute a quality management plan**.

**Manage Quality Assurance** ensures that project processes are performed in a way consistent with stakeholder expectations. It turns the project management plan into **executable activities** that include the organization's standards, regulations and policies, and is performed **throughout the project**.

| Tools and techniques | Outputs |
|---|---|
| **Audits**; checklists; affinity diagrams, **cause-and-effect diagrams**, flowcharts; decision-making; problem-solving; **process improvement** | **Quality reports**; change requests; plan and document updates |

It builds **confidence** that outputs will meet requirements (through **quality audits** and **failure analysis**), improves process efficiency, and helps the project be governed properly. In **predictive** projects it is usually **formal**; in **agile** projects it may be formal or informal. It may be done by the project team, an external entity, or both.

A **quality audit** is usually done by people **outside the project** (internal audit, the PMO, an external auditor). It identifies good practices, **nonconformities and gaps**, shares good practices from similar projects, offers help to improve processes, and adds to the **lessons learned** repository.

A **quality report** contains quality issues, **recommended corrective actions** and a summary of quality control findings.

## Quality control and ongoing quality reviews

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.2.2.5; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.2.2.6; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Testing/product evaluations; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Control charts; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Quality control measurements

The enabler: **conduct ongoing quality reviews**.

**Monitor and Control Scope** measures the quality of the deliverables. Its tools include variance, trend and **root cause analysis**, performance reviews, **audits and inspections**, and **testing/product evaluations**. Outputs: **verified deliverables**, quality reports, **quality control measurements**, change requests. **Validate Scope** then turns verified deliverables into **accepted** deliverables (module 2.5).

- **Testing** gives objective information about quality: it finds errors, defects and nonconformance. **Early testing** reduces the cost of fixing problems.
- **Control charts** show whether a process is **stable** or predictable:
  - **specification limits** (upper and lower) come from the **requirements**: the maximum and minimum allowed;
  - **control limits** are **calculated statistically** and show the natural capability of a stable process;
  - a point outside the control limits calls for **corrective action**, even if the part is within specification.
- Control charts can also track cost and schedule variances or the frequency of scope changes.

> **On the exam.** "Verified" (checked by the team against the requirements) comes before "accepted" (formally accepted by the customer or sponsor).

## Regulatory compliance

@source PMBOK 8, Standard 3.5; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.5; PMBOK 8, Standard 3.5.2

The enabler: **help ensure regulatory compliance**.

- **Compliance** is a quality dimension: deliverables and processes meet **regulations** and industry and organizational **standards**.
- Quality assurance turns the plan into activities that include the organization's **standards, regulations and policies**; **audits** check that this is happening.
- Compliance is the minimum. Stakeholders' own standards may be stricter, and meeting them adds value.
- Compliance as a whole (security, safety, regulation, sustainability) is taught in module 3.2.

## Cost of quality and sustainability

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Cost of quality (CoQ); PMBOK 8, Guide Figure 5-3; PMBOK 7, Guide 2.6.3.1; PMBOK 8, Standard 3.5.1

The enabler: **manage cost of quality (CoQ) and sustainability**.

| | Category | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Cost of conformance** (spent to avoid failures) | **Prevention**: build a quality product | Training, documented processes, equipment, time to do it right, quality planning |
| | **Appraisal**: assess the quality | Testing, destructive testing loss, inspections, quality audits, supplier rating |
| **Cost of nonconformance** (spent because of failures) | **Internal failure**: found by the project | Rework, scrap, failure analysis |
| | **External failure**: found by the customer | Liabilities, **warranty work**, complaints, returns, **lost business**, reputation |

- The **optimal** CoQ balances prevention and appraisal against failure: past a point, more prevention is not cost-effective.
- **Early inspection and review** are good investments; trying to "test quality in" late usually fails, because scrap and rework are expensive.
- **Sustainability:** less rework means less waste, fewer emissions and less unnecessary use of natural resources. **Sustainability** is also one of the quality dimensions.

## Implementing continuous improvement

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Continuous improvement; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Process improvement; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Root cause analysis; PMBOK 8, Standard 3.5; Agile Practice Guide, 5.2.7

The enabler: **implement continuous improvement**.

- **Continuous improvement** finds, analyzes and implements **incremental** improvements to processes, products and services. Approaches: **plan-do-check-act (PDCA)**, **Lean**, **Total Quality Management**, **Six Sigma**, the **Five Focusing Steps**.
- **Process improvement** analyzes current processes and changes them to cut waste and raise value.
- **Root cause analysis** finds the basic reason for a variance, defect or risk. When **all root causes** are removed, the problem does not recur.
- Continuous improvement and **waste elimination** are foundational to embedding quality.
- Agile teams improve through **retrospectives** at a regular cadence.

> **On the exam.** A defect keeps coming back: find the **root cause** and fix the **process**, not just the defect. That is quality assurance at work.

## Quality in agile teams

@source Agile Practice Guide, 5.2.9; Agile Practice Guide, 5.2.8; Agile Practice Guide, Software-Centric Approaches and Metrics for Agile Teams; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.5

"If the team does not pay attention to quality, it will soon become impossible to release anything rapidly."

| Practice | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Definition of done** | Shared criteria every increment must meet; the makers keep it achievable |
| **Continuous integration** | Frequent incorporation of work into the whole, then retesting |
| **Testing at all levels** | Unit, integration, system, smoke and regression tests; a strong preference for **automated** tests |
| **Acceptance-test-driven development (ATDD)** | The team agrees the acceptance criteria and writes the tests first |
| **Test-driven and behavior-driven development (TDD, BDD)** | Automated tests written **before** the product, to design and mistake-proof it |
| **Spikes** | Timeboxed research to learn a critical technical or functional element |

## Predictive and agile

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.5; PMBOK 8, Standard 3.5.1; Agile Practice Guide, 5.2.9

| | Predictive | Agile |
|---|---|---|
| Quality requirements | Specifications and acceptance criteria up front | Acceptance criteria per story and a definition of done, refined over time |
| Quality assurance | Usually **formal**, with audits | Formal or informal; retrospectives |
| Quality control | Inspections and tests of deliverables, often at set points | **Built in**: continuous integration and automated tests every iteration |
| Goal | The same either way: meet the objectives and avoid rework, whether requirements are defined up front or elaborated over time | |

## Exam tips

- Quality assurance checks the process; quality control checks the deliverable.
- Prevention is cheaper than inspection; inspection is cheaper than a customer finding the defect.
- A point outside the control limits needs action even if it is within specification.
- Verified deliverables come from quality control; accepted deliverables from Validate Scope.
- A recurring defect calls for root cause analysis and a process fix.
- In agile, quality is built in with the definition of done and continuous testing.
