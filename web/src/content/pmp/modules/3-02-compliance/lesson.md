## Key points

- **Compliance** means the project and its deliverables meet the **laws, regulations, standards, policies and contracts** that apply: security and data privacy, health and safety, sustainability, regulatory and industry rules.
- **Confirm** the requirements early with the people who know them (legal, compliance officers, auditors, regulators), and **classify** them, for example by subject and by source (external or internal).
- **Threats** include changing regulations, underestimated requirements, missing expertise, suppliers, shortcuts under pressure and careless use of data.
- **Support** compliance with requirements and traceability, the quality management plan, audits and checklists, risk responses and, in agile, a **definition of done** that includes compliance.
- **Noncompliance** can stop the project, bring litigation and failure costs, and damage reputation; individuals can face discipline under the PMI Code.
- **Measure** compliance throughout planning, execution and closure, with KPIs, audits and a final check of all obligations.

## Confirming compliance requirements

@source PMBOK 8, Standard 1.3.2; PMBOK 8, Standard 2.2.1; PMBOK 8, Standard 3.5; PMI Code of Ethics, 2.3; The PMI Guide to Business Analysis, 3.3.3.1

The first enabler of Business Environment task 2 is to **confirm project compliance requirements (e.g., security, health and safety, sustainability, regulatory compliance)**.

- **Organizational governance** enforces **legal, regulatory and compliance** requirements and defines **ethical, social and environmental** responsibilities. Projects inherit them.
- **External enterprise environmental factors** bring many of them: **legal restrictions** (security, data protection, business conduct, employment, procurement), **government or industry standards** (products, environment, safety, quality, workmanship), and **public health and safety regulations**.
- **Compliance** is a quality dimension: deliverables and processes comply with **regulations, industry standards and organizational standards**.
- The PMI Code requires us to **inform ourselves of, and uphold**, the policies, rules, regulations and laws that govern our work.
- **Who knows the requirements:** governance roles such as legal, risk, release management, change control boards and **compliance auditors or officers** give insight into regulations, auditing obligations, business rules and required processes. Involve them **early**.

## Classifying compliance categories

@source PMBOK 8, Standard 2.2.1; PMBOK 8, Standard 2.2.3; PMBOK 8, Standard 3.7; PMBOK 8, Appendix X3.3; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.5.4

The enabler: **classify compliance categories**. Classifying shows who owns each requirement, how it is checked, and how serious a breach would be.

| Category | Examples | Typical source |
|---|---|---|
| **Security and data privacy** | Data protection laws, information security, privacy of personal data (also data used by AI tools) | External laws; internal security policies |
| **Health and safety** | Workplace safety, public health and safety regulations, product safety | External regulations |
| **Sustainability and environment** | Environmental regulations, sustainability KPIs, social and governance commitments | External regulations; organizational strategy |
| **Regulatory and legal** | Industry regulation, employment and procurement law, licenses and permits | External: regulators, laws |
| **Standards and quality** | Industry standards, quality standards, workmanship | External standards bodies; internal quality policy |
| **Organizational policy** | Policies, procedures, templates, change control, procurement rules | Internal OPAs |
| **Contractual** | Terms of agreements, service levels, warranties | Contracts with customers and suppliers |

**By source:** **external** requirements come from enterprise environmental factors (laws, regulators, standards) and cannot be changed by the organization; **internal** requirements come from organizational process assets and change only through organizational policy.

## Threats to compliance

@source PMBOK 8, Standard 3.7; Risk Management: A Practice Guide, 7.2.2; Risk Management: A Practice Guide, 7.3.3; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.6; PMBOK 8, Appendix X3.3

The enabler: **determine potential threats to compliance**.

| Threat | Example |
|---|---|
| **Underestimated requirements** | Underestimating regulatory compliance tied to sustainability is a named **sustainability risk** |
| **Changing rules** | New environmental standards update the compliance requirements in the middle of the project |
| **Missing expertise** | No one on the team has **regulatory compliance competence**; external experts may be needed |
| **Schedule pressure** | Delays cause a license or permit to lapse; tests are skipped to save time |
| **Suppliers and partners** | Contracted work that does not meet the same rules |
| **Data and AI** | Sensitive data exposed through AI tools; data used in ways that violate privacy laws |
| **Cost over stewardship** | Choosing the cheapest materials and harming the environment or people's health |

These threats are **risks**: put them in the **risk register** with owners and responses (modules 3.5 and 3.6).

## Methods to support compliance

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.5; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Audits; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Checklists; Agile Practice Guide, 5.4; Agile Practice Guide, X2.3.1; PMBOK 8, Appendix X3.3

The enabler: **use methods to support compliance**.

- **Requirements and traceability:** write compliance needs as requirements with acceptance criteria and trace them to deliverables and tests (module 2.5).
- **Quality assurance:** turn the plan into activities that include the organization's **standards, regulations and policies**; **audits** check that the project follows them (module 2.11).
- **Checklists** built from past projects and regulations; remember that no checklist is complete.
- **Experts:** include legal, security and compliance people, or external consultants, in planning and reviews.
- **Agile:** bring **audit, security and compliance roles into backlog reviews early**; replace large gate reviews with **small, frequent check-ins** on completed increments; use concise checklists and **automate evidence capture**. The **definition of done** may include documentation and **regulatory compliance being complete**.
- **Data:** secure sensitive data and collect it under a **privacy policy**; a **human** stays accountable for decisions supported by AI.

## Consequences of noncompliance

@source PMBOK 8, Standard 1.3.1; PMBOK 8, Standard 3.7; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Cost of quality (CoQ); PMI Code of Ethics, 1.5

The enabler: **analyze the consequences of noncompliance**.

| Consequence | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **The project ends** | A project may be **terminated for legal, regulatory or compliance issues** |
| **Rework and failure costs** | Fixing nonconforming work, recalls, **liabilities** and warranty work are failure costs |
| **Litigation** | Compliance reduces **litigation costs**; noncompliance raises them |
| **Reputation** | Sustainability and compliance failures cause stakeholder dissatisfaction and **long-term harm to reputation** |
| **Lost value** | Benefits are delayed or never realized; a product may not be allowed on the market |
| **Personal consequences** | Breaking the Code's **mandatory standards** leads to PMI's Ethics Review Committee |

Analyze each requirement's consequence like a risk: how likely is a breach, and how severe would it be? The more severe, the more effort it deserves.

## Approach and actions for compliance needs

@source PMBOK 8, Standard 3.7; PMBOK 8, Standard 3.7.1; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.2.5; Risk Management: A Practice Guide, 7.3.3

The enabler: **determine the necessary approach and action(s) to address compliance needs**.

- **Put compliance into the formal documents:** sustainability and compliance goals and KPIs belong in the **business case, charter, scope statement and contracts**, confirmed by the **sponsor**. If they are missing, the project manager still raises them.
- **Plan the work:** include compliance activities in the **WBS or backlog** (for example, assessing CO2 emissions or protecting local biodiversity).
- **Respond to risks:** when the wind turbine project's environmental compliance was threatened by a schedule delay and new standards, the team bought a **temporary license** and then revisited the risk to make sure it was under control.
- **Choose the stronger response** for negative impacts, following the **sustainability pyramid**, from most to least desirable: **avoid** them altogether, **minimize** them, **restore** their impacts, **compensate or offset** them.
- **Go beyond the minimum** when it adds value: compliance-oriented project management looks only at the rules; sustainability-oriented project management considers the community, society and future generations.

## Measuring compliance

@source PMBOK 8, Standard 3.7.1; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.9.1; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Quality metrics; PMBOK 8, Appendix X3.3; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.2.5

The enabler: **measure the extent to which the project is in compliance**.

- Compliance is **monitored during planning, execution and closure**, not only at the end.
- Use **measurable criteria**: sustainability-related **KPIs** in the charter or contracts, **quality metrics** such as the percentage of requirements covered by tests, audit findings and open nonconformities.
- **Audits** (internal, PMO or external) report nonconformities and gaps; **inspections and tests** check deliverables.
- AI tools can automate **compliance checks** and compliance reporting, with a human checking the results.
- At **closure**, verify that all **contractual, regulatory, legal, social and environmental obligations** have been met (module 2.15).

## Predictive and agile

@source Agile Practice Guide, X2.3.1; Agile Practice Guide, 5.4; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.5

| | Predictive | Agile |
|---|---|---|
| Requirements | Defined up front, traced through the plan | Compliance stories and acceptance criteria; part of the definition of done |
| Assurance | Formal audits and gate reviews | Compliance roles in backlog reviews; small, frequent check-ins on increments |
| Evidence | Documents produced for each gate | Evidence captured continuously, automated where practical |
| Rigor | Usually formal | A **continuum of practices**: add rigor where regulation demands it while keeping agile collaboration |

## Exam tips

- Identify compliance requirements early, with legal, security and compliance experts.
- External rules (laws, regulators) cannot be negotiated away; internal policies change only through the organization.
- Treat compliance threats as risks with owners and responses.
- Noncompliance can end the project; never skip a required check to save time.
- In agile, put compliance into the definition of done and involve auditors early.
- Verify all obligations at closure.
