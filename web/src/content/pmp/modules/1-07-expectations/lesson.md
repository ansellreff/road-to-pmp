## Key points

- To **align expectations**, bring the people who disagree together and **facilitate**: effective participation, mutual understanding, every contribution considered, buy-in by the agreed decision process, and follow-up on what was agreed.
- **Mentoring opportunities** come from mixing people of different seniority and expertise; team performance assessments and the team's own uncertainty show where they are needed.
- **Internal customers** sit inside the performing organization, **external customers** outside it. Both have expectations the project must find, align and keep meeting.
- **Value** is the ultimate indicator of success. Focus on the **outcome** a customer needs, not only the deliverable they asked for.
- **Quality** means satisfying stakeholders' stated or implied needs, at or above expectations; it is measured against **acceptance criteria**.
- **Monitor satisfaction** continuously, with surveys, Net Promoter Score, pulse surveys, feedback on increments and customer satisfaction scores, and **respond** when it drops.

## Facilitating discussions to align expectations

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Facilitation; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Brainstorming; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Nominal group; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Multicriteria decision analysis; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Voting

People task 5's third enabler is to **facilitate discussions to align expectations**. Module 1.5 found each stakeholder's expectations; when they differ, the project manager brings the people together.

**Facilitation** is the ability to guide a group event to a successful decision, solution or conclusion. A facilitator makes sure that:

1. there is **effective participation**;
2. participants reach **mutual understanding**;
3. **all contributions are considered**;
4. conclusions have **full buy-in** according to the decision process established for the project;
5. actions and agreements are **followed up** afterward.

**Tools for the discussion:**

| Tool | Use when aligning expectations |
|---|---|
| **Brainstorming** | Generate options quickly, then analyze them. Led by a facilitator. Can be virtual (brain-netting) |
| **Nominal group technique** | Large groups and **controversial** topics: gathers everyone's input systematically, **equalizes participation** and limits the dominance of vocal members before any discussion |
| **Multicriteria decision analysis** | Weighted criteria in a decision matrix to score and rank alternatives objectively |
| **Voting** | **Unanimity** (everyone agrees), **majority** (more than 50%; an uneven group size avoids ties), **plurality** (the largest block decides, used with more than two options) |

> **On the exam.** Two departments expect different things from the same deliverable. The project manager does not choose for them, and does not tell each what it wants to hear. The best answer brings both together in a facilitated session, with the project's objectives and value as the yardstick, and records what was agreed.

## Organizing and acting on mentoring opportunities

@source PMBOK 8, Standard 2.5.1.2; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Coaching and mentoring; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Team performance assessments; PMBOK 8, Guide Table 3-1; Agile Practice Guide, 4.3.1

People task 5's last enabler is to **organize and act on mentoring opportunities**. (What coaching and mentoring are was covered in module 1.4.)

**Where opportunities come from:**

- **Mixing seniority and expertise.** By strategically mixing team members of different seniority levels and expertise, the project management team creates an exchange of knowledge and skills; this mix "provides **mentorship and coaching opportunities** that nurture less-experienced team members" (competency development).
- **Team performance assessments.** Evaluating the team's effectiveness shows the specific training, coaching, **mentoring**, assistance or changes required.
- **Uncertainty in the team.** When team members are unsure how to proceed, the Eighth Edition's tailoring advice is to assess their needs and choose the right support: guidance, **mentorship**, training and knowledge-sharing practices.

**Acting on them:**

- **Integrate** competency development into the project's workflow, identifying individual learning needs.
- Create opportunities for people to take on **challenges that stretch their skills**, with constructive feedback.
- Responsibility for growth may also lie with functional managers, PMOs, line managers, or the individuals themselves; the project management team still facilitates it.
- A supportive leader **builds the team's capabilities through mentoring**, encouragement and support.
- Coaching and mentoring can also be offered to **stakeholders**, not only to team members: the Eighth Edition's definition of coaching names "a project team member or a stakeholder".

## Internal and external customers

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.5; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.5.1; Agile Practice Guide, 5.5.3

People task 6 is to **manage stakeholder expectations**, and its enablers speak of **internal and external customers**. The Eighth Edition counts stakeholders inside and outside the organization; customers are among them.

| | Who they are | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Internal customers** | People or units **inside the performing organization** who receive or use what the project delivers | The sales department that will use a new system; the operations team that will run it |
| **External customers** | Those **outside the organization**: the client who pays, and the people who buy or use the product | A client company under contract; members of the public using an app |

Both have expectations, and both count for satisfaction. The Agile Practice Guide, for example, suggests pulse surveys of **end users or internal customers**.

**Identifying their expectations** (the first enabler of task 6) uses the same work as module 1.5: the stakeholder register's assessment information, interviews, personas, and continuous communication. Stakeholder satisfaction should be **prioritized and integrated into the project objectives**.

## Aligning and maintaining outcomes

@source PMBOK 8, Standard 3.4; PMBOK 8, Standard 3.4.1; PMBOK 8, Standard 3.4.2; PMBOK 8, Standard 3.5; Agile Practice Guide, 3.9.2

The second enabler is to **align and maintain outcomes** with internal and external customer expectations. The Eighth Edition's principles give the yardstick.

**Focus on value.**

- **Value** is "the ultimate success indicator and driver of projects": the overall worth of the outcomes and the net benefits to stakeholders, such as financial returns, social benefits, or **the customer's perceived benefits**.
- **Outcomes, not just deliverables.** A customer may ask for software because they believe it will raise productivity. The software is the **output**; it does not raise productivity by itself. Adding a deliverable such as **training** can enable the higher-value outcome. Everyone should understand both the deliverable and the **intended outcome**.
- Desired outcomes are **assessed iteratively** with quality gates, **feedback loops** and regular reviews. If the project is unlikely to deliver the intended value, it may be best to terminate it.
- **In action:** a value-focused team might deliver **fewer features** that match a culture which values simplicity, raising usage and satisfaction, rather than the solution with the most features.

**Embed quality.**

- Project quality means satisfying stakeholders' **stated or implied needs**, **at or above expectations**, and fulfilling project and product requirements.
- Quality is linked to **acceptance criteria** (in the charter, statement of work or other documents). Where the project evolves through experimentation, the criteria are refined as it goes, and validated during acceptance.
- One of the quality dimensions is **satisfaction**: do the deliverables and processes earn valuable feedback from customers and end users, including usability and user experience?

**Agile delivery.** The Agile Manifesto's principles put it simply: **customer satisfaction increases with early and continuous delivery** of valuable products, and a working increment is the primary measure of progress.

> **On the exam.** A customer insists on a feature list that will not achieve what they actually need. The answer is to work with them on the **outcome** and the value, not to deliver the list silently, and not to refuse it. Changes that follow go through the project's change process.

## Monitoring satisfaction and responding

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.5.5; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Quality metrics; Agile Practice Guide, 5.5.3; Agile Practice Guide, 6.5.4

The third enabler is to **monitor internal and external customer satisfaction and expectations, and respond as needed**.

| Measure | Source |
|---|---|
| Feedback through **interviews or surveys**; periodic alignment with stakeholders on objectives; the **Net Promoter Score** | Check of the Stakeholders domain (Table 2-9) |
| **Customer satisfaction scores** as a quality metric | Quality metrics |
| **Pulse surveys** of end users or internal customers; customer satisfaction or NPS linked to delivery data | Agile Practice Guide, metrics that capture value and outcomes |
| Surveys, NPS or **usage patterns**, reviewed regularly so the organization can adapt | Agile Practice Guide, portfolio feedback loops |
| The number of **change requests to requirements**; feedback on increments | Table 2-9, agreement with objectives |

**Responding as needed:** when satisfaction falls or expectations shift, find the cause with the customer, then act through the normal routes: reprioritize the backlog, raise a change request, adjust the engagement or communication plan (module 1.6), or reset expectations with facilitated discussion.

## Predictive and agile

@source PMBOK 8, Standard 3.4; PMBOK 8, Standard 3.5; Agile Practice Guide, 3.9.2; Agile Practice Guide, 5.2.6

| | Predictive | Agile |
|---|---|---|
| Where expectations are fixed | Acceptance criteria in the charter, statement of work or other key documents | Acceptance criteria refined as the product evolves through experimentation |
| How satisfaction is checked | Quality gates and regular reviews against the business case | Reviews of working product at least every two weeks; the product owner accepts or declines |
| How outcomes stay aligned | Alignment checked against baselines, the business case and intended outcomes | Early and continuous delivery; the team works with the customer on which features are worth the investment |

## Exam tips

- Different expectations? Facilitate a discussion with everyone concerned, using value and objectives as the yardstick.
- Mentoring: mix seniority, use assessments to find needs, act on them as part of the work.
- Customers are internal and external; both expectations count.
- Deliver the outcome, not just the output.
- Measure satisfaction continuously and respond through the normal routes, never by hiding problems.
