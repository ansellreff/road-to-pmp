## Key points

- Projects operate inside the organization's internal environment, which sits inside a larger **external environment**: the economy, competition, laws, technology, society and the physical world.
- External factors are **enterprise environmental factors** the project cannot control: **marketplace** conditions, social and cultural influences, **legal** restrictions, standards, **financial** conditions (exchange rates, inflation, tariffs), **physical and geopolitical** constraints, **emerging technologies**, public health rules.
- **Survey** changes with structured prompts such as **PESTLE**, **TECOP** or **VUCA**, SWOT's external side, stakeholders and data tools.
- **Assess and prioritize** each change's impact on scope or the backlog: is it a threat, an opportunity or an issue? Predictive projects use **change requests**; adaptive projects add and **reprioritize backlog items**.
- **Review continually**: in dynamic environments, build in **iterative feedback** so scope can adjust to real-time external inputs.
- If the need for the project disappears because of an external change, the project may be **ended**.

## The project in its environment

@source PMBOK 8, Standard 2.1.1; PMBOK 8, Standard 2.2; PMBOK 8, Standard 2.2.1; PMBOK 7, Standard 2.4.2

- A system for value delivery is part of the organization's **internal environment** (policies, governance, methodologies), which exists within the larger **external environment**: the economy, the competitive environment, legislative constraints and more.
- Internal and external environments can have a **favorable, unfavorable or neutral** effect on project characteristics, stakeholders and teams.
- **Enterprise environmental factors** are conditions not directly influenced by the team that impact, constrain or direct the project.

| External factor | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Marketplace conditions** | Competitors, customer behavior, market share, brand recognition, technology trends |
| **Social and cultural influences** | Political climate, codes of conduct, ethics, perceptions |
| **Legal restrictions** | Laws on security, data protection, business conduct, employment, procurement |
| **Academic research** | Industry studies, benchmarking results, emerging trends |
| **Government or industry standards** | Regulations on products, environment, safety, quality, workmanship |
| **Financial considerations** | Currency exchange rates, interest rates, inflation, tariffs, taxes |
| **Physical environment** | Working conditions, weather, **geopolitical** constraints |
| **Emerging technologies** | AI, automation, blockchain, the Internet of Things |
| **Public health and safety** | Health protocols, travel restrictions, safety mandates |

## Surveying changes in the external environment

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Prompt lists; The PMI Guide to Business Analysis, 4.2.3.1; Risk Management: A Practice Guide, 3.2.2; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.7.4; Agile Practice Guide, 6.1.1

The first enabler of Business Environment task 8 is to **survey changes to the external business environment (e.g., regulations, technology, geopolitical, market)**.

| Way to survey | How it helps |
|---|---|
| **PESTLE** prompt list | Political, economic, sociocultural, technological, legal, environmental sources of change |
| **TECOP** and **VUCA** | Technical, environmental, commercial, operational, political; volatility, uncertainty, complexity, ambiguity |
| **SWOT, external side** | **Opportunities** in the external environment to seize, and **threats** in the market that could impede success |
| **Stakeholders** | Critical sources of information about risks and uncertainty, including regulators, customers and suppliers |
| **Data and AI tools** | Analysis of market trends and historical data to inform decisions |

External factors named by the risk practice guide include **regulatory requirements or mandates, market conditions, supply chain disruptions, cyberattacks, geopolitical events and environmental risks**. Strategic drivers of change include **market and competitive pressure**, **compliance and policy mandates**, and **technology shifts**.

## Assessing and prioritizing the impact on scope or backlog

@source PMBOK 8, Guide Figure 2-11; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.7.1; PMBOK 8, Standard 1.3.1; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.1; Agile Practice Guide, 5.2.1; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.8.2

The enabler: **assess and prioritize the impact on project scope or backlog based on changes in the external business environment**.

1. **Classify the change.** Is it a **threat** or an **opportunity** that might happen (risk register), or has it already happened (**issue**)?
2. **Analyze the impact** across scope, finance, schedule, resources, stakeholders and risk.
3. **Check the value proposition.** Does the business case still hold? Governance may decide to continue, **replan**, **expand or contract**, or **end the project**: a project usually ends when, due to changes in strategy, priorities or the **external environment**, the need for it no longer exists.
4. **Act through the right channel:**

| Predictive | Adaptive |
|---|---|
| Raise a **change request** with the impact analysis; the CCB or designated person decides | Add the change to the **backlog** as an item; impact analysis sets its **priority** |
| Update baselines, plans and the risk register when approved | The product owner **replans the roadmap** with the emerging information |

**Prioritizing techniques** for backlog changes include **cost of delay** (time-sensitivity and economic impact), **WSJF**, MoSCoW and value versus effort (module 2.6). A new regulation with a fixed deadline often has a high cost of delay.

> **On the exam.** A new law affects the product halfway through: analyze the impact and act through change control or the backlog. Do not ignore it, and do not change the scope without the right decision.

## Continually reviewing the environment

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.2.3; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.7.2.2; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.7.5; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.7.3; Agile Practice Guide, 5.2.1

The enabler: **continually review the external business environment for impacts on project scope or backlog**.

- **Environmental dynamics:** where market volatility, technology and customer preferences change rapidly, scope management should be **flexible**, with **iterative feedback mechanisms** that allow scope adjustments in response to **real-time external inputs**.
- **Risk identification is iterative**: new risks from the environment are identified as the project evolves; risk reviews check existing risks and find new ones.
- **Check result (Table 2-11):** the team is **aware of the environment** (technical, social, political, market and economic contexts) and incorporates it when evaluating uncertainty, risks and responses.
- **Resilience planning:** prepare for major disruptions with **scenario planning**, aligned with the organization's business continuity or emergency response plan.
- In agile, the **roadmap is replanned** regularly, so external changes reach the backlog quickly.

## Predictive and agile

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.2.3; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.8.2; Agile Practice Guide, 5.2.1

| | Predictive | Agile |
|---|---|---|
| Survey | At planning and set review points; risk workshops | Continuously; refinement, reviews, roadmap updates |
| Response | Change requests, baseline updates | Backlog items, reprioritization |
| Speed of reaction | Slower, controlled | Faster, through short feedback loops |

## Exam tips

- External factors are EEFs: the project cannot control them but must respond.
- Use PESTLE-style prompts to survey; stakeholders are sources too.
- Classify a change as threat, opportunity or issue, then analyze its impact.
- Predictive: change request. Agile: backlog item and reprioritization.
- Review the environment continually, not just at the start.
- If the external need disappears, the project may end.
