## Key points

- **Monitor and Control Project Performance** tracks, reviews and reports overall progress against the plan, and gives stakeholders **forecasts** of cost and schedule. Changes to a baseline go through **integrated change control**.
- **Leading indicators** warn early (items in progress, unengaged stakeholders); **lagging indicators** report after the fact (deliverables completed, variances). Metrics should be **SMART** and **actionable**.
- Watch the **measurement pitfalls**: the Hawthorne effect, vanity metrics, demoralization, misuse, confirmation bias, correlation mistaken for causation.
- An **artifact** is a template, document, output or deliverable. **Tailor** the artifacts to the project, keep them **current** under **configuration management**, and make them **accessible**.
- Show status with **dashboards** (often red-amber-green), **information radiators** and **visual controls**. Adapt reports to each audience.
- Act **before** a threshold is crossed when a trend shows it coming. **Stop** collecting metrics that no longer influence decisions.

## Monitoring and controlling project performance

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.7; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Work performance reports

The enabler of Process task 9: **assess current progress**.

**Monitor and Control Project Performance** tracks, reviews and reports overall progress against the performance objectives in the project management plan. It lets stakeholders see the **current state**, the actions taken on performance issues, and the **future** status through cost and schedule forecasts.

- **Monitoring**: collecting, measuring and assessing data and trends.
- **Controlling**: deciding **corrective or preventive actions**, replanning, and following up on them.
- If a decision would change a **baseline**, use **integrated change control**.

It involves evaluating performance against the plan, tracking resources, work and budget, showing accountability, informing stakeholders, checking that deliverables are on track to deliver the **planned benefits**, discussing trade-offs, threats and opportunities, monitoring risks, and ensuring deliverables will meet the **acceptance criteria**.

| Tools and techniques | Outputs |
|---|---|
| Expert judgment; alternatives, cost-benefit, **earned value**, **root cause**, **trend** and **variance** analysis; voting; meetings; **project dashboards**; **visual controls**; **information radiators** | **Work performance reports**; change requests; updates to the plan and documents (cost and schedule forecasts, issue log, lessons learned, risk register) |

## Developing project metrics

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.5.1; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.5.2; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.5.3; PMBOK 7, Guide 2.7.1; PMBOK 7, Guide 2.7.2; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.7

Enablers: **develop project metrics, analysis and reconciliation**, and **measure, analyze and update project metrics**.

| Indicator | What it does | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Leading** | Predicts changes or trends, so the team can act **before** a threshold is crossed | Size of the project, items in progress in the backlog; harder to quantify: no risk process, stakeholders not engaged, poorly defined success criteria |
| **Lagging** | Measures deliverables or events **after** the fact; easier to measure | Deliverables completed, schedule or cost variance, resources consumed |

- **Key performance indicators (KPIs)** are quantifiable measures of project success. They are useful only when they are **discussed and acted on**.
- **SMART** metrics are **specific, measurable, achievable, realistic and time-bound**.
- Measurement costs time, so measure **only what is relevant** and make every metric **actionable**.
- **What to measure** (a balanced set): deliverable metrics (defects, performance, technical performance), delivery (WIP, lead time, cycle time), **baseline performance** (schedule and cost), resources, **business value**, stakeholders (satisfaction, mood), and **forecasts**.
- **Reconciliation:** compare measures with each other and with the baselines. A lagging schedule variance may correlate with team dissatisfaction, pointing to a **root cause** that schedule status alone would hide.

## Measurement pitfalls

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.7.1

| Pitfall | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Hawthorne effect** | Measuring something changes behavior: counting only deliverables encourages volume over customer satisfaction |
| **Vanity metric** | Data that does not help decisions: page views rather than new viewers |
| **Demoralization** | Unachievable targets lower morale as the team keeps failing |
| **Misusing the metrics** | Focusing on less important or short-term measures, or doing easy out-of-sequence work to look good |
| **Confirmation bias** | Seeing only the data that supports what we already believe |
| **Correlation versus causation** | Assuming one variable causes another because they move together |

## Thresholds and troubleshooting

@source PMBOK 7, Guide 2.7.5; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Configuration management plan

- Agree **plans for measures outside their thresholds** in advance: schedule, budget, velocity or other measures. The width of a threshold follows the stakeholders' **risk tolerance**.
- Example: a spend threshold at +10% and −20% of the planned rate. When actual spend passes +10%, the **exception plan** starts.
- **Do not wait** for a breach. If a trend or new information shows one coming, act **proactively**.

## Identifying and tailoring artifacts

@source PMBOK 7, Guide 4.6; PMBOK 7, Guide 4.7; PMBOK 7, Guide 3.3.5

The enabler: **identify and tailor needed artifacts**.

An **artifact** is a template, document, output or project deliverable. The project manager and team **select and tailor** the artifacts for their project, based on the delivery approach, the product and the organization.

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Strategy artifacts** | Business case, project charter, vision statement, roadmap |
| **Logs and registers** | Assumption log, backlog, change log, issue log, lessons learned register, risk register, stakeholder register |
| **Plans** | Project management plan and its subsidiary plans, iteration plan, release plan, test plan |
| **Hierarchy charts** | Work, organizational, product, resource and risk breakdown structures |
| **Baselines** | Budget, milestone schedule, project schedule, scope baseline, performance measurement baseline |
| **Visual data and information** | Burn charts, dashboards, information radiators, Gantt charts, cycle time and throughput charts, S-curves, story maps |
| **Reports** | Status reports, quality reports, risk reports |
| **Agreements and contracts** | Fixed-price and cost-reimbursable contracts, other agreements |

Tailoring the **documents, templates and other artifacts** makes sure they suit the project and the organization: no artifact for its own sake.

## Keeping artifacts current and documented

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Configuration management plan; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.7; Agile Practice Guide, 5.5.4

Two enablers: **help ensure artifacts are created, reviewed, updated and documented**, and **continually assess the effectiveness of artifact management**.

- The **configuration management plan** sets the procedures to **track project artifacts** and control changes to them: which items are under configuration control, and how changes are **recorded and reported**.
- Performance monitoring produces **updates**: cost and schedule forecasts, the issue log, the lessons learned register and the risk register are kept current.
- Review artifacts regularly: is each one still **used**, still **accurate**, still worth its cost?
- **Measurement guidelines** from the Agile Practice Guide: **start small and evolve**; **visualize openly**; **review and act** in retrospectives; **decommission low-value metrics** that no longer influence decisions.

## Making artifacts accessible

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Project management information system (PMIS); PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Information radiators; PMBOK 7, Guide 2.7.3

The enabler: **help ensure accessibility of artifacts**.

- Information is useful only if it is **timely, accessible, easy to absorb**, and shows its **uncertainty** correctly.
- A **PMIS** gathers, integrates and distributes project outputs: scheduling tools, configuration management systems, information distribution systems, links to **knowledge repositories**, and automated KPI reporting.
- **Information radiators** (big visible charts) are posted where everyone can see them, rather than hidden in a tool; easy and frequent to update, often **low tech, high touch**.
- Think of **who** needs each artifact and **where** they will look: a shared repository, a dashboard, a wall.

## Communicating project status

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Project dashboards; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Visual controls; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Project reporting; Agile Practice Guide, 5.5.5

The enabler: **communicate project status**.

| Tool | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **Project dashboard** | Charts of progress against key measures, collected electronically, with **drill-down**; often **stoplight (red-amber-green)** charts, bar and pie charts, control charts, with a text explanation for anything **outside its threshold** |
| **Information radiator** | A visible physical display of work done, work remaining and risks |
| **Visual controls** | Task boards, kanban boards, burn charts, impediment lists |
| **Work performance reports** | Work performance information compiled for decisions and communication |

- **Project reporting** adapts the **level, format and detail** to each stakeholder group, regularly or **by exception**.
- **Reporting to sponsors** in agile: sponsors still need to know when significant scope may be finished and **how confident** the team is; stable throughput with an up-to-date item count gives that forecast.
- Report honestly: green status until a month before release is a **watermelon** (module 2.13).

## Predictive and agile

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.7; PMBOK 7, Guide 2.7.3; Agile Practice Guide, 5.5.3

| | Predictive | Agile |
|---|---|---|
| Main measures | Variances, CPI and SPI against the baselines | Accepted work, flow metrics, value and outcome measures |
| Typical artifacts | Status reports, dashboards, logs and registers | Information radiators, burn charts, task boards |
| Value | Benefits tracked against the business case | Customer satisfaction, business value delivered, adoption and usage, time to learn |
| Cadence | Reporting periods in the communications plan | Every iteration or flow review |

## Exam tips

- Leading indicators allow prevention; lagging indicators confirm what happened.
- Measure only what is relevant and actionable; drop metrics no one uses.
- The Hawthorne effect: measuring changes behavior; a vanity metric helps no decision.
- Act on a trend before the threshold is crossed.
- Tailor artifacts; keep them under configuration management; put them where people can see them.
- Changes to a baseline found while monitoring go through integrated change control.
