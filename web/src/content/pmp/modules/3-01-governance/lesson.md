## Key points

- **Project governance** is the framework, functions and processes that guide project decisions to **optimize value delivery**. It is shaped by the **organization's governance**, customers and regulators, and applies to predictive, adaptive and hybrid projects.
- Governance can be **structured** (sponsor, PMO, governance board, project manager) or **self-governed** (the team ensures value). Too much governance wastes resources; too little loses alignment.
- Effective governance needs three things: **target metrics** tied to strategy, **signaling mechanisms** (leading indicators) and **feedback mechanisms**.
- The structure, rules, procedures, reporting and policies come largely from **organizational process assets (OPAs)**: policies, processes and procedures, and knowledge repositories.
- **Ethics:** the PMI Code rests on **responsibility, respect, fairness and honesty**, each with **aspirational** and **mandatory** standards.
- **Success** has two dimensions: the **outcomes** (value realized) and the **project management** (cost, scope, time, quality). **Escalation paths and thresholds** say who decides what, and when a variance must go up.

## Governance in the Eighth Edition

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1; PMBOK 8, Standard 1.3.2; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.1

The first enabler of Business Environment task 1 is to **describe and establish the structure, rules, procedures, reporting, ethics and policies through the use of OPAs**.

- **Organizational governance** gives direction and control through policies, processes, procedures and decisions. It enforces **legal, regulatory and compliance** requirements, defines **ethical, social and environmental** responsibilities, sets operational, financial and risk policies, and aligns portfolios, programs and projects with **strategy**.
- **Project governance** is the adaptable framework that guides project activities to create value aligned with those goals: structure, systems and processes, **roles and responsibilities**, and decision-making models such as **RACI matrices** or **governance boards**.
- Governance is **tailored**: lightweight for adaptive methods, moderate or combined for hybrid, comprehensive for large predictive work. **Feedback loops** (reviews, lessons learned) let it improve.
- The overarching **value proposition** is the main factor in every governance decision.

| Governance scenario | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Project initiation** | A project or phase is judged worthwhile and launched |
| **Replanning** | A change to the plan that needs review by the team, sponsor or senior management |
| **Expansion or contraction** | Schedule, budget, quality, compliance or other constraints change to keep or capture value |
| **Early termination, positive cause** | Closed early because the **value has already been achieved** |
| **Early termination, negative cause** | Closed early because the value is **no longer achievable** |

## Governance models

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.2; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.3; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.7

| | Structured governance | Self-governance |
|---|---|---|
| Typical use | More **predictive** projects | More **adaptive** projects |
| Who governs | Executive **sponsor**, **PMO** leader, a **governance board**, and the project manager for project-level oversight | The **team**, or a group of project managers collectively accountable; responsibilities may be **distributed** |
| Risk | Too heavy, wasting resources | **Fragmented decisions**, unclear direction or accountability |
| Remedy | Fit it to the project | Clear, measurable **common objectives**, **leading indicators**, feedback |

**Three core components of effective governance**, whatever the model:

1. **Target metrics** clearly aligned with the organization's **strategic goals**;
2. **Signaling mechanisms** (alarm systems) for those metrics, typically **leading indicators**;
3. **Feedback mechanisms** so decision-makers can judge and improve their decisions.

Good governance metrics measure **outcomes** (how effective prioritization was, for example by **ROI**; due-date performance; whether the baseline carries the **highest value**), not inputs such as resource utilization or training completed.

**Guided self-governance:** agile teams decide their processes and priorities within **boundaries and guardrails**; daily coordination meetings are one self-governing mechanism.

## Structure, rules and procedures through OPAs

@source PMBOK 8, Standard 2.2.2; PMBOK 8, Standard 2.2.3; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.7; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6

**Organizational process assets** are internal plans, processes, documents, templates and knowledge used by the performing organization. Two groups:

| Group | Examples | Who updates it |
|---|---|---|
| **Policies, processes and procedures** | Tailoring guidelines; life cycles and methods; **templates** (plans, registers, reports, contracts, risk categories, probability and impact matrices); preapproved supplier lists; **change control procedures**; issue and defect management; work authorization; performance measurement guidelines | Usually set by the **PMO** or another function outside the project; changed only through organizational policy. Some organizations let teams **tailor** templates and checklists |
| **Organizational knowledge repositories** | Lessons learned, historical information, financial performance, metrics, issues and defects | **Updated throughout** the project |

Establishing governance for a project means:

- identifying the **control boards, committees** and other stakeholders that take part;
- defining the **status reporting requirements**;
- deciding whether and how a **PMO** is involved, and whether to set up a **governance board**;
- recording the tailoring decisions in the project management plan or another record.

## Ethics in project governance

@source PMI Code of Ethics, 1.4; PMI Code of Ethics, 1.5; PMI Code of Ethics, 2.3; PMI Code of Ethics, 3.3; PMI Code of Ethics, 4.3; PMI Code of Ethics, 5.3

Ethics is part of the governance enabler. The **PMI Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct** applies to PMI members, certification holders, applicants and volunteers.

| Value | Meaning | Mandatory examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Responsibility** | Ownership of decisions and actions, and their consequences | Uphold policies, rules, regulations and laws; **report unethical or illegal conduct**; file complaints only when backed by facts; **no retaliation** against people who raise concerns |
| **Respect** | High regard for ourselves, others and entrusted resources | **Negotiate in good faith**; do not use expertise or position for personal benefit; no abuse; respect **property rights** |
| **Fairness** | Impartial, objective decisions, free of self-interest and favoritism | **Disclose conflicts of interest** and stay out of the decision until disclosed, mitigated and approved; no favoritism, nepotism or bribery; no discrimination |
| **Honesty** | Understanding the truth and acting truthfully | No deceiving: no false statements, **half-truths** or withheld information that would mislead; no dishonesty for personal gain |

- **Aspirational** standards describe the conduct we strive for; **mandatory** standards are firm requirements, and breaking them leads to PMI's **Ethics Review Committee**.
- Treat the **appearance** of a conflict of interest as a conflict of interest.
- Honesty includes the **courage to share bad news** and not burying information or shifting blame.

> **On the exam.** A vendor offers a gift while bidding, a relative applies for a role, a sponsor asks you to hide a delay: disclose, follow the policy, report truthfully. Never accept, hide or retaliate.

## Defining success metrics

@source PMBOK 8, Standard 2.1.2; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.3; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.5.3; PMBOK 8, Guide Table 4-1

The enabler: **define success metrics**.

| Dimension | What it measures | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Success of project outcomes** | The **effectiveness** of the project in realizing the intended **value**, during or long after the project | Strategic objectives: sales targets, **ROI**, new customers, first to market, compliance, **sustainability goals** |
| **Success of project management** | The **efficiency** of the processes: adherence to **cost, scope, time and quality** | On time, on budget, to the required standards |

- Both matter. The Sydney Opera House ran far over budget and schedule, yet became a world landmark; a well-managed overpass in Montreal was demolished a year later because it did not fit the next project.
- The **project charter** holds the **measurable project objectives and related success criteria**.
- Success metrics should be **SMART** and tied to strategy; agree them with the sponsor and key stakeholders at the start.

## Escalation paths and thresholds

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.4; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Financial management plan; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.3.2.1; PMBOK 7, Guide 2.7.5

The enabler: **outline governance escalation paths and thresholds**.

- **Escalation** is useful in hierarchical organizations where decision authority sits outside the team, or where senior people can remove persistent obstacles or resolve conflicts.
- **Investment control** is needed where funds need formal stewardship (public companies, government, pension funds): defined **decision points** decide whether the next round of funding still makes sense.
- **Control thresholds** in the financial and schedule management plans set the variance allowed before action is needed.
- Agree in advance **who** decides at each level (team, project manager, sponsor, governance board, change control board) and **what** triggers escalation: a variance beyond a threshold, a change beyond the project manager's authority, a conflict the team cannot settle.
- Do not wait for a breach: if a trend shows one coming, escalate early.

> **On the exam.** Try to solve a problem at your level first. Escalate when it exceeds your **authority** or the agreed **threshold**, and follow the agreed path, rather than going around it.

## Predictive and agile

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.2; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.7; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.4

| | Predictive | Agile |
|---|---|---|
| Model | Structured: sponsor, PMO, governance board | Self-governance within guardrails |
| Decisions | Escalation through a hierarchy; investment control at decision points | Collaborative; the team decides its process and priorities |
| Signals | Thresholds and status reports | Leading indicators, visible boards, reviews |
| Weight | Often heavier, due to internal and external constraints | "Just enough" process; governance may live in the team's artifacts and events |

## Exam tips

- Governance protects the value proposition; choose the lightest model that keeps alignment.
- OPAs: policies and procedures are changed through the organization; knowledge repositories are updated by the project.
- Disclose conflicts of interest; report unethical conduct; never retaliate.
- Success = outcomes (value) and project management (cost, scope, time, quality).
- Escalate beyond your authority or a threshold, along the agreed path.
