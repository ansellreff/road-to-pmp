## Key points

- A **project** is a temporary initiative in a unique context undertaken to **create value**. **Project management** applies knowledge, skills, tools and techniques to meet or exceed the intended value; that does not mean gold plating or scope creep.
- **Portfolios** link strategy to execution; **programs** group related projects for benefits not available separately; projects and operations deliver and sustain value. Together they form a **system for value delivery**.
- The **mindset** has three dimensions, **proactive**, **ownership** and **value-driven**, and **six principles**: Adopt a Holistic View, Focus on Value, Embed Quality Into Processes and Deliverables, Be an Accountable Leader, Integrate Sustainability Within All Project Areas, Build an Empowered Culture.
- **Seven performance domains**: Governance, Scope (including quality), Schedule, Finance, Stakeholders, Resources, Risk. They run concurrently and interdependently.
- **Five focus areas**: Initiating, Planning, Executing, Monitoring and Controlling, Closing. They are **not phases** and they apply to every approach.
- **40 processes** sit in the domains and focus areas (Table 2-1). They are illustrative, **not prescriptive**: tailor what you use.

## Projects and the value they create

@source PMBOK 8, Standard 1.2; PMBOK 8, Standard 2.1; PMBOK 8, Standard 2.1.1

**Key terms of the Eighth Edition:**

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Project** | A temporary initiative in a unique context undertaken to create value. Temporary: a beginning and an end of the work or a phase. Unique context: distinct goals, environment, approach or stakeholders |
| **Project management** | Applying knowledge, skills, tools and techniques to project activities to **meet or exceed the intended value**; value-driven decisions, not gold plating or scope creep |
| **Output** | A product, result or service generated by a process |
| **Outcome** | The end result or consequence: the long-term effects, changes or value. Positive outcomes are **benefits**; negative ones are **disbenefits** |
| **Benefit** | A gain or asset realized by the organization and stakeholders as a result of outcomes |
| **Program** | Related projects and program activities managed together to obtain **benefits not available from managing them individually** |
| **Portfolio** | Programs, projects and operations managed as a group to maximize value and achieve strategic objectives |
| **Artifact** | A document or other item created to help manage the work and inform the team, stakeholders and management |

**Creating value.** Projects deliver value, or enhance the capability to produce it. **Business value** is a net quantifiable benefit, **tangible** (monetary assets, market share, productivity, profitability) or **intangible** (goodwill, reputation, brand, compliance, employee well-being, acquired knowledge). Value today extends to **societal impact and sustainability**. The expected value should meet or exceed a target threshold of return on the investment.

**The system for value delivery.** Portfolios, programs, projects, products and operations form an integrated system aligned with the organization's strategy. **Portfolio management is the central framework that links strategy to execution**, optimizing resources across all of them.

## The project management mindset

@source PMBOK 8, Standard 3.1; PMBOK 8, Standard 3.2

Project management is "much more than a collection of performance domains, processes, and methods": it is a **mindset**, sometimes called a growth mindset. It has three dimensions, each integrating two principles:

| Dimension | Emphasis | Principles |
|---|---|---|
| **Proactive** | Systems thinking, the right level of planning, anticipating challenges | Adopt a Holistic View; Embed Quality Into Processes and Deliverables |
| **Ownership** | Accountability of leaders and a high-performance team culture | Be an Accountable Leader; Build an Empowered Culture |
| **Value-driven** | Maximum value, with sustainability throughout: the **triple bottom line** of people, profit and planet | Focus on Value; Integrate Sustainability Within All Project Areas |

**Principles and domains.** The principles are the foundation; the **performance domains are the mechanics** that put them into practice. All principles relate to all domains, some more strongly than others.

## The six principles

@source PMBOK 8, Standard 3.3; PMBOK 8, Standard 3.4; PMBOK 8, Standard 3.5; PMBOK 8, Standard 3.6; PMBOK 8, Standard 3.7; PMBOK 8, Standard 3.8

| Principle | What it means | In action (the Eighth Edition's example) |
|---|---|---|
| **Adopt a Holistic View** | See the project as a system of interdependent parts (systems thinking); patterns, not snapshots; trace problems to their root | A health NGO learns midway of a government initiative with similar goals, aligns its communication, and wins a grant: stricter rules, but more resources and reach |
| **Focus on Value** | Value is the ultimate success indicator; deliver outcomes, not only deliverables; terminate if value is out of reach | Fewer features for an internal system in a culture that values simplicity raise usage and satisfaction (module 1.7) |
| **Embed Quality Into Processes and Deliverables** | Meet stakeholders' expectations and requirements; acceptance criteria; continuous improvement and waste elimination | A shipping firm entering a new market investigates customers' stricter expectations, not only the regulator's, and gains market share |
| **Be an Accountable Leader** | Take ownership of objectives, actions and decisions; integrity, honesty, fairness; self-awareness | Vendors' crews clash; instead of enforcing contracts, cross-vendor discussions find the root cause and adjust the shifts |
| **Integrate Sustainability Within All Project Areas** | Meet present needs without compromising future generations; environmental, social and economic impacts; avoid, minimize or at least compensate for negative outcomes | A construction team chooses materials with the least environmental impact, such as recycled concrete, rather than the cheapest |
| **Build an Empowered Culture** | Mutual trust; full clarity on roles, responsibilities, agreements and processes; collaboration with all stakeholders | Stakeholders across a diverse territory are included from inception and given guidelines to contribute |

> **On the exam.** Many situational questions are decided by a principle. The answer that looks at the whole system, keeps value in view, builds quality in from the start, takes ownership, considers sustainability and involves people is usually right; the answer that optimizes one part, hides problems or decides alone usually is not.

## The seven performance domains

@source PMBOK 8, Standard 3.2; PMBOK 8, Guide Table 2-1

A **performance domain** is a group of related processes critical for delivering project value. There are seven, **without weighting or order**:

| Domain | Covers (taught in) |
|---|---|
| **Governance** | Initiating, integrating plans, sourcing, execution, quality assurance, knowledge, performance, changes, closing (2.3, 2.15, 3.1, 3.3) |
| **Scope** (including quality) | Requirements, scope, the scope structure, validation (2.5, 2.11) |
| **Schedule** | Planning and developing the schedule, monitoring it (2.12, 2.13) |
| **Finance** | Costs, budget, financial monitoring (2.9, 2.10) |
| **Stakeholders** | Identification, engagement, communication (1.5, 1.6, 1.9) |
| **Resources** | Planning, estimating, acquiring, leading the team (1.3, 1.4, 2.7) |
| **Risk** | Planning, identification, analysis, responses, monitoring (3.5, 3.6) |

The domains **run concurrently** throughout the life cycle, whatever the delivery cadence, and work as an **integrated system**: none is a silo.

## The five focus areas

@source PMBOK 8, Standard 4.5; PMBOK 8, Standard 4.5.1; PMBOK 8, Standard 4.5.2; PMBOK 8, Standard 4.5.3; PMBOK 8, Standard 4.5.4; PMBOK 8, Standard 4.5.5; PMBOK 8, Standard 4.5.6

The **Project Management Focus Areas** group the fundamental actions of every project, whatever its approach.

| Focus area | Actions | Key benefit |
|---|---|---|
| **Initiating** | Define a new project or phase; often formal authorization; initial scope and funds; identify stakeholders; assign roles. Artifacts: charter, stakeholder register | Projects are authorized only when aligned with strategy, with the business case, benefits and stakeholders considered from the start |
| **Planning** | Establish scope, refine objectives, develop the course of action; artifacts such as the product backlog or the project management plan; repeated feedback loops | Defines the course of action for successfully completing the project or phase |
| **Executing** | Complete the work as currently agreed: coordinate resources, manage engagement, perform the work | Focused execution toward the value in the integrated baseline; the plan can and should change when that enhances value |
| **Monitoring and Controlling** | Track, measure, review and regulate progress; identify needed changes and initiate them | The project stays on track to meet expectations |
| **Closing** | Formally complete or close a project, phase or contract, or terminate a project early when that maximizes the return; verify outputs, outcomes and benefits | Phases, projects and contracts are closed out appropriately, transitioning to operations in a way that meets the target objectives |

**Three facts to remember:**

- Focus areas are **not phases**. Within each phase, their actions interact.
- They are **not strictly sequential**: they overlap, and planning and monitoring can happen at the same time.
- They are **independent of approach and industry**: predictive, adaptive and hybrid projects all honor them in some way.

**Business documents** (the business case, the benefits management plan) usually originate outside the project; the sponsor should provide the minimum information needed for the project manager to start.

## Where the 40 processes sit

@source PMBOK 8, Guide Table 2-1; PMBOK 8, Appendix X5.2

The Guide presents **40 processes** inside the seven domains, each with sample inputs, tools and techniques, and outputs. They are "illustrative but not comprehensive" and **not a prescriptive methodology**: use them as a reference when tailoring.

| Focus area | Processes | Count |
|---|---|---|
| **Initiating** | Initiate Project or Phase; Identify Stakeholders | 2 |
| **Planning** | Integrate and Align Project Plans; Plan Sourcing Strategy; Plan Scope Management; Elicit and Analyze Requirements; Define Scope; Develop Scope Structure; Plan Schedule Management; Develop Schedule; Plan Financial Management; Estimate Costs; Develop Budget; Plan Stakeholder Engagement; Plan Communications Management; Plan Resource Management; Estimate Resources; Plan Risk Management; Identify Risks; Perform Risk Analysis; Plan Risk Responses | 19 |
| **Executing** | Manage Project Execution; Manage Quality Assurance; Manage Project Knowledge; Manage Stakeholder Engagement; Manage Communications; Acquire Resources; Lead the Team; Implement Risk Responses | 8 |
| **Monitoring and Controlling** | Monitor and Control Project Performance; Assess and Implement Changes; Monitor and Control Scope; Validate Scope; Monitor and Control Schedule; Monitor and Control Finances; Monitor Stakeholder Engagement; Monitor Communications; Monitor and Control Resourcing; Monitor Risks | 10 |
| **Closing** | Close Project or Phase | 1 |

In total 2 + 19 + 8 + 10 + 1 = 40. By domain: Governance 9, Scope 6, Schedule 3, Finance 4, Stakeholders 7, Resources 5, Risk 6.

Why processes are back: in the market research for the Eighth Edition, most practitioners asked for Process Groups (now **focus areas**) and processes to return, as long as they stay **nonprescriptive**.

## Edition note: the Seventh Edition

@source PMBOK 8, Appendix X5.4; PMBOK 8, Appendix X5, Table X5-2; PMBOK 7, Guide section 2

Older questions and courses may use the Seventh Edition's **12 principles** and **8 performance domains**. The Eighth Edition merged and refined them to be more actionable and to avoid overlap:

| Seventh Edition principle(s) | Eighth Edition |
|---|---|
| Navigate Complexity; Recognize, Evaluate, and Respond to System Interactions | **Adopt a Holistic View** |
| Effectively Engage With Stakeholders; Focus on Value; Embrace Adaptability and Resiliency; Enable Change to Achieve the Envisioned Future State | **Focus on Value** (stakeholders become a performance domain) |
| Build Quality Into Processes and Deliverables | **Embed Quality Into Processes and Deliverables** |
| Create a Collaborative Project Team | **Build an Empowered Culture** |
| Demonstrate Leadership Behaviors; Be a Diligent, Respectful, and Caring Steward | **Be an Accountable Leader** |
| Tailor Based on Context | Moved to the **Tailoring** section (module 2.2) |
| Optimize Risk Responses | Moved to the **Risk** performance domain |
| (new) | **Integrate Sustainability Within All Project Areas** |

The Seventh Edition's eight domains were Stakeholder, Team, Development Approach and Life Cycle, Planning, Project Work, Delivery, Measurement, and Uncertainty.

## Exam tips

- Project: temporary, unique context, creates value. Output is the thing; outcome is the effect; benefit is the gain.
- Six principles, three mindset dimensions (proactive, ownership, value-driven).
- Seven domains: Governance, Scope, Schedule, Finance, Stakeholders, Resources, Risk.
- Five focus areas: not phases, not strictly sequential, for every approach.
- 40 processes: a reference to tailor, not a recipe.
