## Key points

- **Procurement** gets goods, services or results from **outside** the organization. The Eighth Edition has no procurement domain: sourcing is planned in the **Governance** domain (**Plan Sourcing Strategy**), and procurement is explained in **Appendix X4**.
- Three stages: **plan** the procurement, **conduct** it (bids, selection, negotiation, award), **monitor and control** it (manage the relationship and performance, close the contract).
- The **make-or-buy analysis** decides what is done inside and what is bought. Buying transfers delivery risk; making keeps control.
- **Bid documents**: an **RFI** gathers market information, an **RFP** asks for a solution to a complex scope, an **RFQ** asks for a price when price decides.
- **Contract types** share risk differently: **fixed price** puts cost risk on the seller, **cost-reimbursable** on the buyer, **time and materials** sits between them. Agile contracts keep flexibility (fixed-price increments, not-to-exceed T&M, early cancellation).
- **Negotiate for a win-win**, led by the person with **authority to sign**. Settle claims by **negotiation first**; litigation is the last option.

## Procurement in the Eighth Edition

@source PMBOK 8, Appendix X4.1; PMBOK 8, Appendix X4.2; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.5.4

The **buyer** purchases; the **seller** (vendor, contractor, supplier) provides. The buyer-seller relationship has **information asymmetry**: each side knows different things about the deal, which is a source of risk.

Procurement is **not a separate performance domain**, because project procurements usually run through the organization's **procurement department**, with its policies and vendor relationships. Large projects may have their own procurement team, still aligned with the organization's standards.

Agreements can be contracts, purchase orders, **memoranda of agreement (MOAs)** or **service-level agreements (SLAs)**.

| Stage | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Plan** | Identify the needs; involve key stakeholders early; decide internal or external; set the rules; identify potential sellers; prepare documents such as an **RFP** or an **invitation for bids (IFB)**, also called an **invitation to tender (ITT)** |
| **Conduct** | Get responses, evaluate them, select a seller, negotiate and award the contract. The process is **transparent and fair**, and bidders' questions are answered |
| **Monitor and control** | Manage the relationship, monitor contract performance, make changes and corrections, and **close the contract** when all obligations are met |

## Planning procurement: the sourcing strategy

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.3; PMBOK 8, Guide Table 2-3; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Make-or-buy analysis and decisions; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Market research; PMBOK 8, Appendix X4.3

The first enabler of Process task 5 is to **plan procurement**.

**Plan Sourcing Strategy** documents the sourcing decisions, the **source selection approach**, the **scope of work** for external sources, and the contracts and sources to use: **what** to acquire, **how** and **when**. It applies when the project needs external sources, and differs from Plan Resource Management because it covers **outsourced** work.

| Inputs (examples) | Tools and techniques | Outputs |
|---|---|---|
| Project charter; scope baseline; quality, schedule, financial and resource management plans; requirements documentation; resource requirements; **risk register**; stakeholder register | Expert judgment; **market research**; **make-or-buy analysis**; **source selection analysis**; document analysis | **Sourcing strategy plan**: insourcing or outsourcing decisions, **source selection criteria** |

The purchasing department and the **legal department** may take part; their responsibilities are written in the sourcing strategy plan.

**The make-or-buy decision**

| Insourcing (make) | Outsourcing (buy) |
|---|---|
| Uses internal expertise when it exists | Brings in expertise missing internally |
| Often cheaper when much **new innovation** is needed | Often cheaper when the goods or services are **common** |
| Tighter link to the strategic advantage | Frees capacity and capital for core strengths |
| More **control** and oversight of deliverables | **Transfers delivery risk** to the supplier |

Other factors: current resource allocation and skills, the need for specialized or **independent** expertise, and avoiding more permanent employment. The analysis may use **payback period, ROI, IRR, NPV**, discounted cash flow or cost-benefit analysis, and weighs the risks of each choice.

**Market research** examines industry and seller capabilities: conferences, online reviews and other sources.

## The procurement strategy and the delivery solution

@source PMBOK 8, Appendix X4.4; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Procurement management plan

Two enablers: **plan and manage the procurement strategy**, and **develop a delivery solution**.

Once the decision is to buy, the **procurement strategy** sets the **delivery method**, the **type of legally binding agreement**, and how the procurement moves through its **phases**.

| Part | Options |
|---|---|
| **Delivery method: professional services** | Provider with no subcontracting; provider with subcontracting allowed; **joint venture** between buyer and provider; provider acts as the buyer's representative |
| **Delivery method: industrial, integration or build-out** | **Turnkey**; **design-build (DB)**; **design-bid-build (DBB)**; **design-build-operate (DBO)**; **build-own-operate-transfer (BOOT)** |
| **Integrated project delivery (IPD)** | One **multiparty agreement** (owner, designer, contractor); **shared risks and rewards**; early involvement of key participants; collaborative decisions; **open-book accounting** |
| **Procurement phases** | Sequence and objectives of each phase; performance indicators and milestones; **criteria for moving from phase to phase**; monitoring plan; knowledge transfer to later phases |

The **procurement management plan** describes how the team acquires goods and services from outside the performing organization.

## Bids and source selection

@source PMBOK 8, Appendix X4.5; PMBOK 8, Appendix X4, Figure X4-1; PMBOK 8, Appendix X4.6; PMBOK 8, Appendix X4.7; The Project Management Answer Book, Chapter 9, Q20

The next enabler is to **execute a procurement management plan**.

| Bid document | Used when |
|---|---|
| **Request for information (RFI)** | To gather information from the **market** before bid documents go to selected vendors |
| **Request for proposal (RFP)** | The scope is **complex** and the buyer wants the vendor to **propose a solution** |
| **Request for quote (RFQ)** | **Price** is the main deciding factor and the solution is readily available |

The flow (Figure X4-1): prepare the **statement of work (SOW)** and high-level estimates, prepare the bid documents, advertise, hold a **bidder conference** to answer questions, receive proposals or quotes, evaluate and select, negotiate terms, contract.

**Source selection methods.** Put the evaluation method **in the bid documents**, so bidders know how they will be judged.

| Method | When it fits |
|---|---|
| **Least cost** | Standard, routine work with a well-defined outcome; avoid it for complex work, where it may hurt quality |
| **Qualifications only** | The procurement is small, so a full selection process is not worth it |
| **Quality-based (highest technical score)** | The best technical proposal wins, if its price can be negotiated |
| **Quality and cost based** | Cost is a factor; the higher the risk or uncertainty, the more weight on quality |
| **Single source** | Only one supplier can meet the requirement (patents, exclusive rights); not a way to favor a vendor |
| **Fixed budget** | The budget is disclosed and the best proposal within it wins; only when the SOW is precise and no changes are expected |

**Source selection criteria** include capability and capacity, product and **life cycle cost**, delivery dates, technical approach, relevant experience, key staff, **financial stability**, management experience, knowledge transfer and **sustainability credentials**. They are **weighted**, and the weighted scores rank the proposals and set the negotiating order. Agree the criteria with the key stakeholders **before** reading the proposals.

## Selecting contract types

@source PMBOK 8, Appendix X4.8; PMBOK 8, Appendix X4.8.1; PMBOK 6, 12.1.1.6; The Project Management Answer Book, Chapter 9, Q11; The Project Management Answer Book, Chapter 9, Q12; The Project Management Answer Book, Chapter 9, Q13

The enabler: **select preferred contract types**. The contract type decides **who carries the risk**. The aim is a contract that benefits **all** parties.

| Contract | How it pays | Fits when | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fixed price** | A set price for a clearly defined scope | Requirements are **well defined**, few changes expected | Cost risk on the **seller**; buyer gets budget certainty but may pay a risk premium |
| **Cost-reimbursable** | Actual costs plus a fee (the seller's profit) | Scope is **uncertain** or the work is high risk (research, complex builds) | Cost risk on the **buyer** |
| **Time and materials (T&M)** | Actual time and materials plus a margin | Small jobs, staff augmentation, scope not clear at the start | A hybrid; the buyer must **monitor closely** |
| **Target cost** | A target cost, with savings or overruns **shared** | Large infrastructure or complex manufacturing | Shared; needs a clear target and sharing rule |

> **Edition note.** Exam questions still name the Sixth Edition's variants. **Fixed price:** firm fixed price (**FFP**, the most common), fixed price incentive fee (**FPIF**, with a price ceiling), fixed price with economic price adjustment (**FPEPA**, for long contracts or other currencies: inflation, commodity prices). **Cost-reimbursable:** cost plus fixed fee (**CPFF**), cost plus incentive fee (**CPIF**, overruns and savings shared by a formula such as 80/20), cost plus award fee (**CPAF**, fee based on the buyer's subjective judgment of performance).

## Agile contracts

@source Agile Practice Guide, Agile Procurement and Contracts; PMBOK 8, Appendix X4.8.2

The Agile Manifesto values **customer collaboration over contract negotiation**. A collaborative contract pursues **shared risk and reward**.

| Technique | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Multitiered structure** | Fixed items in a master agreement; changeable items in a schedule of services; scope, schedule and budget in a **lightweight SOW** |
| **Emphasize value delivered** | Milestones and payments tied to **value-driven deliverables**, not intermediate documents |
| **Fixed-price increments** | Scope split into fixed-price micro-deliverables, such as user stories |
| **Not-to-exceed T&M** | T&M with a **budget cap**; new ideas replace planned work within that capacity |
| **Graduated T&M** | A higher rate for early delivery, a lower one for late delivery |
| **Early cancellation option** | The customer can stop once enough value is delivered, paying a cancellation fee |
| **Dynamic scope option** | Fixed budget, but the customer may change the scope at set points |
| **Team augmentation** | The supplier's people work inside the customer's team; fund **teams**, not a fixed scope |
| **Favor full-service suppliers** | Each supplier delivers full value, not one piece of a web of dependencies |

Newer trends in the Eighth Edition: **agile contracting**, **smart contracts** (blockchain, automatic milestone payments), **outcome-based** contracts (pay for measurable results), **sustainable** contracting (environmental, social and governance terms) and **collaborative** contracting (for example a T&M contract with a cap).

## Negotiation: strategy and participation

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Negotiation; PMBOK 8, Appendix X4.5; The Project Management Answer Book, Chapter 9, Q3; The Project Management Answer Book, Chapter 9, Q4; The Project Management Answer Book, Chapter 9, Q7

Two enablers: **participate in agreement negotiations** and **determine a negotiation strategy**.

- **Procurement negotiation** clarifies the structure, rights and obligations of both parties before the contract is signed. It ends with a **signed contract** that reflects everything agreed.
- It is **led by a member of the procurement team with authority to sign**. The project manager may attend to **assist**.
- **Almost everything is negotiable**: cost, delivery and payment dates, location of work, ownership of intellectual property.
- **Strategy: aim for a win-win.** A vendor that loses the negotiation may do the bare minimum or send its least experienced people. Ask what success looks like for the other side.
- **Concessions:** ask only for the important ones, offer something in return, never ask for something unfair, and accept a "no".

> **On the exam.** The project manager supports the negotiation; the person with contracting authority signs. A project manager who promises the vendor extra scope or money without that authority has made a mistake.

## Managing suppliers, contracts and vendor performance

@source PMBOK 8, Appendix X4.2; PMBOK 6, 12.3.2.3; PMBOK 6, 12.3.2.4; PMBOK 6, 12.3.2.5; PMBOK 6, 12.3.3.1; PMBOK 6, 12.3.3.7; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Procurement documentation

Three enablers: **manage suppliers and contracts**, **evaluate vendor performance**, and **verify the objectives of the agreement are met**.

| Tool | What it checks |
|---|---|
| **Performance reviews** | Quality, resource, schedule and cost performance against the agreement |
| **Earned value and trend analysis** | Variances and forecasts of the seller's cost and schedule |
| **Inspection** | A structured review of the contractor's work, for example a site walkthrough by buyer and contractor |
| **Audits** | A structured review of the **procurement process**; rights to audit are written in the contract |

- **Seller performance evaluation** documents how well the seller performed and whether it may work on future projects; poor performers may leave the **prequalified seller list**.
- **Verifying objectives are met:** before closing, all deliverables are provided on time and meet the technical and quality requirements, there are **no outstanding claims or invoices**, and final payments are made. The buyer then gives the seller **formal written notice** that the contract is complete (module 2.15).
- **Procurement documentation** keeps the full record: the SOW, payments, the contractor's work performance information, drawings and correspondence.

## Claims and disputes

@source PMBOK 8, Appendix X4.9; PMBOK 8, Appendix X4.9.1; PMBOK 6, 12.3.2.2

A **claim** is a contested change: buyer and seller cannot agree on the compensation, or on whether a change happened. Unresolved claims become **disputes**. Time and cost overruns from **variation orders** are a main cause.

**Alternative dispute resolution (ADR)**, from the least to the most formal:

| Method | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Negotiation** | Direct talks, no third party; the **preferred** way to settle claims |
| **Mediation** | A neutral third party helps the parties reach their own agreement |
| **Dispute review board** | Neutral experts appointed **at the start** of the project to avoid and resolve disputes |
| **Expert determination** | An independent expert decides one technical or financial issue |
| **Arbitration** | An arbitrator or panel hears both sides and makes a **binding** decision |
| **Litigation** | A court decides; the **last option** when all else fails |

The project manager knows the contract terms, keeps **detailed records** of activities, changes and communications, spots conflicts early, and works with the **legal team**.

## Predictive and agile

@source PMBOK 8, Appendix X4.8.1; Agile Practice Guide, Agile Procurement and Contracts; PMBOK 8, Appendix X4.8.2

| | Predictive | Agile |
|---|---|---|
| Scope in the contract | Defined in a detailed SOW | A lightweight SOW; scope varies within a budget |
| Typical contract | Fixed price, or cost-reimbursable when scope is uncertain | Fixed-price increments, not-to-exceed T&M, team augmentation |
| Payment | On milestones or phase gates | On delivered increments of value |
| Change | Through formal contract change control | Dynamic scope options, early cancellation |
| Relationship | Monitored against the agreement | Collaboration and frequent feedback |

## Exam tips

- Make-or-buy comes first; the procurement strategy follows a decision to buy.
- RFI for market information, RFP for a solution, RFQ for a price.
- Fixed price: seller's risk. Cost-reimbursable: buyer's risk. T&M: in between.
- Well-defined scope points to fixed price; uncertain scope to cost-reimbursable or an agile contract.
- The person with contracting authority negotiates and signs; the project manager assists.
- Claims: negotiate first, litigation last.
