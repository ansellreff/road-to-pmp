## Key points

- **Value components** are what makes the outcome worth more than it costs: tangible (revenue, market share, productivity) and intangible (reputation, compliance, well-being), agreed with key stakeholders.
- **Prioritize by value and feedback**: MoSCoW, stack ranking, value versus effort, **cost of delay**, **WSJF** ((business value + time criticality + risk reduction) / size), Kano, buy-a-feature. The product owner orders the backlog.
- **Deliver incrementally** where possible: early, usable increments confirm value, surface hidden work and reduce risk. A **minimum viable product** tests value with the fewest features.
- **Examine business value throughout**, not only at the end: if the intended value is no longer likely, recommend stopping.
- The **benefits management plan** is the **measurement system**: target benefits, strategic alignment, timeframe, **benefits owner**, metrics, assumptions and risks.
- **Demonstrate value** through reviews of working product, a suitable delivery cadence and outcome metrics, not only output counts.

## Identifying value components with key stakeholders

@source PMBOK 8, Standard 2.1; PMBOK 8, Standard 3.4; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.2.1

Process task 3, **help ensure value-based delivery**, begins with the enabler **identify value components with key stakeholders**.

- **Business value** is a net quantifiable benefit, tangible or intangible, during the project, at its end or in the long term.

| Tangible | Intangible |
|---|---|
| Monetary assets, productivity, profitability, stockholder equity, market share, infrastructure capabilities, utility, environmental improvements | Goodwill, brand recognition, public benefit, acquired knowledge, compliance, reputation, employee well-being, environmental awareness |

- Value is assessed with **measurable metrics** (such as return on investment) or **qualitative** observations (testimonials, societal benefits), particularly from the perspective of **key stakeholders**.
- A **value breakdown structure** gives each major deliverable a value estimate, or a percentage of the total (100% if mandatory), so deliverables and work can be prioritized by value (module 2.5).

> **On the exam.** Do not assume what "value" means for a stakeholder. Ask: a hospital board may value patient safety and compliance more than speed. Agree the value components and how they will be measured before prioritizing work.

## Prioritizing work by value and feedback

@source Agile Practice Guide, 5.2.1; Agile Practice Guide, 5.2.2; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Prioritization/ranking; Scrum Guide, Scrum Team

The second enabler is to **prioritize work based on value and stakeholder feedback**. The **backlog** is the ordered list of all the team's work; only enough is detailed for the first release in broad strokes and the next iteration in detail.

| Technique | How it works | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **MoSCoW** | Must have, should have, could have, will not have | Fast in workshops; coarse and subjective |
| **Stack rank** (forced ranking) | One ordered list, no ties | Clarifying; needs a single decision owner |
| **Value versus effort** (2 by 2) | Plots impact against effort | Good for quick cuts; depends on rough estimates |
| **Cost of delay** (CD3 = cost of delay / duration) | Prioritizes by time sensitivity and economic impact | |
| **WSJF** (weighted shortest job first) | (User business value + time criticality + risk reduction) / size | Scales well; relies on relative scoring |
| **Kano** | Classifies features as **basic**, **performance** or **delighter** | Guides the mix of outcomes; not a queueing method |
| **Buy-a-feature** ($100 test) | Stakeholders "spend" a budget on items | Shows true preferences; can be gamed if scope is unclear |

**A WSJF example.** An item scores 8 for business value, 5 for time criticality and 3 for risk reduction, with a size of 4: (8 + 5 + 3) / 4 = 4. A second item scores (5 + 3 + 2) / 5 = 2, so the first goes ahead of it.

**Who orders the work.** The **product owner** orders the Product Backlog (Scrum Guide), may form a product owner value team, and **replans the roadmap** as the team delivers and new information emerges. **Backlog refinement** sessions, usually in the middle of an iteration, prepare upcoming stories with the team. Stakeholder feedback from reviews flows back into the order.

## Delivering value incrementally

@source Agile Practice Guide, 5.2.8; Agile Practice Guide, 2.3.3; PMBOK 8, Standard 4.2.1; PMBOK 8, Standard 4.4

The third enabler is to **assess opportunities to deliver value incrementally**.

**Why ship often** (Agile Practice Guide): working products, even in small increments, are the primary measure of progress. Shipping:

- **confirms value**: only a released increment lets customers decide whether the outcome is worth the investment;
- **surfaces hidden work**: packaging, deployment, documentation, compliance and handoffs become visible;
- **reduces risk**: wrong requirements cost less and integration problems appear early.

**Smallest useful releases.** A **minimum viable product (MVP)** is the smallest testable version of an idea, used to learn in a build, measure, learn loop (module 2.4).

**Incremental delivery in predictive work.** Even with scope planned up front, delivering in parts can give stakeholders value earlier, while the overall scope and timeline stay largely fixed (module 2.2).

## Examining business value throughout the project

@source PMBOK 8, Standard 3.4; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Business case; Agile Practice Guide, 5.5.3

The fourth enabler is to **examine the business value throughout the project**.

- Desired outcomes are **described, iteratively assessed and updated** with quality gates, feedback loops and regular reviews. If misalignment persists or the project is unlikely to deliver the intended value, **it may be best to terminate**.
- The **business case** is used throughout the life cycle, not only to start the project.
- **Outcome measures** (Agile Practice Guide) link delivery to value: customer satisfaction or Net Promoter Score, **business value delivered** (features mapped to OKRs, benefit hypotheses, revenue), adoption and usage analytics, and **time to learn**.

## A system to measure benefits

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Benefits management plan

The fifth enabler is to **verify a measurement system is in place to track benefits**. That system is the **benefits management plan**: how and when benefits will be delivered and the mechanisms to measure them. It begins early, with the target benefits, and may include:

| Element | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Target benefits** | The expected tangible and intangible value; financial value may be expressed as **NPV** |
| **Strategic alignment** | How the project aligns with the organization's strategies |
| **Timeframe** | Short term, long term, ongoing, or by phase |
| **Benefits owner** | The person **accountable for monitoring, recording and reporting** realized benefits |
| **Metrics** | Direct and indirect measures of the benefits realized |
| **Assumptions** | Factors expected to be in place |
| **Risks** | Risks to realizing the benefits |

It also describes how the business value becomes part of **ongoing operations**, with the metrics that verify it, because many benefits arrive **after the project closes**.

> **On the exam.** If nobody owns a benefit, nobody will measure it. A missing benefits owner or missing metrics is a gap to fix during planning, not after closure.

## Evaluating delivery options to demonstrate value

@source PMBOK 8, Standard 4.4; Agile Practice Guide, 5.2.6; Agile Practice Guide, 5.5.3

The last enabler is to **evaluate delivery options to demonstrate value**.

- **Delivery cadence** (module 2.2): single, multiple, periodic or continuous delivery. Earlier and more frequent deliveries show value sooner.
- **Demonstrations and reviews**: show working product at least every two weeks; the product owner accepts or declines stories, and stakeholders give feedback.
- **Outcome metrics** show the value of what was delivered, not just how much was delivered.

## Predictive and agile

@source PMBOK 8, Standard 4.2.1; Agile Practice Guide, 5.2.1; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Benefits management plan

| | Predictive | Agile |
|---|---|---|
| Value defined | Business case and benefits management plan; VBS | Product vision, roadmap and backlog, ordered by value |
| Prioritizing | Requirements prioritized during planning | Continuous backlog ordering with stakeholder feedback |
| Delivering | Single or incremental deliveries against a fixed scope | Frequent increments; MVP to test assumptions |
| Measuring | Benefits measured against the plan, often after closure | Outcome metrics each release, plus the benefits plan |

## Exam tips

- Agree the value components with key stakeholders first.
- WSJF = (business value + time criticality + risk reduction) / size; higher goes first.
- MoSCoW: must, should, could, will not. Kano: basic, performance, delighter.
- Ship small and early to confirm value and reduce risk.
- Every benefit needs an owner and a metric; keep examining value throughout.
