## Key points

- **Initiate Project or Phase** authorizes the project, usually with a **project charter**, and gives the project manager authority to use organizational resources. Assign the project manager as early as possible, **always before planning begins**.
- The **business case** justifies the project and is used throughout, even after completion; the **benefits management plan** says how and when benefits are delivered and measured.
- **Integrate and Align Project Plans** consolidates every management plan and baseline into one **project management plan**, checks they align, and records the tailoring, development approach and life cycle.
- The project management plan is a **living document**: the basis for all decisions, changed when that enhances value, with **baselines** changed only through formal change control.
- Plan **only as much as needed** to move forward; estimates come with a **basis of estimates** (assumptions, ranges, confidence).
- **Work performance data** (raw observations) becomes **work performance information** (compared with the plan), which supports decisions.

## The charter and the business documents

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.1; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Project charter; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Business case; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Benefits management plan; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Assumption log

**Initiate Project or Phase** officially authorizes the start of a project and grants the project manager the authority to allocate organizational resources, typically through a **project charter**. Authorization links the project, the **business case** and the organization's strategic goals, and shows the organization's commitment.

| Artifact | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Project charter** | Issued by the initiator or sponsor; formally authorizes the project and gives the project manager authority to apply resources. Forms a partnership between the performing and requesting organizations |
| **Business case** | A documented economic feasibility study that establishes the validity of the benefits; lists objectives and reasons; may lead to a go/no-go decision before initiation; used **throughout** the life cycle and to measure success at the end |
| **Benefits management plan** | How and when benefits will be delivered, and the mechanisms to **measure** them; target benefits (financial value may be expressed as NPV) |
| **Assumption log** | All assumptions and constraints throughout the project; high-level ones come from the business case and flow into the charter |

- The **project manager** should be identified as early as possible, ideally while the charter is developed and **always before planning begins**.
- The charter can be developed by the sponsor or by the project manager with the initiating entity, which helps the project manager understand the purpose, objectives and benefits.
- Between organizations, **contracts** set the agreement; charters are then used inside each organization.
- **Adaptive charters** are lighter, from a worksheet or vision statement to a moderate document, answering some or all of: why, who will be engaged, what, where, when, how, and who is the customer.

## Critical information requirements

@source PMBOK 8, Standard 3.7.1; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Work performance information; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.5.2.3

The enabler **determine critical information requirements (e.g., sustainability)** asks: what must the project know and track to be steered well?

- **Sustainability as information.** Enterprise sustainability or information management strategies may impose requirements on deliverables and teams. Sustainability **KPIs** may be written into the **scope statement, charter, business case or contracts**, and compliance monitored through planning, execution and closure. Because there are no universal sustainability criteria, each project needs a tailored approach, and the team needs **tools that provide the necessary information** to manage the sustainability of its work.
- **Performance metrics.** Specific metrics for **scope, schedule, budget and quality** are defined **at the start**, as part of the project management plan, so performance data can later be compared with them.
- **Stakeholders' information needs** are analyzed in communication planning (module 1.9).

> **On the exam.** If an organization has sustainability goals, the right time to capture them is at the start: as KPIs in the charter or business case, requirements in the scope, and criteria in contracts. Adding them at closure is too late.

## Creating the integrated plan

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.2; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Project management plan; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Scope baseline; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Schedule baseline; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Cost baseline; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Change management plan

**Integrate and Align Project Plans** integrates, aligns and coordinates all plan components into **one project management plan**: a thorough basis for how all project activities will be carried out. It is done once or at specific intervals.

**At the beginning**, this process establishes the overall **tailoring**, the **development approach** and the **life cycle** (module 2.2); these become part of the plan and guide planning in every performance domain. Then **all management plans and baselines** from the domains are integrated and **their alignment verified**, along with other plans such as a change management or configuration management plan. The plan may be called a governance, execution, development or implementation plan; whatever its name, it transparently defines the framework in which decisions are made.

**The project management plan** describes how the project will be executed, monitored and controlled, and closed. It **defines the basis for all project decisions** and is a **living document**. Typical components (tailored to include only what is relevant):

| Plans | Also |
|---|---|
| Change, communications, financial, procurement, quality, requirements, resource, risk, schedule, scope, sourcing strategy and stakeholder engagement management plans; test plan | Iteration plan and release plan (adaptive work) |

**Baselines** are approved versions, changed only through **formal change control**, and compared with actual results:

| Baseline | Contents |
|---|---|
| **Scope baseline** | The approved scope statement (scope, major deliverables, exclusions), the WBS and the WBS dictionary |
| **Schedule baseline** | The approved schedule model, with baseline start and finish dates |
| **Cost baseline** | The approved time-phased budget, **excluding management reserves** |

The **change management plan** establishes the change control board (also called a steering committee), its authority, and how the change control system works (module 3.3).

**How much to plan.** The time spent planning, up front and throughout, depends on the circumstances: enough to move forward appropriately, **not more detailed than necessary**. Planning artifacts confirm stakeholder expectations and give stakeholders what they need to decide and act.

## Estimating effort and resources

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.6.2.2; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Basis of estimates

The enabler **estimate work effort and resource requirements** is introduced here; the techniques follow in modules 2.7 (resources), 2.9 (costs) and 2.12 to 2.13 (schedule and story points).

- **Estimate Resources** identifies the **type, quantity and characteristics** of resources needed. It helps the project manager anticipate **shortages or surpluses** and manage allocation risks.
- Every estimate needs a **basis of estimates**: how it was derived, assumptions, constraints, the level of detail, the **range** (for example ±10%), the **confidence level**, and the risks that influence it.

> **On the exam.** An estimate given as a single number with no assumptions is weak. Ask for the range, the confidence and the assumptions behind it, and record them.

## Checking the consolidated plan

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.2; PMBOK 8, Standard 3.4; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Business case

The enabler **assess consolidated project plans for dependencies, gaps and continued business value** is the "align" in Integrate and Align Project Plans.

- **Dependencies and gaps.** When the domain plans are combined, verify that they align: the schedule fits the resource plan, the budget covers the scope, risk responses have owners and funds, and nothing required by one plan is missing from another.
- **Continued business value.** The business case is used throughout the life cycle. Desired outcomes are assessed iteratively with quality gates, feedback loops and reviews, and the team keeps evaluating alignment with the business case and intended outcomes. **If misalignment persists or the intended value is unlikely, it may be best to terminate** the effort.

## Maintaining the plan

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Project management plan; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.4; PMBOK 8, Standard 4.5.3

The enabler **maintain the integrated project management plan**:

- The plan **is expected to change**: it is a living document.
- **Manage Project Execution** leads and performs the work in the plan and **implements approved changes**, including corrective and preventive actions and defect repair, and adjusts plans based on the analysis of work performance data.
- The plan **can and should be changed** whenever that would enhance the project's value proposition, but changes to **baselines** go through **formal change control**.

## Data for informed decisions

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Work performance data; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Work performance information; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.5.1; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.4

The last enabler is to **collect and analyze data to make informed project decisions**.

| | What it is | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Work performance data** | Raw observations and measurements from the work: the lowest level of detail | Work completed, KPIs, actual start and finish dates, story points completed, number of change requests, number of defects |
| **Work performance information** | Data analyzed against plan components and documents, showing **how the project is performing** | Variances against the baselines; status of deliverables |
| **Work performance reports** | Information compiled to generate decisions, actions or awareness (module 1.9) | Status reports, dashboards |

Data is collected during execution and passed to the controlling processes for analysis; it also feeds lessons learned. **Data-driven decision-making** means preparing supporting material thoroughly and guiding the decision; AI tools such as predictive analytics and data visualization can help, combined with leadership skills.

## Predictive and agile

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.1; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.2; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Project management plan; PMBOK 8, Standard 4.2.2

| | Predictive | Agile |
|---|---|---|
| Charter | A formal document that authorizes the project and the project manager | A light charter or vision answering why, who, what, where, when, how, and for whom |
| Plan | Detailed management plans and baselines, much of it up front | A clear vision first; release and iteration plans; the whole team plans at several levels |
| Change | Baseline changes through formal change control | Requirements progressively elaborated or replaced through feedback |

## Exam tips

- No charter, no authority: the charter authorizes the project and the project manager.
- Assign the project manager before planning begins.
- The project management plan integrates all plans and baselines; check them for gaps and dependencies.
- Baselines change only through change control; the plan itself is a living document.
- Keep checking the business case: if the value is gone, recommend stopping.
