## Key points

- **Close Project or Phase** finalizes all activities of a project, phase, release, iteration or contract, whether it **succeeded or not**. It archives information, completes the planned work, **releases resources**, and confirms how far **value** was achieved.
- Close only when the **exit criteria** are met: deliverables **formally accepted**, issues resolved, value delivered with a plan to **maintain** it, costs charged and accounts closed.
- **Validate readiness for transition**: a **readiness assessment** finds the gaps, a **transition plan** closes them, and the product is **handed over** to operations or the next phase.
- **Contracts** close with formal acceptance, settled claims, paid invoices and archived records.
- **Lessons learned** and **retrospectives** go into the organizational process assets; outstanding **risks** are accepted or **transitioned** to operations.
- The **final report** summarizes scope, quality, cost and schedule results, and the **benefits** achieved or still expected.

## Close Project or Phase

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.9; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.9.1

Process task 10 is to **manage project closure**.

**Close Project or Phase** finalizes all activities for **successful and unsuccessful** projects, phases, releases, iterations or contracts. Its benefits: project or phase information is **archived**, the planned work is completed, and organizational **resources are released** for new work. It also confirms **how far value**, or the capability to deliver value, has been achieved.

| Inputs (examples) | Tools and techniques | Outputs |
|---|---|---|
| Project charter; project management plan; assumption, change and issue logs; lessons learned register; quality reports; risk register and report; **accepted deliverables**; **business case**; **benefits management plan**; agreements; procurement documentation | Expert judgment; document, regression, trend and variance analysis; meetings | Lessons learned register updates; **final product, service or result transition**; **final report**; **organizational process asset updates** |

## Criteria for closing the project or phase

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.9.1; PMBOK 8, Guide Table 4-1; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.9

The enabler: **determine criteria to successfully close the project or phase**.

The **project charter** states the measurable objectives and **success criteria**; the scope statement gives the acceptance criteria. Before closing, the project manager reviews the plan to confirm that all work is complete and the objectives are met. **Exit criteria** in a predictive project:

- all documents and deliverables are **updated** and all **issues resolved**;
- the measurable project **value was delivered**, with a plan to **maintain** it after transition;
- deliverables are delivered and **formally accepted** by the customer;
- **all costs are charged** to the project, and project **accounts are closed**;
- personnel are **reassigned or released**; excess materials are dealt with; facilities and equipment are released;
- **final reports** are prepared as organizational policy requires.

If a project ends **early**, closure still happens: there are procedures to **investigate and document why** it was terminated.

> **On the exam.** A project cancelled halfway still goes through closure: document the reasons, accept or hand over what was finished, release resources, record lessons learned, and archive.

## Obtaining stakeholder approval of completion

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Accepted deliverables; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.2.2.6; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.9.1

The enabler: **obtain project stakeholder approval of project completion**.

- **Accepted deliverables** are validated by an **appointed individual or group** against established criteria; when the criteria are met, they are approved and accepted.
- The authorized stakeholder who approves should be **involved early** and give feedback on quality along the way, not only at the end.
- **Validate Scope** formalizes acceptance (module 2.5); Close Project or Phase uses the accepted deliverables.
- Closure also **measures stakeholder satisfaction** and verifies that all **contractual, regulatory, legal, social and environmental obligations** have been met.

## Validating readiness for transition

@source The PMI Guide to Business Analysis, 5.5; The PMI Guide to Business Analysis, 5.5.3.1; The PMI Guide to Business Analysis, 5.5.3.2; The PMI Guide to Business Analysis, 5.5.4; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Final product, service, or result transition; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.9.1

The enabler: **validate readiness for transition (to the operations team or the next phase)**.

- **Prepare for Transition to Future State** decides whether the organization is **ready**, and how it will move from the current to the future state, so that the solution is **adopted** into operations.
- A **readiness assessment** checks the organization's **ability and interest** to use the new capability. It identifies **gaps in readiness** as risks, with responses. It may be a report or a **readiness checklist**.
- A **transition plan** builds on the assessment: operational procedures, **training**, service-level agreements, rollout material, coordination with other releases and **contingency plans**.
- The **final product, service or result transition** hands the result to the group that will **operate, maintain and support** it (or, at phase end, to the next phase).
- **Outstanding risks** are either **accepted** or **transitioned** to operations.

| Predictive | Adaptive |
|---|---|
| Readiness assessment and transition plan, possibly with a checklist and a schedule | Transition tasks inside stories, separate transition stories, or the **definition of done** |
| Formal plan for a large release | A reserved block of time or an iteration for transition, coordinated with the operations owner, including cleanup of technical debt |

## Closing contracts

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.9.1; PMBOK 6, 12.3.3.1; PMBOK 8, Appendix X4.2

Part of the enabler **conclude activities to close the project or phase**: procurement.

- **Confirm formal acceptance** of the external source's work.
- **Finalize** open **claims, guarantees and warranties**; resolve disputes or refer them to the **dispute board**.
- **Pay all invoices** due to external sources.
- **Update the records** with the final results and **archive** the information.
- The buyer, usually through its procurement administrator, gives the seller **formal written notice** that the contract is complete (module 2.8).

## Closing the finances and releasing resources

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.9.1; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.6.2.5

The enabler continues with **financials and resources**:

| Area | Closure activity |
|---|---|
| **Financials** | Verify that **all costs** are charged to the project; **close the project accounts** |
| **People** | **Reassign or release** project personnel |
| **Materials** | Deal with **excess** project materials |
| **Facilities and equipment** | **Reallocate or release** facilities, equipment and other resources |

Releasing physical and virtual resources when they are no longer needed is also part of Monitor and Control Resourcing (module 2.7): closure makes sure nothing is left charging the project.

## Lessons learned, retrospectives and knowledge

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Lessons learned register; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.9.1; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.9.2; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.6

The enabler names **final lessons learned and retrospectives**.

- The **lessons learned register** records knowledge gained during a project, phase or iteration: the situation, its impacts, **recommendations** and proposed actions. It is created **early** and updated throughout, not only at the end.
- At closure, the team **collects and audits** records, **assesses success or failure**, manages **knowledge sharing and transfer**, documents lessons learned and **archives** project information for future use.
- Suggestions for improving **organizational policies and procedures** are forwarded to the right unit: **organizational process asset updates**.
- Adaptive teams hold **retrospectives** on the iteration or release and **knowledge-sharing sessions**.

## The final report and benefits

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Final report; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Benefits management plan; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.9

The **final report** summarizes project performance:

- a summary description of the project or phase;
- **scope** objectives, criteria and evidence that completion criteria were met;
- **quality** objectives, criteria, verification and actual milestone dates, with reasons for variances;
- **cost** objectives, the acceptable range, actual costs and reasons for variances;
- the validation of the final product, service or result;
- **schedule** objectives and whether the **benefits** were achieved; if not yet, the **degree achieved and an estimate** for future realization;
- how the result met the **business needs**, or when it will;
- the **risks and issues** met and how they were addressed.

The **benefits management plan** says how and when benefits are delivered and measured. Many benefits arrive **after** the project closes, so the plan names a **benefits owner**: the person accountable for monitoring, recording and reporting realized benefits over the planned timeframe.

## Predictive and agile

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.9.1; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.9.2

| | Predictive | Adaptive |
|---|---|---|
| What closes | The project or phase, after administrative closure | Each **release or iteration**, and finally the project |
| Completion check | Exit criteria, formal acceptance, accounts closed | Prioritized backlog items finished, **acceptance criteria** met, deliverables reviewed and accepted |
| Learning | Lessons learned identified, documented, archived | **Retrospectives** and knowledge-sharing sessions |
| Risks | Outstanding risks accepted or transitioned to operations | Outstanding risks accepted or moved to later iterations, releases, clients or operations |

## Exam tips

- Every project closes, including cancelled ones; document why it ended.
- Close only after formal acceptance; Validate Scope comes before closing.
- Check transition readiness (training, support, procedures) before the handover.
- Contracts: acceptance, claims settled, invoices paid, records archived, written notice.
- Release people and resources, charge all costs, close the accounts.
- Lessons learned are captured throughout, and finalized at closure.
