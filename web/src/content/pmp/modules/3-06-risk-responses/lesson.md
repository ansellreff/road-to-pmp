## Key points

- **Plan Risk Responses** chooses strategies and agreed actions, with a **risk owner** for each, and adds reserves and activities to the plan. It is done **throughout** the project.
- **Threats:** escalate, **avoid**, **transfer**, **mitigate**, accept. **Opportunities:** escalate, **exploit**, **share**, **enhance**, accept. The same strategies apply to **overall project risk**.
- **Active acceptance** usually means a **contingency reserve**; passive acceptance means only periodic review.
- **Contingent responses** (contingency or fallback plans) run only when a **trigger** occurs. Watch for **residual** risks (left after responses) and **secondary** risks (caused by responses).
- **Implement Risk Responses** makes sure agreed responses are actually carried out; **Monitor Risks** tracks risks, finds new ones and checks that responses work, with **technical performance analysis**, **reserve analysis**, **risk audits** and **risk reviews**.
- **Communicate** risk status through the **risk register**, the **risk report** and work performance information.

## Planning risk responses

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.7.2.4; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.7.1

**Plan Risk Responses** develops options, selects strategies and agrees actions for **overall project risk exposure** and for **individual risks**. It allocates resources or **reserves** and inserts activities into the project documents and plan. It is performed **throughout** the project.

| Tools and techniques | Outputs |
|---|---|
| Expert judgment; interviews; facilitation; **strategies for threats**; **strategies for opportunities**; **contingent response strategies**; **strategies for overall project risk**; alternatives and **cost-benefit analysis**; **multicriteria decision analysis** | **Change requests**; updates to the management plans and the **scope, schedule and cost baselines**; updates to the risk register, risk report, schedule, team assignments and cost forecasts |

- Each response has a **risk owner** who maintains continuity and acts on it.
- **Adequate and appropriate** responses minimize individual and overall threats and maximize opportunities.
- Responses are **aligned with project constraints** such as budget, schedule and performance.

## Strategies for threats

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Strategies for threats

| Strategy | What it does | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Escalate** | The threat is **outside the project's scope**, or the response exceeds the **project manager's authority**: it is managed at program, portfolio or another level, which must **accept ownership** | The project manager notifies the right person; the team no longer monitors it |
| **Avoid** | **Eliminates** the threat (probability to zero) or isolates the objectives from it; for high-probability, high-impact threats | Remove the cause, extend the schedule, change the strategy, **reduce scope**; clarify requirements, get information, acquire expertise |
| **Transfer** | Shifts ownership and impact to a **third party**, often for a **risk premium** | **Insurance**, performance bonds, warranties, guarantees, agreements |
| **Mitigate** | **Reduces probability and/or impact**; early action works better than repair | Simpler processes, more tests, a more stable seller, a **prototype**, **redundancy** in a system |
| **Accept** | Acknowledges the threat but takes no proactive action; for low-priority threats or where nothing else is cost-effective | **Active:** a **contingency reserve** of time, money or resources. **Passive:** periodic review only |

## Strategies for opportunities

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Strategies for opportunities; PMBOK 8, Glossary (risk sharing)

| Strategy | What it does | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Escalate** | The opportunity is outside the project's scope or the response exceeds the project manager's authority; managed at a higher level that accepts ownership | Notify the program or portfolio |
| **Exploit** | Makes sure the opportunity **definitely happens** (probability to 100%); for high-priority opportunities | Assign the **most talented** people; use new technology to cut cost and duration |
| **Share** | Gives ownership to a **third party best able to capture** the benefit, who shares in it | Risk-sharing partnerships, teams, special-purpose companies, **joint ventures** |
| **Enhance** | **Increases probability and/or impact**; early action works better | Focus on the causes; add resources to an activity to **finish early** |
| **Accept** | Acknowledges the opportunity without proactive action | **Active:** a contingency reserve to take advantage of it. **Passive:** periodic review |

## Overall project risk, contingent responses and remaining risk

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Strategies for overall project risk; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Contingent response strategies; PMBOK 6, 11.5.3.3; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.7.1

**Overall project risk** uses the same strategies:

| Strategy | At project level |
|---|---|
| **Avoid** | Remove high-risk scope; if the project cannot get back within its thresholds, it may be **canceled** (the most extreme avoidance) |
| **Exploit** | Add high-benefit scope; or change the thresholds with key stakeholders' agreement |
| **Transfer or share** | Involve a third party: collaborative buyer-seller structures, a **joint venture**, subcontracting key elements |
| **Mitigate or enhance** | Replan, change scope or boundaries, priority, resource allocation or delivery times |
| **Accept** | Continue as defined, with an **overall contingency reserve** (active) or periodic review (passive) |

- **Contingent response strategies** (also called **contingency plans** or **fallback plans**) are carried out **only if a trigger condition occurs**, for example engaging an alternate supplier only if the preferred supplier cannot perform. Effort is spent only when needed, and attention shifts to watching the **trigger**.
- **Residual risks** remain after the planned responses, or were deliberately accepted.
- **Secondary risks** arise as a **direct result of implementing a response**; they are analyzed and planned like any other risk.

## Executing the risk management plan

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.7.2.5; PMBOK 8, Standard 3.7; PMBOK 8, Appendix X3.3; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.7.3

The enabler of Business Environment task 5: **execute a risk management plan (e.g., risk response for security and managing sustainability risks)**.

**Implement Risk Responses** makes sure the agreed responses are **executed as planned**, to address overall risk exposure, minimize threats and maximize opportunities.

| Inputs | Tools and techniques | Outputs |
|---|---|---|
| Risk management plan; lessons learned register; risk register; risk report | Expert judgment; **influencing** (to help risk owners carry out the agreed actions); the PMIS | **Change requests**; updates to the issue log, lessons learned, team assignments, risk register and risk report |

| Example risk | Possible responses |
|---|---|
| **Security:** customer data could be exposed | **Mitigate** with security testing and data protection under a privacy policy; **transfer** part of the impact through insurance or contract terms |
| **Sustainability:** cheapest materials could harm the environment or breach rules | **Avoid** by choosing sources with less impact; **mitigate** by planning materials carefully; follow the sustainability pyramid (module 3.2) |

Responses are adjusted **flexibly** as conditions change, without losing sight of the project's goals, and with **open communication** to the stakeholders concerned.

## Monitoring and controlling risks

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.7.2.6; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Technical performance analysis; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Reserve analysis; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Audits; PMBOK 8, Glossary (risk review); PMBOK 8, Guide 2.7.5

The enabler: **monitor and control risks**.

**Monitor Risks** monitors the response plans, tracks identified risks, **identifies and analyzes new risks**, plans responses for them, and **evaluates the effectiveness** of responses and of the risk process, throughout the project. It makes sure **risk owners** stay assigned.

| Tool | What it checks |
|---|---|
| **Technical performance analysis** | Actual technical results (weight, transaction times, delivered defects, capacity) against the plan; deviations hint at threats or opportunities |
| **Reserve analysis** | Whether the remaining contingency reserve is still enough for the remaining risk |
| **Risk audit** | The **effectiveness of the risk management process**, at the frequency set in the risk management plan; the project manager makes sure it happens |
| **Risk review (risk reassessment)** | The status of existing risks and identification of **new** ones, often in meetings |

**Outputs:** work performance information, change requests, updates to any plan component, the assumption log, issue log, lessons learned, risk register and risk report, and **organizational process asset** updates.

**Checking results (Table 2-11):** responses aligned with constraints; reserves in place and used effectively; threats prevented so **less contingency is used**; opportunities realized; awareness of the organization's **business continuity** or emergency plan; a **management reserve** for unknown risks.

## Communicating risk status

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Risk report; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Risk register; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.7.4; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Strategies for threats

The enabler: **communicate the status of a risk's impact on the project**.

- The **risk report** grows through the risk processes: sources of overall risk, numbers of threats and opportunities, distribution by category, **metrics and trends**, and the results of responses.
- The **risk register** shows each risk's owner, response and current **status**.
- **Work performance information** from Monitor Risks feeds status reports and dashboards.
- **Stakeholders** are both sources of risk information and audiences: engage them so risk processes match their expectations.
- For an **escalated** risk, the project manager makes sure the right person is told and **accepts ownership**.
- Agile teams can show risk on visible charts: a **burndown chart** can show how much **risk exposure** has been reduced.

## Predictive and agile

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.7.3.1; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Visual controls; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.7.4

| | Predictive | Agile |
|---|---|---|
| Responses | Planned early, with reserves in the baselines | Planned each iteration; risk-adjusted backlog items |
| Monitoring | Risk reviews, audits, reserve analysis at set points | Risk review with stakeholders at the end of each iteration |
| Reserves | **Schedule reserves** for stable scope | Plans adjusted as new risks appear |
| Status | Risk register and risk report | Visible boards, risk burndown |

## Exam tips

- Threats: avoid, transfer, mitigate, accept (and escalate). Opportunities: exploit, share, enhance, accept (and escalate).
- Escalate when the risk is outside the project or beyond the project manager's authority; the receiver must accept it.
- Active acceptance = contingency reserve; passive acceptance = review only.
- A contingency plan waits for its trigger.
- Responses create secondary risks and leave residual risks: analyze both.
- Monitor Risks never stops: new risks, response effectiveness, reserves.
