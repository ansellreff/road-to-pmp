## Key points

- **Projects create change**: they move the organization from a **current state** to a **future state**, sometimes through a transition state. **Change management** helps people get there; it is different from project **change control**.
- **Organizational culture** (vision, values, norms, leadership styles, hierarchy) is an internal enterprise environmental factor. **Assess** it by talking with stakeholders, team members and management about where the organization puts its emphasis: speed or stability, flexibility or predictability.
- Some characteristics **support** change (willing executives, focus on long-term goals); others are **roadblocks** (silos, rewards for local efficiency, people spread over too many projects).
- **Organizational changes** (reorganizations, new strategies, market pressure, mandates) can change a project's priorities, resources and stakeholders: evaluate the impact and act through governance and change control.
- **Change models:** PMI's Managing Change in Organizations, **ADKAR**, Kotter's **8 steps**, the **Satir** model and **Bridges'** transition model.
- Support adoption with a **motivational** approach, two-way communication, visible sponsorship, a **pace** people can absorb, and reinforcement so people do not slip back.

## Projects as agents of change

@source PMBOK 8, Standard 1.3.1; PMBOK 7, Standard 3.12; Agile Practice Guide, 6.1

- A project's purpose is to **move an organization from one state to another**: from the **current state** to a desired **future state**, sometimes through a structured **transition state**. Completing the project achieves value as defined by key stakeholders.
- **Change management** (or enablement) is a comprehensive, cyclic, structured approach for transitioning individuals, groups and organizations from a current state to a future state in which they **realize the desired benefits**.
- It is **different from project change control**, which approves or rejects modifications to documents, deliverables or baselines (module 3.3).
- **Organizational change management** in the Agile Practice Guide: the holistic, structured approach for transitioning individuals and organizations to achieve intended business benefits.

## Assessing organizational culture

@source PMBOK 8, Standard 2.2.1; Agile Practice Guide, 6.2; Agile Practice Guide, 6.2.1; Agile Practice Guide, 6.1.2; PMBOK 8, Standard 3.8

The first enabler of Business Environment task 7 is to **assess organizational culture**.

- **Organizational culture, structure and governance** are **internal enterprise environmental factors**: vision, mission, values, beliefs, cultural norms, **leadership styles**, hierarchy and authority, organizational styles, ethics and codes of conduct.
- **Culture is the organization's DNA.** It runs along a continuum, from highly predictive plans to Lean Startup, where everything is an experiment. Even a predictive organization can move toward agility through empirical measures, small experiments and learning.
- **How to assess it:** talk with stakeholders, team members and senior management about organizational priorities, and record them as positions on **sliding scales** between two extremes: **exploration or execution**, **speed or stability**, **quantity or quality**, **flexibility or predictability**. Use the results to choose techniques that fit.
- A digital bank may favor **speed**; a government program may favor **stability**. The model matters less than **understanding the forces** that shape the context.
- People's **commitment and passion** decide whether any plan succeeds.

| Change-friendly characteristics | Roadblocks |
|---|---|
| Executives willing to change | Work split into **departmental silos** |
| Willing to change how employees are viewed and assessed | Procurement based on short-term pricing |
| Suitable centralization of portfolio, program, project and product functions | Leaders rewarded for **local efficiency** rather than end-to-end flow |
| Balance of short-term and long-term goals and metrics | Specialists without tools or incentives to broaden their skills |
| Mature talent management | People pulled onto **too many projects at once** |

**An empowered culture** has mutual trust and full clarity on roles, responsibilities, team agreements and processes, so people collaborate freely.

## Evaluating the impact of organizational change on the project

@source Agile Practice Guide, 6.1.1; PMBOK 7, Standard 3.12; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.1; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.8

The enabler: **evaluate the impact of organizational change on the project and determine required actions**.

| Driver of change | Example |
|---|---|
| **Accelerated delivery** (internal) | Increments arrive faster than the receiving organization can adopt them |
| **Shifts in ways of working** (internal) | More collaboration and iterative prototypes feel disruptive and invite resistance |
| **Market and competitive pressure** | New offerings, pricing shifts, customer expectations |
| **Compliance and policy mandates** | Regulatory deadlines, audit requirements |
| **Technology and operating model shifts** | Platform changes, automation, **reorganizations** |
| **Closing value gaps** | Strategic goals that current processes cannot meet |

When the organization changes (a reorganization, a merger, a new strategy or leadership), ask:

- Is the project still **aligned** with strategy and still worth doing? The **value proposition** guides the governance decision: continue, **replan**, **expand or contract**, or terminate early.
- Have **sponsors, stakeholders or decision paths** changed? Update the stakeholder register and engagement plan.
- Are **resources** or priorities shifting? Check the resource plan and the risk register.
- Do the plan or baselines need to change? Use **change control** (module 3.3).
- Will the people receiving the result be **ready** to adopt it? Plan change management activities.

## Change models

@source PMBOK 7, Guide 4.2.4

| Model | Key idea |
|---|---|
| **Managing Change in Organizations (PMI)** | Five iterative elements with feedback loops: **formulate** change (the why), **plan** change, **implement** change, **manage transition**, **sustain** change |
| **ADKAR** | Five steps individuals go through: **Awareness** (why), **Desire**, **Knowledge** (how), **Ability** (practice, help), **Reinforcement** |
| **Kotter's 8 steps** (top-down) | Create urgency; form a powerful coalition; create a vision; communicate it; **remove obstacles**; create **short-term wins**; build on the change; **anchor it in the culture** |
| **Satir model** | Late status quo; foreign element; **chaos** (performance lowest); transforming idea; practice and integration; new status quo, often at a **higher** level |
| **Bridges' transition model** | Change is situational; **transition** is psychological. Ending, losing and letting go; the **neutral zone**; the new beginning |

## Actions that help people adopt change

@source PMBOK 7, Standard 3.12; Agile Practice Guide, 6.1.2; Agile Practice Guide, 6.1.3; Agile Practice Guide, 6.1.1

- Use a **motivational** strategy, not a forceful one. **Engagement and two-way communication** help adoption and surface valid concerns from resistant users.
- **Communicate the vision and goals early** to gain buy-in; communicate the benefits and the impact on work processes to all levels throughout the project.
- **Pace** the change to the stakeholders' **change appetite**, cost and ability to absorb it: too much change too fast causes **change saturation**, fatigue and resistance.
- **Reinforce** the change after implementation so people do not return to the old state.
- Leaders can build cultural compatibility through **visible and active executive sponsorship**, communication and coaching, progressive pacing, incremental introduction of practices, and **leading by example**.
- **Agile change management:** treat the change as a project with its own **backlog** of changes, try each as an **experiment**, track them on a **kanban board**, and watch metrics such as change lead time and **adoption rate**.
- Change management is a **value enabler**: it drives **adoption**, **outcomes** and lasting **benefits realization**; start it in strategic planning and sustain it into operations.

> **On the exam.** Users resist a new system: find out why, involve them, communicate the benefits, train and support them. Do not force it through, and do not ignore the resistance.

## Predictive and agile

@source Agile Practice Guide, 6.2; Agile Practice Guide, 6.1.3; PMBOK 7, Standard 3.12

| | Predictive | Agile |
|---|---|---|
| Culture | Bias toward stability and predictability | Bias toward speed, flexibility and experiments |
| Change approach | Planned change management activities, often top-down | Change as a backlog of experiments, introduced incrementally |
| Adoption risk | Change arrives late, all at once | Frequent increments can outpace the organization's readiness |

## Exam tips

- Change management helps people adopt; change control manages baselines.
- Assess culture through conversations; fit the techniques to the culture.
- When the organization changes, check alignment, stakeholders, resources and the plan.
- ADKAR is individual; Kotter is top-down; Satir and Bridges describe what people feel.
- Pace change to what people can absorb; reinforce it afterwards.
