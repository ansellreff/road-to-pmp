## Key points

- **Assess and Implement Changes** runs from start to finish: **any stakeholder** may request a change to scope, the plan, documents or deliverables. How formal it is depends on the project's scope, complexity, contracts and approach.
- Change requests can be **corrective actions, preventive actions, defect repairs or updates**.
- In **predictive** projects, once a **baseline** exists, every change to it goes through **integrated change control**: written request, **impact analysis**, then a decision (**approved, rejected or deferred**) by the designated person or the **change control board (CCB)**.
- In **adaptive** projects, a change becomes a **backlog item**; its **priority** decides whether it is done now, deferred or dropped.
- Record every request and its status in the **change log**, and **communicate** decisions to the requester and stakeholders.
- **Implement** approved changes, **update** the plan, baselines and documents, and check that the change gave the desired result.

## Change in the Eighth Edition

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.8; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Change requests; PMBOK 6, 4.3.3.4; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Integrated change control; PMBOK 8, Glossary (scope creep)

**Assess and Implement Changes** (Governance domain, Monitoring and Controlling) manages changes that affect project scope, product scope, plan components and project documents.

| Type of change request | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Corrective action** | Realign the performance of the work with the plan |
| **Preventive action** | Make sure future performance stays aligned with the plan |
| **Defect repair** | Modify a nonconforming product or product component |
| **Update** | Reflect modified or additional ideas or content in documents or plans |

- A **change request** is a formal proposal to modify a document, deliverable or baseline; it may come from **inside or outside** the project.
- **Integrated change control** manages changes in a coordinated way: evaluate requests, analyze impact, decide, document and communicate, so the project stays aligned with its objectives and constraints.
- **Scope creep** is the uncontrolled expansion of product or project scope **without adjusting time, cost and resources**. Change control prevents it.

| Input or tool | Role |
|---|---|
| **Change management plan** | Establishes the **CCB** (also called a steering committee), the extent of its **authority**, and how the change control system works |
| **Configuration management plan** | Says which artifacts are under **configuration control**: changes to them need a formal change request |
| **Change control tools** | Manual or automated tools to register, assess, approve, track and communicate changes |
| **Tools and techniques** | Expert judgment, alternatives and cost-benefit analysis, voting, autocratic decisions, multicriteria decision analysis, meetings, integrated change control, **backlog management** |
| **Outputs** | **Approved change requests**, plan updates, **change log** updates |

## Executing the change control process

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.8.1; PMBOK 8, Guide Figure 2-11; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Change management plan; The Standard for Earned Value Management, 4.6.2

The first enabler of Business Environment task 3 is to **execute the change control process**. In a predictive project:

1. **Before baselines**, changes are not formally controlled. **After**, every change to a baseline goes through the formal process.
2. A change may be raised **verbally**, but it is **documented in writing** and entered in the change or configuration management system.
3. **Impact analysis** looks at scope, finance, schedule, resources, stakeholders and risk, with estimated **schedule and cost impacts**.
4. A **decision** is made by the designated person (sponsor or project manager, as the plan says) or the **CCB**: **approved, rejected, deferred**, or more information is requested.
5. **While waiting** for the CCB, the project manager keeps executing the **planned** work and analyzes the impact and risks of accepting or rejecting the change.

The **CCB** is a formally established group that reviews, evaluates, approves, defers or rejects changes, and **documents and communicates** its decisions. Depending on the change, **customer or sponsor approval** may also be needed after the CCB, unless they sit on it.

> **On the exam.** A stakeholder asks for a change: do not refuse it, and do not just do it. Record it, analyze the impact, take it to the person or board with authority, and communicate the decision.

## Change in adaptive approaches

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.8.2; The Standard for Earned Value Management, 4.4.3.3; PMBOK 7, Guide 2.6.2

- Changes are handled by **backlog management** rather than formal change requests, and are assessed and prioritized continuously.
- A proposed change is recorded as a **product backlog item**; an **impact analysis** sets its priority.
- There is usually **no formal approval**: a **lower priority** means the change is effectively **deferred**; not adding it, or removing it, means it is **rejected**.
- The **product owner** owns the backlog. Changes after each iteration re-plan the release, and recalculating progress shows the product owner early whether the release is still within schedule and budget.
- Even on stable projects, a change control system presents changes to the governance body, **product owner** or sponsor for approval; this combats scope creep.

## Communicating the status of proposed changes

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Change log; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Approved change requests; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Change control tools; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.8.1

The enabler: **communicate the status of proposed changes**.

- The **change log** is a comprehensive list of the changes submitted, with the **current status** of each. The **disposition** of every request is recorded there.
- **Deferred or rejected** requests are communicated to the person or group who asked for them.
- The CCB **documents and communicates** its decisions; change control tools help **distribute decisions** to the right stakeholders.
- Keep requesters informed while a change is being analyzed, not only when it is decided. In agile, the visible, ordered backlog shows where each change stands.

## Implementing approved changes

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Approved change requests; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.4; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.8.1

The enabler: **implement approved changes to the project**.

- **Approved change requests** are implemented through **Manage Project Execution**, which also implements corrective and preventive actions and defect repairs.
- Approved changes may need **new cost estimates, schedule adjustments, resource requirements and risk assessments**.
- Changes aim to **improve** the original plan and baseline, often because conditions changed. **Monitor the results** to make sure the change gave the desired outcome.

## Updating project documentation

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.8.1; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Configuration management plan; Practice Standard for Scheduling, 3.3.4; The Standard for Earned Value Management, 4.6.3

The enabler: **update project documentation to reflect changes**.

- Update the **project management plan** (any component, including the scope, schedule and cost **baselines**) and the **project documents**: change log, requirements traceability matrix, basis of estimates, risk register.
- **Configuration management** records and reports changes to artifacts under control, so everyone works from the current version.
- The **schedule model** is updated with approved changes so it represents **100% of the current known scope**.
- A **rebaseline** is the result of approved changes, never a way to hide variances (module 2.10).

## Predictive and agile

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.8.1; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.8.2

| | Predictive | Adaptive |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | Formal change requests, integrated change control | Backlog management |
| When it applies | To baselines, once they exist | Continuously, every iteration |
| Who decides | Designated person or CCB, maybe the sponsor or customer too | The product owner, through priority |
| Decisions | Approved, rejected, deferred | High priority (do), low priority (defer), not in backlog (reject) |
| Record | Change log, configuration management | The ordered backlog |

## Exam tips

- Any stakeholder may request a change; document it even if it was raised verbally.
- Analyze the impact on all constraints before any decision.
- Only the person or board with authority approves changes to a baseline.
- Keep working on the plan while a change waits for a decision.
- Record every disposition in the change log and tell the requester.
- In agile, a change is a backlog item; its priority is the decision.
