## Key points

- **Manage Project Knowledge** uses existing knowledge and creates new knowledge to reach the project's objectives and contribute to **organizational learning**.
- **Explicit** knowledge can be codified in words, pictures or numbers and shared through documents. **Tacit** knowledge lives in people's minds (skills, experience, insight) and is shared mostly through **conversation and interaction**.
- An important aim is **converting tacit knowledge into explicit knowledge** where possible. But codified knowledge loses context, so documents alone are not enough.
- The **lessons learned register** is created **early** in the project and updated throughout, not only at the end.
- The **most important part** of knowledge management is an **atmosphere of trust**, so that people are motivated to share what they know.
- Knowledge-sharing tools connect **people** (communities of practice, shadowing, workshops, storytelling); information tools connect **people to information** (registers, libraries, the PMIS).

## Manage Project Knowledge

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.6; PMBOK 8, Guide Figure 2-8

**Manage Project Knowledge** is the process of using existing knowledge and creating new knowledge to achieve the project's objectives and contribute to organizational learning. Its two key benefits:

1. using **prior organizational knowledge** to produce or improve the project outcome;
2. **collecting new knowledge** created by the project to support operations and future projects or phases.

The key activities behind both are **knowledge sharing** and **knowledge integration** (bringing together knowledge from different domains, the context, and project management).

| Inputs (examples) | Tools and techniques | Outputs |
|---|---|---|
| All components of the project management plan; lessons learned register, project team assignments, resource breakdown structure, stakeholder register; deliverables | Expert judgment; **knowledge management**; **information management**; **after-action reviews**; **in-progress postmortems**; **storytelling**; **retrospective meetings**; active listening, facilitation, leadership, networking, political awareness | **Lessons learned register**; plan updates; **organizational process asset updates** |

The outline's task 7, **help ensure knowledge transfer**, has three enablers: **identify** critical knowledge, **gather** it, and **foster an environment** for transferring it. The sections below follow them.

## Explicit and tacit knowledge

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.6

| | Explicit knowledge | Tacit knowledge |
|---|---|---|
| **What it is** | Formal and systematic; readily codified in words, pictures or numbers | Personal and embedded in a person's mind: technical skills, experience, insights, practical know-how |
| **Examples** | Manuals, procedures, documented processes | How an expert senses that a design will fail; how to handle a difficult client |
| **How it is shared** | Easily, through information management tools such as databases, registers and searches | Through **conversations and interactions** between people; hard to articulate and to transfer |
| **Weakness** | Lacks context and is open to different interpretations, so it is not always understood or applied the right way | Hard to codify, and lost when the person leaves |

Managing knowledge is **more than documenting** explicit knowledge or collecting lessons at the end. An important objective is **converting tacit knowledge into explicit knowledge when possible**, while keeping people talking to each other for what cannot be written down.

## Identifying knowledge critical to the project

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.6; PMBOK 8, Guide Figure 2-8; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Knowledge management; Agile Practice Guide, 4.6

The first enabler is to **identify knowledge critical to the project**.

- **Decide early** what information should be collected **during** the project and **at its closure**, how and when lessons learned and retrospectives will be held, and how historical information will be made available to this and future projects.
- **Look where knowledge sits.** The process's inputs point there: **project team assignments** and the **resource breakdown structure** (who knows what), the **stakeholder register** (who outside the team holds knowledge), the lessons learned register and organizational process assets (what was learned before), and the deliverables themselves.
- **Match the effort to the project.** The right knowledge management tools depend on the **degree of innovation**, the **complexity** of the project, and the **diversity** of disciplines in the team.
- **Watch for single points of knowledge.** In agile teams, **generalizing specialists** with T-shaped skills can help one another and avoid bottlenecks, where people with a single deep skill (I-shaped) cannot.

> **On the exam.** A key engineer will leave in a month and is the only one who understands an interface. The best answer starts transferring that knowledge now (pairing, shadowing, documenting what can be documented), not at the engineer's last day, and not by asking for the departure to be postponed.

## Gathering knowledge

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Lessons learned register; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Information management; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, After-action reviews; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, In-progress postmortems; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Retrospectives; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.6

The second enabler is to **gather knowledge**.

**The lessons learned register** records knowledge gained during a project, phase or iteration so it can improve future performance for the team and the organization.

- It may hold the **category and description** of the situation, its **impacts**, **recommendations** and **proposed actions**: challenges, problems, realized risks and opportunities.
- It is **created early**, as an output of Manage Project Knowledge, and then used and updated by **many processes throughout** the project.
- The **people involved in the work** capture the lessons, by any suitable means, including video, pictures or audio.
- Lessons from previous projects are used in the current one and entered into the organization's **lessons learned repository**, part of the organizational process assets.

**Ways to collect lessons as the project goes:**

| Technique | When and how |
|---|---|
| **Retrospectives** | Regularly, at least at the end of each iteration; a form of lessons learned meeting (module 1.4) |
| **After-action reviews** | A simple, structured review of what happened, why, and how to do it better; typically at the **end of a project or after significant milestones** |
| **In-progress postmortems** | Structured reviews at **various stages** of the life cycle, unlike traditional postmortems held at the end |

**Information management** tools share simple, unambiguous, codified explicit knowledge: methods for **codifying** lessons, the lessons learned register, **library services**, information gathering such as web searches and articles, and the **project management information system** with document management. They work better with an element of **interaction**, such as a "contact me" link to the person who wrote a lesson.

**AI.** Tools such as **interview bots** add strong capabilities for capturing tacit and explicit knowledge, and AI can be a significant source of knowledge. The risks are real: **exposing sensitive information** and **hallucinations**. Projects should have **responsible AI policies**.

The transition of the product to operations at closure is part of module 2.15.

## Fostering an environment for knowledge transfer

@source PMBOK 6, 4.4; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.1.6.6; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Knowledge management; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Storytelling; Agile Practice Guide, 5.2.5

The third enabler is to **foster an environment for knowledge transfer**.

**Trust comes first.** Knowledge resides in people's minds, and people cannot be forced to share what they know. The Sixth Edition put it plainly: the **most important part of knowledge management is creating an atmosphere of trust** so that people are motivated to share; "even the best knowledge management tools and techniques will not work" otherwise. The Eighth Edition agrees: tacit knowledge is shared by fostering a **collaborative and trust-filled environment**, with open communication, **mentoring relationships**, team-building activities, **face-to-face** interaction and regular brainstorming.

**Knowledge management tools connect people:**

- networking, including informal social interaction and online forums ("What does anyone know about...?");
- **communities of practice** and special interest groups;
- **work shadowing** and reverse shadowing;
- discussion forums such as focus groups; seminars and conferences;
- workshops, including problem-solving sessions and learning reviews;
- creativity techniques; **knowledge fairs and cafés**;
- training with interaction among learners.

**Storytelling** conveys lessons, good practices and outcomes as narratives. It is particularly effective for **tacit knowledge**, which formal documentation struggles to carry, and it can shape culture by highlighting values and successes.

**In agile teams**, people collaborate in real time: **pairing or mobbing**, with roles rotating so everyone both drives and reviews work, uncovers knowledge gaps early and builds collective ownership.

> **On the exam.** If team members guard what they know, a new documentation tool will not fix it. Build trust and give people opportunities to share: pairing, shadowing, communities of practice, safe retrospectives.

## Predictive and agile

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Lessons learned register; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Retrospectives; Agile Practice Guide, 5.2.5; Agile Practice Guide, 5.2.7

| | Predictive | Agile |
|---|---|---|
| Capturing lessons | Lessons learned register, after-action reviews at milestones and the end, in-progress postmortems | Retrospectives at least every iteration; improvements tried at once |
| Sharing tacit knowledge | Mentoring, shadowing, communities of practice | Pairing or mobbing, swarming, generalizing specialists |
| Where knowledge ends up | The lessons learned repository and other organizational process assets | The same; within the team, also in shared knowledge spread by pairing and rotating roles |

## Exam tips

- Knowledge transfer starts at the beginning of the project, not at closure.
- Tacit knowledge moves through people: pairing, shadowing, mentoring, storytelling. Explicit knowledge moves through documents and systems.
- Convert tacit to explicit where possible, but keep people talking.
- No trust, no sharing: tools cannot replace a climate of trust.
- AI helps capture knowledge, with responsible AI policies against leaks and hallucinations.
