## Key points

- **Plan Stakeholder Engagement** turns the analysis of module 1.5 into strategies for each stakeholder: the **stakeholder engagement plan**. Its key benefit is an **actionable plan** to interact with stakeholders.
- **Five engagement levels**: unaware, resistant, neutral, supportive, leading. The **stakeholder engagement assessment matrix** marks each stakeholder's **current (C)** and **desired (D)** level; the gap decides how much communication is needed, and closing it is the core of monitoring.
- **Tailor communication** to each stakeholder's needs, culture and role. For unsupportive stakeholders, a **communication styles assessment** shows the method, format and content that will reach them.
- **Manage Stakeholder Engagement** carries out the plan: work with stakeholders to meet their needs, address issues and foster involvement, to **increase support and minimize resistance**.
- **Monitor Stakeholder Engagement** checks whether the strategies work and adjusts them as the project and its environment change.
- Project managers often have **little direct authority**: they reach objectives through **influence** (persuasion, clear positions, active listening, awareness of other views) and **trust** (integrity, honesty, fairness).

## Planning engagement

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.5.2.2; PMBOK 8, Guide Figure 2-34; PMBOK 8, Guide section 4, Stakeholder engagement plan

**Plan Stakeholder Engagement** develops strategies to engage stakeholders based on their **needs, expectations, interests, requirements and potential impact** on the project. Its key benefit is an **actionable plan to interact effectively** with stakeholders. Like identification, it is performed **periodically throughout the project**.

| Inputs (examples) | Tools and techniques | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Project charter; resource, communications and risk management plans; assumption log, change log, issue log, schedule, risk register, **stakeholder register**; agreements | Expert judgment; benchmarking; assumption and constraint analysis; **root cause analysis**; prioritization/ranking; mind mapping; **stakeholder engagement assessment matrix**; meetings | **Stakeholder engagement plan** (an update to the project management plan) |

The **stakeholder engagement plan** identifies the **strategies and actions** needed to promote productive involvement of stakeholders in decisions and execution. It can be **formal or informal**, **highly detailed or broadly framed**, depending on the project's needs and stakeholders' expectations, and it may set out strategies for individuals or groups.

## Engagement levels and the assessment matrix

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Stakeholder engagement assessment matrix

The **stakeholder engagement assessment matrix** compares **current and desired** engagement levels.

| Level | The stakeholder is |
|---|---|
| **Unaware** | Unaware of the project and its potential impacts |
| **Resistant** | Aware, but resistant to changes the work or outcomes may bring; unsupportive |
| **Neutral** | Aware, neither supportive nor unsupportive |
| **Supportive** | Aware and supportive of the work and its outcomes |
| **Leading** | Aware and **actively engaged** in making the project a success |

**An example matrix:**

| Stakeholder | Unaware | Resistant | Neutral | Supportive | Leading |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Regional sales manager | | C | | D | |
| IT security officer | C | | | D | |
| Sponsor | | | | C | D |
| Finance analyst | | | C D | | |

- **C** is the current level, **D** the level the team judges **essential for project success** (desired).
- The **gap** between them directs **how much communication** is needed to engage that stakeholder. The finance analyst needs no extra effort; the sales manager needs the most.
- **Closing the gaps** is an essential part of monitoring stakeholder engagement.

> **On the exam.** "Desired" is not always "leading". The desired level is the one the project needs from that stakeholder. A neutral stakeholder whose desired level is neutral needs no campaign to win them over.

## Tailoring communication to each stakeholder

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.5.1; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.5.2.3; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Communication styles assessment; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.5.3; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.5.3.1

The outline's enabler **analyze and tailor communication to stakeholder needs** sits between engagement and communication. (The full communication plan and reporting are module 1.9.)

- Communication should be **tailored to the needs and best fit for the stakeholders**, so they have the information they need to **make decisions** and know the risks of certain actions.
- Communication planning **overlaps** with identifying, analyzing, prioritizing and engaging stakeholders, and is closely aligned with the stakeholder engagement plan. Information can be internal or external, sensitive or public, general or detailed.
- A **communication styles assessment** identifies the preferred **method, format and content** for a stakeholder. It is **often used with unsupportive stakeholders**, after a stakeholder engagement assessment has shown a gap that needs tailored communication.

**What to tailor to** (tailoring considerations of the Stakeholders domain):

| Consideration | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Organizational culture** | Flat organizations rely on open communication and informal channels; hierarchical ones use more formal approaches |
| **Product** | Products built interactively or incrementally bring their own events, such as product reviews, planning sessions and retrospectives, to update and engage stakeholders |
| **AI and large language models** | Decide where AI can help produce communication artifacts; involve the right stakeholders, such as the cybersecurity team, to judge the risk |
| **Stakeholders' needs and roles** | Different methods (email for formal updates, instant messaging for quick questions, face-to-face meetings, feedback loops) and different **levels of detail** by role |
| **Complexity** | Many stakeholders call for a more sophisticated communications plan than a small project |

**The Eighth Edition's three examples:** an agile project uses daily coordination meetings and dedicated platforms for continuous feedback; a manufacturing ERP project sends biweekly email newsletters and holds monthly town halls to align a hierarchy; a global project uses **multilingual updates** and virtual forums to reach stakeholders across cultures.

> **On the exam.** A busy executive ignores long status reports. The answer is not "send them more often" but to find out how this person prefers to receive information and tailor the format and level of detail.

## Carrying out the engagement plan

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.5.2.4; PMBOK 8, Guide Figure 2-36

The enabler **execute the stakeholder engagement plan** is the process **Manage Stakeholder Engagement**: communicating and working with stakeholders, **including collaborating with sponsors**, to meet their needs and expectations, **address issues** and foster appropriate involvement. Its key benefit: it allows the project manager to **increase support and minimize resistance**. It is performed **throughout the project**.

| Tools and techniques | Outputs |
|---|---|
| Expert judgment; **feedback**; **conflict management**; **cultural awareness**; **negotiation**; **observation/conversation**; **political awareness**; **ground rules**; meetings | Change requests; updates to the communications and stakeholder engagement plans; updates to the change log, **issue log**, lessons learned register and stakeholder register |

Working with a **resistant** stakeholder usually starts with understanding the **cause** of the resistance; **root cause analysis** is among the tools of both planning and monitoring engagement. Concerns that cannot be solved at once are recorded in the **issue log**, and a stakeholder request that would change the project goes through **change requests**.

## Monitoring engagement

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.5.2.6; PMBOK 8, Guide Figure 2-38; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.5.5

**Monitor Stakeholder Engagement** assesses how effective the engagement efforts are, identifies adjustments to improve relationships, and refines strategies or plans. Its key benefit: it **maintains or increases** the efficiency and effectiveness of engagement **as the project evolves and its environment changes**.

- **Tools**: alternatives analysis, root cause analysis, stakeholder analysis; multicriteria decision analysis and voting; the **assessment matrix**; feedback and presentations; active listening, cultural awareness, leadership, networking, political awareness; meetings.
- **Outputs**: **work performance information**, change requests, updates to the plans and to the issue log, lessons learned register, risk register and stakeholder register.

**Checking the results** (Table 2-9 of the Eighth Edition):

| Outcome | How to check it |
|---|---|
| Stakeholder engagement is maintained | Feedback through interviews or surveys; periodic alignment on objectives; indicators such as the **Net Promoter Score** |
| Stakeholders agree with the project objectives | The **number of change requests to requirements**; feedback on increments |
| Communications management is performed | Review the communications plan periodically and tailor it to stakeholders' needs |
| Risk responses are identified and implemented | Review the issue log, risk register and stakeholder register |

## Bringing needs, expectations and objectives into line

@source PMBOK 8, Guide 2.5.1; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.5.4; PMBOK 8, Guide 2.5.5

The enabler **optimize alignment among stakeholder needs, expectations and project objectives** has three parts in the Eighth Edition:

- **Satisfaction is part of the objectives.** Stakeholder satisfaction should be **prioritized and integrated into the project objectives**. Continuous communication finds needs and expectations, addresses issues as they occur, **manages conflicting interests**, and involves stakeholders in decisions.
- **Decisions rest on data.** Securing timely decisions from stakeholders needs thorough preparation of supporting material and the ability to **guide the decision-making process**. AI tools such as predictive analytics and data visualization can help present data-driven recommendations, but they work together with leadership skills.
- **Stakeholders shape the work.** They define and prioritize requirements and scope, help plan the project, and set the **acceptance and quality criteria**. A steady stream of change requests to requirements is a sign that alignment has not yet been reached.

When needs **conflict** (one department wants speed, another wants extra features), use **negotiation and conflict management**, both tools of Manage Stakeholder Engagement (and module 1.2), and refer the decision back to the shared vision and objectives (module 1.1).

## Building trust and influencing

@source PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Influencing; PMBOK 8, Standard 3.6; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Political awareness; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Networking; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Active listening; PMBOK 8, Guide section 5, Cultural awareness

The last enabler of People task 4 is to **build trust and influence stakeholders** to accomplish the project objectives.

**Influencing.** Project managers often have **little or no direct authority**, especially in a matrix environment, so influencing stakeholders on time is critical. Key influencing skills:

- being **persuasive**;
- **clearly articulating** points and positions;
- high levels of **active and effective listening**;
- **awareness of, and consideration for, the various perspectives** in any situation;
- **gathering relevant information** to address issues and reach agreements **while maintaining mutual trust**.

**Trust.** Accountable leaders show **integrity, honesty and fairness**; decisions made with integrity and fairness focus on the common good and **build trust with stakeholders**.

**Skills that support both:**

| Skill | What it adds |
|---|---|
| **Political awareness** | Recognizing **formal and informal power** relationships and being willing to work within them: understanding the organization's strategies, knowing who wields power and influence, and being able to communicate with them |
| **Networking** | Connections that give access to the **informal organization**, to solve problems, influence stakeholders' actions and increase support |
| **Active listening** | Acknowledging, clarifying, confirming and understanding, to reduce misunderstandings, especially across cultures |
| **Cultural awareness** | Understanding differences among individuals, groups and organizations, and adapting the communication strategy to them |

> **On the exam.** A powerful stakeholder opposes the project. Going over their head, ignoring them or escalating at once is rarely right. First meet them, listen to understand their concerns, and look for a way to address them; escalate only if that fails and the issue is beyond your authority.

## Predictive and agile

| | Predictive | Agile |
|---|---|---|
| Engagement plan | Often formal and detailed; updated as the project progresses | Can be informal and broadly framed; engagement happens largely through the product owner and events such as reviews and planning sessions |
| Keeping stakeholders informed | Formal updates such as newsletters, town halls and reports | Reviews of working product at least every two weeks; continuous feedback; shared platforms |
| Acceptance | Against criteria agreed in the plan | The product owner sees each demonstration and accepts or declines the stories |

## Exam tips

- Unaware, resistant, neutral, supportive, leading. Compare current with desired; the gap sets the effort.
- For a resistant stakeholder: understand the cause, then tailor the communication. Do not ignore or bypass them.
- Execute the plan (Manage Stakeholder Engagement) to raise support; monitor to see whether it works, and adjust.
- Little authority? Influence with listening, clear positions, information and trust.
- Many change requests to requirements suggest that stakeholders and objectives are not yet aligned.
